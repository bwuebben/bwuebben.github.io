Research / Calabi–Yau transitions

When can a singular space become smooth, and how do its curve counts change?

Every singularity has a local story. A compact space has a global one, and the two need not agree. Homological relations determine which local smoothings coexist and, for isolated toric singularities and specified del Pezzo cones, the full deformation space. On the mirror, a related question asks which vanishing spheres bound. Universal curve counting asks how enumerative invariants pass through the resulting transitions and how the choice of local factors affects integer exponents.

Smoothability of Calabi–Yau threefolds Integrated programme

From local singularities to global relations.

A singularity may admit a smoothing on its own while the compact threefold admits none. The programme follows this discrepancy from explicit toric examples to a global smoothing criterion, then asks how mirror symmetry transforms the relations that govern a transition.

The route through examples

Begin with Paper I: local obstructions, which constructs compact nonsmoothable threefolds from reflexive polytopes. Paper II: ambient constructions asks when deforming the toric pair produces a smoothing. Paper III: isolated mirror pairs classifies Batyrev mirror pairs with at most isolated singularities and supplies examples where local analysis leaves a global question unanswered.

The route to the criterion

Start with Paper IV: the vanishing-cycle obstruction if the central question is why locally possible smoothings fail to coexist. Its period argument applies to smoothing arcs of arbitrary contact order. Continue to Paper V: the mixed smoothing criterion: for the stated nodes and cone germs, a global homological relation must avoid every local discriminant. This gives necessity, sufficiency and integrability under the specified hypotheses.

The partial smoothings companion treats projective threefolds with nodes and degree-six cones, assuming a smooth projective crepant resolution that is small at the nodes and blows up the cone vertices. A cone that no global first-order deformation moves on a chosen branch stays fixed to every order. Marked periods determine which singularities can persist together, and integral divisor classes determine the restrictions to their links.

The local equations theorem recovers the unrestricted analytic deformation space, including nilpotents, for nodes and exact degree-five and degree-six cones under the stated resolution hypotheses. Its two compact examples have identical reduced germs and tangent dimensions. The square of the nilradical distinguishes them. Read the focused guide to the deformation space.

For isolated Gorenstein toric singularities, link periods put the local polygon equations in common coordinates. Homology determines which of their infinitesimal deformations occur on the compact threefold.

Where mirror symmetry enters

Paper VI: mirror conifold relations compares integral relations among exceptional curves with relations among vanishing spheres on the mirror. Relative tropical cycles construct bounding chains near the maximally degenerate limit; transport continues them along the nodal components reached by that construction. Smoothability of the original nodal partial resolution then gives a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror.

For the explicit X₉ family, the paper also classifies the full codimension-three four-node locus. The open directions below say where the comparison stops.

From global smoothing obstructions to mirror conifold transitions

The ambition is larger than a list of nonsmoothable examples. It is to understand what happens to the topology that controls smoothability when one passes to the mirror. Near the maximally degenerate limit, Paper VI gives an exact answer for the families it treats: an integral combination of vanishing spheres is null-homologous exactly when the corresponding combination of exceptional curves is a relation. The spheres then bound chains lifted from tropical cycles, and the nodal locus has the codimension Morrison predicted. The obstruction is not lost under mirror symmetry. It is exchanged.

  • Components. The comparison holds on the nodal components its construction reaches, and it can fail on others: X₉ has a codimension-four component whose spheres are independent. Classifying the components for other polytopes is open.
  • The reverse transition. Proved for general members of the X₉ family up to birational equivalence; open for the whole admissible class.
  • Tropical generation. Conjecture G asserts that strict tropical cycles alone generate the relation lattice. It holds for 340 of the 364 hexagon-free polytopes tested; on the other 24 the cycles found have corank one to four.
  • Breadth. Paper VI treats nodes and cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven, under a projection hypothesis that fails for some polytopes. Paper V's criterion reaches down to degree five; degrees one to four carry deformations that link homology does not see and need a new comparison.

A subsequent application in the stability and degeneration programme determines the small Hodge spectrum of Ricci-flat metrics on the original-side four-node smoothing from Papers V–VI. It uses a fixed polarization and first-order smoothing at every node along a fixed ray. This is an analytic application of that family, separate from the smoothing criterion and mirror comparison above.

Read Papers IV and V for the smoothing criterion. Follow the smoothability and deformation spaces guide from the criterion to the two companion papers on partial smoothings and the full local equations, or continue to Paper VI for mirror relations. Universal curve counting Integrated programme

Specialization and integral curve counts.

When a threefold with ordinary double points is smoothed, the exceptional rational curves of a small resolution disappear. Several curve classes on the resolution can then give the same class on the smoothing. How do their counts combine?

First divide out the local factor for each exceptional curve. In balanced antidiagonal coordinates, the exponent for a nonzero smoothing class in each genus is the finite sum of the normalized resolution exponents above it. These exponents are integers. In scalar coordinates the same summation transfers integrality when the resolution exponents are integral, a hypothesis that can fail on a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold.

The conifold formula assumes a projective transition with smooth algebraic total space, trivial canonical bundles and torsion-free second homology at both smooth endpoints. If the first and second cohomology of the structure sheaf vanish at both endpoints, the proof uses fixed-target counts. In the broader setting it assumes that the connected Gromov–Witten evaluations respect the relations from analytic families.

In a compact four-node example, the calculation uses stable-pair evaluation on geometric local classes to find that eight of the 37 candidate lifts of a primitive line class contribute. For the sixteen-node quintic containing a plane, the normalized lifts in smoothing degree d have exceptional coordinate between 0 and 4d. The upper bound is attained among the candidates. Determining which of them carry nonzero counts is a further problem.

Opposite circle weights give integral generators for curve counts. Equal weights can give unbounded denominators on one smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold, even in classes of divisibility three. The local wall-crossing results describe how covers and spin structures enter the contribution of an embedded curve.

The two-circle calculation interprets the Euler-characteristic grading as a rotation weight. The first-variation theorem compares the normalized indices over the Picard family in all degrees to first order. At exchanged grading one, the full volume-character dependence agrees for each Young diagram. At exchanged gradings two and three, the diagram-by-diagram comparison gives equality for every Euler characteristic with the volume character free.

Physics companions · M-theory and topological strings

What does a geometric transition mean physically?

The four compact-Higgs papers use the same global compatibility problem to study charges, couplings, magnetic objects and vacua. They complement the smoothability and universal curve-counting programmes. The main physics route is 1 → 2 → 4; Paper 3 is a specialist branch after Paper 2. The physics guide sets out the programme as a whole: what it establishes, what remains open and why it matters.

1 · Global constraints on Higgsing

Global Constraints on Higgsing Localized Five-Dimensional Sectors connects compact divisor pairings, Abelian gauging and the global first-order deformation image. After Papers IV–V, read the partial smoothings theorem for the integral classes on each outgoing branch. In two models an identical residual node has unit electric charge under one bulk photon in the first and zero charge under every bulk photon in the second. The rigid gauging accounts for both. Identifying these branches with a prescribed microscopic Higgs vacuum at finite Planck mass remains open.

2 · Charges and quantum couplings

Bulk Gauging and Quantum Couplings follows the explicit X₉ transition, computing surviving charges and genus-zero vector couplings by continuation of marked periods. The closing Kaluza–Klein gap prevents a radius-independent range of validity for its four-state effective description.

3 · Magnetic sheaves and stability

Magnetic Sheaves and Stability Walls uses those periods to study stable sheaves and numerical Donaldson–Thomas contributions. It treats deformations on the resolved side; it does not determine the physical BPS spectrum at the endpoint of the topology-changing transition.

4 · Vacua at finite Planck mass

The Higgs Branch at Finite Planck Mass proves a quaternionic Kähler quotient normal form and applies it to local vacuum structure with gravity retained. The physical application assumes a smooth Wilsonian hypermultiplet manifold containing the light states. It leaves the identification of the completion at the del Pezzo point with the interacting E₂ Higgs branch open.

For a nontechnical introduction, read Nothing condenses alone. The broader physics guide also covers black-hole information and spectral models, which are separate from this series.

For the local curve factors, the topological strings programme explains how exchanging the external circle turns the stable-pair grading into a rotation weight.

    Non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds from reflexive polytopes

    Local obstructions: construct nonsmoothable examples from reflexive polytopes.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with isolated Gorenstein canonical singularities that admit no smoothing, as anticanonical hypersurfaces in Gorenstein Fano toric fourfolds.     Deformations of toric pairs and the smoothing of Batyrev Calabi–Yau threefolds

    Ambient constructions: identify when toric deformations produce smoothings.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Let Δ\Delta be a reflexive four-polytope with primitive edges. We construct deformations of the toric fourfold defined by its face fan that smooth the singularities of a generic anticanonical Calabi–Yau hypersurface over a chosen two-dimensional face. The construction applies when the face cone is smoothable and the dual edge has an interior lattice point; its equations are trinomials in Cox coordinates. It gives a smoothing of the hypersurface when this is the only face whose cone is singular. If every singular face satisfies these conditions, simultaneous smoothing follows in the nodal case and, in general, under irreducibility of the semiuniversal deformation space. A seven-vertex example with one ordinary double point and no smoothing shows that the dual-edge condition is sharp. A scan of the Kreuzer–Skarke classification shows that the facewise criterion accounts for 3,774 of the 30,241 smoothings in the Batyrev–Kreuzer census; the other 26,467 require relations among exceptional curve classes from different faces.

    A classification: study mirror pairs whose singularities are isolated on both sides.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    We classify reflexive four-polytopes for which the generic anticanonical hypersurfaces associated with the face fans of the polytope and its polar both have at most isolated singularities.   4. 04

    A vanishing-cycle obstruction to smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds

    A global obstruction: locally smoothable singularities need not smooth together.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove a homological obstruction to smoothing projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and anticanonical cones over smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven. On a crepant resolution that is small over the nodes and blows up the cone vertices, any smoothing forces a relation among link classes bounding in the local Milnor fibers. The relation is nonzero at every node and degree-seven point, and at each degree-six point smoothed through the one-dimensional component. The proof uses vanishing periods and applies to smoothing arcs of arbitrary contact order, extending the necessity direction of Friedman’s criterion. We apply the obstruction to a Batyrev hypersurface whose thirty singularities are individually smoothable but which admits no smoothing, even formally. On its mirror partner, two nodes cannot be smoothed by any analytic deformation, independently of a locally nonsmoothable cone point.

  5. 05

    Smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and del Pezzo cone points

    A criterion: combine the relation space with the allowed local smoothing components.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We give a necessary and sufficient homological criterion for smoothing projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and anticanonical cones over smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degrees five, six and seven, or over a smooth quadric surface. For some choice of local smoothing components, the criterion requires a relation on a crepant resolution among link classes whose associated local parameters avoid every discriminant. The complexified relation space describes first-order deformations modulo locally trivial ones. Necessity follows from local period and vanishing-cycle arguments; sufficiency applies Friedman's unobstructedness theorem to a crepant partial resolution. Whenever the criterion holds, the deformation space on the chosen components is smooth. A nonsmoothable example, whose deformation space is reduced with four smooth components, shows why nonvanishing at every singular point is insufficient. We also construct a smoothable Batyrev hypersurface admitting no smoothing induced by proper ambient families obtained from Minkowski decompositions at a single lattice degree.

    Read the homological relations in marked link periods and follow a cubic obstruction from a toric cone to a projective threefold.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    The periods of a relative volume form on the link of an isolated Gorenstein toric threefold singularity embed its semiuniversal deformation germ in the third cohomology of the link. For the homogeneous toric form this embedding is linear in Altmann’s parameters. Multiplication by a holomorphic unit changes the embedding by an automorphism of its source, even over nonreduced bases.   9. 09

    Mirror Symmetry for Conifold Relations in Calabi–Yau Threefolds

    Mirror relations: compare integral relation lattices in specified Batyrev families.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For a class of Batyrev mirror families, we establish Morrison’s predicted codimension for nodal degenerations and strengthen it to an equality of integral relation lattices. The result holds on every connected component of the nodal coefficient locus meeting our construction near the maximally degenerate limit. After marking and orienting the nodes, the relations among exceptional curves of the original small resolution coincide with those among mirror vanishing three-spheres, which generate a torsion-free subgroup. Each such component is smooth of codimension equal to the relative Picard number. We realise every relation near the limit by an integral four-chain lifted from a tropical two-cycle relative to the discriminant on Gross’s degeneration base.     For a balanced local curve of positive genus we prove a first-order circle-exchange identity for the two stable-pair indices over its Picard variety. After their common determinant normalization, the indices agree modulo (1−κ)2 in integral family K-theory in every curve degree and both counting gradings, where κ is the threefold volume character. At positive exchanged grading the first variation is a sum over hooks of Adams operations on the derived pushforwards of powers of the Poincaré line twisted by powers of the canonical bundle. With κ unrestricted, the entire coefficient of exchanged grading one agrees separately for each Young diagram and is given by a sum over its removable boxes. The first comparison deforms the graded moment equations to walls with symmetric powers of the curve as fixed loci. The second evaluates a product of two Macdonald currents and descends the resulting disk identity to the Picard family.

    The quadratic and cubic coefficients of circle exchange for balanced local curves

    At exchanged gradings two and three, the two stable-pair indices agree over the Picard variety of a balanced local curve for arbitrary volume character and every Euler characteristic.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Exchanging two circle actions on a Calabi–Yau fivefold gives stable-pair indices on a balanced local curve and on the product of its base curve with an affine plane. After a common determinant normalization, we prove that the two modified indices agree through cubic order in the exchanged counting variable, in integral K-theory over the Picard variety parametrizing one normal summand. The comparison holds for every smooth projective complex curve of positive genus and every positive curve degree, at every Euler characteristic and arbitrary volume character. On the formal disk we identify the quadratic and cubic coefficients separately for each Young diagram, with the cotangent character also free. One presentation is a Macdonald expectation and the other is a finite sum over reverse plane partitions. At quadratic order the residues from interchanging currents give the two orientations of a domino. At cubic order an exact polynomial interpolation proves that two adjacent diagonal parameters can be interchanged in the sum with currents grouped by sign. Restoring the original order produces an additional pole whose contributions from the ordinary currents and the second Adams operation cancel identically.
