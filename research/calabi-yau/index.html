Research / Calabi–Yau transitions

When can a singular space become smooth, and how do its curve counts change?

Every singularity has a local story. A compact space has a global one, and the two need not agree. The smoothability programme determines when local possibilities coexist and how mirror symmetry transforms their relations. Universal curve counting asks how enumerative invariants pass through the resulting transitions and where integer product expansions fail.

Smoothability of Calabi–Yau threefolds Integrated programme

From local singularities to global relations.

A singularity may admit a smoothing on its own while the compact threefold admits none. The six papers follow this discrepancy from explicit toric examples to a global smoothing criterion, then ask how mirror symmetry transforms the relations that govern a transition.

The route through examples

Begin with Paper I: local obstructions, which constructs compact nonsmoothable threefolds from reflexive polytopes. Paper II: ambient constructions asks when deforming the toric pair produces a smoothing. Paper III: isolated mirror pairs classifies Batyrev mirror pairs with at most isolated singularities and supplies examples where local analysis leaves a global question unanswered.

The route to the criterion

Start with Paper IV: the vanishing-cycle obstruction if the central question is why locally possible smoothings fail to coexist. Its period argument applies to smoothing arcs of arbitrary contact order. Continue to Paper V: the mixed smoothing criterion: for the stated nodes and cone germs, a global homological relation must avoid every local discriminant. This gives necessity, sufficiency and integrability under the specified hypotheses.

Where mirror symmetry enters

Paper VI: mirror conifold relations compares integral relations among exceptional curves with relations among vanishing spheres on the mirror. Relative tropical cycles construct bounding chains near the maximally degenerate limit; transport continues them along the nodal components reached by that construction. Smoothability of the original nodal partial resolution then gives a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror.

For the explicit X₉ family, the paper also classifies the full codimension-three four-node locus. The open directions below say where the comparison stops.

From global smoothing obstructions to mirror conifold transitions

The ambition is larger than a list of nonsmoothable examples. It is to understand what happens to the topology that controls smoothability when one passes to the mirror. Near the maximally degenerate limit, Paper VI gives an exact answer for the families it treats: an integral combination of vanishing spheres is null-homologous exactly when the corresponding combination of exceptional curves is a relation. The spheres then bound chains lifted from tropical cycles, and the nodal locus has the codimension Morrison predicted. The obstruction is not lost under mirror symmetry. It is exchanged.

  • Components. The comparison holds on the nodal components its construction reaches, and it can fail on others: X₉ has a codimension-four component whose spheres are independent. Classifying the components for other polytopes is open.
  • The reverse transition. Proved for general members of the X₉ family up to birational equivalence; open for the whole admissible class.
  • Tropical generation. Conjecture G asserts that strict tropical cycles alone generate the relation lattice. It holds for 340 of the 364 hexagon-free polytopes tested; on the other 24 the cycles found have corank one to four.
  • Breadth. Paper VI treats nodes and cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven, under a projection hypothesis that fails for some polytopes. Paper V's criterion reaches down to degree five; degrees one to four carry deformations that link homology does not see and need a new comparison.

Read I → II → III → IV → V → VI for the full development, or IV → V for the smoothing criterion. The Conifold Lab gives a visual introduction. The physics companions below begin with what the compact geometry permits.

Universal curve counting Developing programme

Curve counts across a transition.

A conifold transition changes both a threefold and the curve classes it carries. Universal curve counting records curve contributions in an algebra before extracting numerical invariants. This programme asks whether transition formulas hold in that algebra, whether they make compact examples computable, and when their product expansions have integer exponents.

Specialization and a compact calculation

The first paper proves a rational universal specialization formula for projective conifold transitions with smooth algebraic total space and torsion-free second homology at both endpoints. A geometric support bound makes the sum finite in each bounded target degree. In a compact four-node example, it determines all 37 candidate contributions over a primitive line class: eight are nonzero, and their sum agrees with an independent stable-pair calculation on the smoothing. The smoothing coefficient in twice the line class is also determined.

The boundary of integrality

The second paper constructs a compact counterexample for Pardon's scalar local-curve factors. The genus-ten exponent at three times a primitive class has fractional part 2/3. An exact local calculation and a stable-pair count on a Hirzebruch surface produce the obstruction. The same paper proves integrality in classes of divisibility at most two. The elementary integer expansion remains an input to the proof; the choice of local factors matters.

What the results leave open

The rational specialization theorem survives the failure of general scalar integrality. Its integrality-transfer statement remains conditional on integral exponents on the resolution. Further work can seek geometric support bounds for larger compact calculations and determine which curve classes and local factors admit integral expansions. Those broader arithmetic and computational questions remain open.

Read the specialization paper first for the transition setting, then the counterexample for the arithmetic distinction. The compact geometry connects this direction to the smoothability programme and the compact Higgs transitions. Papers, sources and exact calculations ↗

Optional companions · Compact M-theory

What does a geometric transition mean physically?

The four compact-Higgs papers use the same global compatibility problem to study charges, couplings, magnetic objects and vacua. They complement the smoothability and universal curve-counting programmes. The main physics route is 1 → 2 → 4; Paper 3 is a specialist branch after Paper 2. The physics guide sets out the programme as a whole: what it establishes, what remains open and why it matters.

1 · Global constraints on Higgsing

Global Constraints on Higgsing Localized Five-Dimensional Sectors connects compact divisor pairings, Abelian gauging and the global first-order deformation image. Read it after mathematical Papers IV–V. The comparison concerns invariant coordinates of the rigid moment-map equations; it does not identify the full scalar geometry with gravity retained.

2 · Charges and quantum couplings

Bulk Gauging and Quantum Couplings follows the explicit X₉ transition, computing surviving charges and genus-zero vector couplings by continuation of marked periods. The closing Kaluza–Klein gap prevents a radius-independent range of validity for its four-state effective description.

3 · Magnetic sheaves and stability

Magnetic Sheaves and Stability Walls uses those periods to study stable sheaves and numerical Donaldson–Thomas contributions. It treats deformations on the resolved side; it does not determine the physical BPS spectrum at the endpoint of the topology-changing transition.

4 · Vacua at finite Planck mass

The Higgs Branch at Finite Planck Mass proves a quaternionic Kähler quotient normal form and applies it to local vacuum structure with gravity retained. The physical application assumes a smooth Wilsonian hypermultiplet manifold containing the light states. It leaves the identification of the completion at the del Pezzo point with the interacting E₂ Higgs branch open.

For a nontechnical introduction, read Nothing condenses alone. The broader physics guide also covers black-hole information and spectral models, which are separate from this series.

  1. 01

    Non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds from reflexive polytopes

    Local obstructions: construct nonsmoothable examples from reflexive polytopes.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with isolated Gorenstein canonical singularities that admit no smoothing, as anticanonical hypersurfaces in Gorenstein Fano toric fourfolds. One example has a unique singularity, the anticanonical cone over the first Hirzebruch surface. Another has a unique singularity whose reduced miniversal base is a smooth curve but whose deformations are all singular. The construction combines Altmann’s deformation theory with the smoothing criterion of Corti–Filip–Petracci: a two-dimensional face of the ambient fan polytope with primitive edges obstructs smoothing if it has no Minkowski decomposition into unit segments and unimodular triangles. We also classify the 217 isolated Gorenstein toric threefold singularities whose polygon edge vectors have coordinates in [−2,2][-2,2] in some lattice basis, recording their deformation types and smoothing components. A scan of the Kreuzer–Skarke classification finds this obstruction in 39,175,53639{,}175{,}536 of its 473,800,776473{,}800{,}776 polytopes (approximately 8.27%8.27\%), whose generic anticanonical hypersurfaces therefore admit no smoothing.

  2. 02

    Deformations of toric pairs and the smoothing of Batyrev Calabi–Yau threefolds

    Ambient constructions: identify when toric deformations produce smoothings.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Let Δ\Delta be a reflexive four-polytope with primitive edges. We construct deformations of the toric fourfold defined by its face fan that smooth the singularities of a generic anticanonical Calabi–Yau hypersurface over a chosen two-dimensional face. The construction applies when the face cone is smoothable and the dual edge has an interior lattice point; its equations are trinomials in Cox coordinates. It gives a smoothing of the hypersurface when this is the only face whose cone is singular. If every singular face satisfies these conditions, simultaneous smoothing follows in the nodal case and, in general, under irreducibility of the semiuniversal deformation space. A seven-vertex example with one ordinary double point and no smoothing shows that the dual-edge condition is sharp. A scan of the Kreuzer–Skarke classification shows that the facewise criterion accounts for 3,774 of the 30,241 smoothings in the Batyrev–Kreuzer census; the other 26,467 require relations among exceptional curve classes from different faces.

  3. 03

    Doubly isolated Batyrev mirror pairs and non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds

    A classification: study mirror pairs whose singularities are isolated on both sides.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We classify reflexive four-polytopes for which the generic anticanonical hypersurfaces associated with the face fans of the polytope and its polar both have at most isolated singularities. There are exactly 590 such polytopes up to lattice equivalence in the Kreuzer–Skarke classification. Their two-dimensional faces belong to twelve lattice-isomorphism classes, all triangles, zonotopes, or reflexive polygons. The only nonsmoothable local models are the cyclic quotients 13(1,1,1)\frac13(1,1,1) and 15(1,1,3)\frac15(1,1,3) and the anticanonical cone over the first Hirzebruch surface; no singularity has a positive-dimensional reduced deformation space without a smoothing component. Within this classification, the Hirzebruch cone occurs in a unique mirror pair, associated with polytopes having 22 and 26 vertices. We exhibit this pair explicitly: one hypersurface is nonsmoothable, while all singularities of its mirror are locally smoothable. The crepant resolutions have Hodge numbers (20,26)(20,26) and (26,20)(26,20). At the opposite extreme, the self-dual 24-cell is the unique member of the classification for which both hypersurfaces are smooth before crepant resolution.

  4. 04

    A vanishing-cycle obstruction to smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds

    A global obstruction: locally smoothable singularities need not smooth together.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove a homological obstruction to smoothing projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and anticanonical cones over smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven. On a crepant resolution that is small over the nodes and blows up the cone vertices, any smoothing forces a relation among link classes bounding in the local Milnor fibers. The relation is nonzero at every node and degree-seven point, and at each degree-six point smoothed through the one-dimensional component. The proof uses vanishing periods and applies to smoothing arcs of arbitrary contact order, extending the necessity direction of Friedman’s criterion. We apply the obstruction to a Batyrev hypersurface whose thirty singularities are individually smoothable but which admits no smoothing, even formally. On its mirror partner, two nodes cannot be smoothed by any analytic deformation, independently of a locally nonsmoothable cone point.

  5. 05

    Smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and del Pezzo cone points

    A criterion: combine the relation space with the allowed local smoothing components.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We give a necessary and sufficient homological criterion for smoothing projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and anticanonical cones over smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degrees five, six and seven, or over a smooth quadric surface. For some choice of local smoothing components, the criterion requires a relation on a crepant resolution among link classes whose associated local parameters avoid every discriminant. The complexified relation space describes first-order deformations modulo locally trivial ones. Necessity follows from local period and vanishing-cycle arguments; sufficiency applies Friedman's unobstructedness theorem to a crepant partial resolution. Whenever the criterion holds, the deformation space on the chosen components is smooth. A nonsmoothable example, whose deformation space is reduced with four smooth components, shows why nonvanishing at every singular point is insufficient. We also construct a smoothable Batyrev hypersurface admitting no smoothing induced by proper ambient families obtained from Minkowski decompositions at a single lattice degree.

  6. 06

    Mirror Symmetry for Conifold Relations in Calabi–Yau Threefolds

    Mirror relations: compare integral relation lattices in specified Batyrev families.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For a class of Batyrev mirror families, we establish Morrison’s predicted codimension for nodal degenerations and strengthen it to an equality of integral relation lattices. The result holds on every connected component of the nodal coefficient locus meeting our construction near the maximally degenerate limit. After marking and orienting the nodes, the relations among exceptional curves of the original small resolution coincide with those among mirror vanishing three-spheres, which generate a torsion-free subgroup. Each such component is smooth of codimension equal to the relative Picard number. We realise every relation near the limit by an integral four-chain lifted from a tropical two-cycle relative to the discriminant on Gross’s degeneration base. The comparison characterises smoothability of the nodal partial resolution and, when it is smoothable, yields a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror. We treat specified projective toric partial resolutions of hypersurfaces with nodes and degree-six or degree-seven del Pezzo cone points. In an explicit four-node family, every member of the unique codimension-three nodal component admits a projective small resolution. For general members, these resolutions are birational to Batyrev mirrors of smoothings of the original nodal threefold, giving the reversed transition predicted by Morrison in this family.

Universal curve counting · 2 papersThe programme ↑

  1. 07

    Universal curve counting under conifold transitions

    Specialization: a rational universal transition formula and a complete compact source calculation with 37 candidates.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For projective conifold transitions of Calabi–Yau threefolds, we lift the numerical degeneration formula to Pardon's rational universal curve-counting algebra. We assume a smoothing with smooth algebraic total space and torsion-free second homology at both endpoints. After removing the exceptional local-curve factors, effective lifts to the common blow-up give a support bound that makes specialization finite in every bounded target degree. We carry out this specialization completely in the primitive line class of a plane in the smoothing of a compact four-node transition. The source calculation uses an Euler-characteristic bound for pure curves on two intersecting rational surfaces, allowing arbitrary multiple exceptional components. Together with a finite genus-zero mirror calculation, this bound determines all 37 candidate universal coefficients: eight are nonzero, and their sum agrees with an independent stable-pair calculation on a plane in the smoothing. We also determine the smoothing coefficient in twice the line class. If the resolution has integral product exponents, then so does the smoothing.

  2. 08

    A counterexample to integral product exponents in universal curve counting

    Arithmetic boundary: a compact scalar exponent with fractional part 2/3, alongside integrality through divisibility two.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold with torsion-free second homology whose product expansion in Pardon's scalar equivariant local-curve generators has a nonintegral exponent. The genus-ten exponent at three times a primitive class has fractional part 23, disproving the integer-exponent assertion for these generators. The obstruction comes from an exact degree-three calculation in the local Gromov–Witten Frobenius algebra. Comparison with the integer elementary expansion of Ionel–Parker gives a necessary divisibility condition on the primitive genus-three count. We realize a count violating this condition on a first Hirzebruch surface in a compact toric hypersurface. A filtration by powers of the surface ideal controls transverse thickenings and disconnected products; the stable-pair moduli space at minimal Euler characteristic is a projective space of dimension thirteen, with virtual count −14. We also prove integrality in classes of divisibility at most two. The counterexample concerns the scalar local factors; the integer elementary expansion is an input to its proof.
