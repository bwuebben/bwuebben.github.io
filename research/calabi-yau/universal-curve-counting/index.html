Universal curve counting

An effective one-cycle is a sum of curves with positive multiplicities. Pardon’s algebra records the space of these cycles in each homology class.

On one smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold, opposite circle weights on the two normal lines give integer product exponents. Equal weights give exponents with arbitrarily large denominators.

Which local factors give integral counts?

Take a smooth curve C of genus g and two normal line bundles of degree g − 1 whose tensor product is its canonical bundle. A local factor collects the contributions of the zero section C and all its multiples 2C, 3C, … in this threefold. The scalar and antidiagonal actions give the same class for C itself. Its multiples can distinguish them.

With opposite circle weights, the classes of the multiple zero sections freely generate the integral part of Pardon’s cycle algebra in first Chern number and virtual dimension zero. In the comparison with an isolated holomorphic multiple of multiplicity m, the complex orientation gives leading coefficient +1. Every correction uses generators of smaller multiplicity. This triangular comparison is what makes the change of generators integral. On a smooth compact Kähler threefold whose first Chern class is nonnegative on every irreducible curve, the series of effective cycles of first Chern number zero has a unique product in these factors. Its integer exponents retain the full integral homology class, including torsion. Integral generators for curve counting on threefolds

What an exponent measures

For a smooth projective threefold with trivial canonical bundle and H1(𝒪) = H2(𝒪) = 0, the balanced exponent of class β and genus h is the signed multiplicity of Ionel–Parker elementary clusters of that class and genus. An earlier proof recovered these integers by evaluating the universal series, assuming Gromov–Witten evaluation for analytic families. The geometric proof deforms the normal geometry near an elementary curve and separates disjoint clusters at bounded energy. Fixed-genus counts distinguish the multiplicity-one classes and recover the product in the rational algebra. Antidiagonal local curves · Geometric product formula

Where a fraction first appears

In a compact example, a primitive class α has genus-three cluster multiplicity −14. At three times that class, the genus-ten exponent in scalar factors has fractional part 2/3. The two products encode the same curve spaces. It is the change of local factors that introduces the denominator. For projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with torsion-free second homology, scalar integrality holds in classes of divisibility one and two. A counterexample to integral product exponents

The size of the discrepancy is already visible in a local calculation. In odd genus g = 2k + 1, the scalar class of the tripled zero section has exact denominator 3k in the integral algebra. Globally, a single smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold has scalar exponents of unbounded denominator even among classes of divisibility three. The scalar-to-antidiagonal comparison is known in prime degree, with explicit results in degrees four and six. Determining the denominators in arbitrary composite degree remains open. Triple curves and unbounded denominators

The balanced factors also have a physical interpretation. A second circle on a Calabi–Yau fivefold turns the Euler-characteristic grading into a rotation of a plane and recovers the local partition function. See the two-circle comparison and the topological strings programme.

How do curve counts pass through a conifold transition?

A small resolution Y replaces each node by a rational curve. On the smoothing S those exceptional classes disappear. The map π from the second homology of Y to that of S can therefore send several effective classes to a single class β.

Divide the universal series of Y by the genus-zero factor of each exceptional curve, keeping its actual homology class. Write ñ for the balanced exponents of this normalized series. For each nonzero effective smoothing class β and each genus h, the specialization law is

nβ,h(S)= ∑πγ=β n~γ,h(Y).

Only classes whose lifts to the common blowup are effective can contribute. This makes the sum finite in bounded target degree. In scalar factors one also has a finite sum, now of rational exponents. It preserves integrality when the resolution exponents are integral.

The hypotheses are a projective smoothing with smooth algebraic total space, ordinary double points, a projective small resolution, and smooth endpoints with trivial canonical bundles and torsion-free second homology. When H1(𝒪) and H2(𝒪) vanish at both endpoints, fixed-target Gromov–Witten counts prove the balanced formula. In the broader setting the reconstruction assumes that connected Gromov–Witten evaluations descend to the relations from analytic families. The coefficients in a four-node transition

Over the primitive line class of a plane on the smoothing, there are 37 candidate lifts. Eight contribute. Their sum agrees with a stable-pair calculation on the plane and gives balanced exponent 3 for the line in genus zero. The smoothing calculation also gives exponent −6 for its double. The reconstruction of the individual source coefficients uses stable-pair evaluation on geometric local classes.

The support on a sixteen-node quintic

For the quintic containing a plane, write a resolution class as (a, d), where d is its degree on the smoothing. After the sixteen exceptional factors are removed, the candidate lifts satisfy 0 ≤ a ≤ 4d. A line in the plane realizes the upper edge at (4, 1). Which candidates have nonzero coefficients, and with what values? The support bound leaves that calculation open.

How does the contribution of a single curve change?

Keep an embedded curve fixed and vary its normal Cauchy–Riemann operator. When the pullback operator on a cover acquires a kernel, that cover has an infinitesimal deformation away from the curve. Crossing such a wall can change the local count even when the total count on the compact target stays fixed.

Two unramified double covers

Two independent double covers of a curve of genus at least two have a common fourfold cover. When their two walls meet transversely in the constructed family, the alternating sum of the connected counts in the four chambers, in degree 4m downstairs, is the degree-m local invariant on the common cover. This holds in every source genus at each fixed degree cutoff. The terms surviving the alternating sum come from maps that lift to both double covers. Unramified covers and wall crossing

A branched cover changes what the signs can see

A triple cover of a genus-two curve, simply branched at two values, has genus five. At a crossing realized on this cover, the genus-five elementary multiplicity in degree three changes by two in absolute value. Every determinant sign on connected unramified covers through degree seven stays the same. On a fixed symplectic six-torus, the other curves compensate in the same integral class and elementary genus. How to allocate that compensation along an arbitrary compact deformation, and how to combine simultaneous ramified crossings, remain open. Branched triple covers and wall crossing

An absolute count from spin structures

There are also settings in which one can compute the full local series at a single endpoint. For a curve of genus at least two with positive maximal flat symplectic normal holonomy, the harmonic reduction determines an orthogonal bundle. When it is orientable, the answer is the Bryan–Pandharipande antidiagonal series for the corresponding splitting degrees. When it is nonorientable only self-conjugate partitions contribute, with the degree variable multiplied by the determinant sign on the embedded curve. Both formulas cover every degree and source genus. Spin structures and local Gromov–Witten invariants of curves

  1. 01

    Integral generators for curve counting on threefolds

    Opposite circle weights give explicit integral polynomial generators and a unique product on the zero-Chern face of a compact Kähler threefold with nonnegative first Chern class on curves.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Pardon’s integral curve-counting algebra has geometric generators whose local models may depend on the covering multiplicity. In first Chern number and virtual dimension zero we give a single model in each genus for all multiplicities. On a genus-g curve take two line bundles of degree g−1 with canonical determinant and act on them with opposite weights. The equivariant classes of the multiple zero sections freely generate the integral algebra. The comparison with an isolated holomorphic multiple is triangular in multiplicity, with leading coefficient one given by the complex orientation of the embedded curve. The resulting factors give a unique product with integral exponents for effective cycles of Chern number zero on every compact Kähler threefold whose first Chern class is nonnegative on curves.

  2. 02

    A counterexample to integral product exponents in universal curve counting

    Arithmetic boundary: a compact scalar exponent with fractional part 2/3, alongside integrality through divisibility two.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold with torsion-free second homology whose product expansion in Pardon's scalar equivariant local-curve generators has a nonintegral exponent. The genus-ten exponent at three times a primitive class has fractional part 23, disproving the integer-exponent assertion for these generators. The obstruction comes from an exact degree-three calculation in the local Gromov–Witten Frobenius algebra. Comparison with the integer elementary expansion of Ionel–Parker gives a necessary divisibility condition on the primitive genus-three count. We realize a count violating this condition on a first Hirzebruch surface in a compact toric hypersurface. A filtration by powers of the surface ideal controls transverse thickenings and disconnected products; the stable-pair moduli space at minimal Euler characteristic is a projective space of dimension thirteen, with virtual count −14. We also prove integrality in classes of divisibility at most two. The counterexample concerns the scalar local factors; the integer elementary expansion is an input to its proof.

  3. 03

    Antidiagonal local curves and integral exponents in universal curve counting

    Integral exponents: opposite circle weights realize the elementary-cluster factors under the universal Gromov–Witten evaluation.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Assuming Pardon's Gromov–Witten evaluation for analytic families of threefolds, we lift the elementary-cluster expansion of Ionel and Parker to his rational algebra of one-cycles. For every smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold the universal series factors uniquely with the elementary-cluster integers as exponents, indexed by effective classes in integral homology. We construct the genus-g factor using the volume-preserving circle in the torus that scales the two line summands of a balanced local curve. Symmetric functions of the two fiber projections isolate the multiples of the zero section and give compactly supported classes compatible with addition of cycles and multiplication by positive integers. Among series with these compatibilities, the local Gromov–Witten series of an elementary cluster determines the universal factor. Among split local Calabi–Yau curves with the antidiagonal action, agreement with the elementary cluster in degree two already forces the splitting degrees to be balanced.

  4. 04

    A geometric product formula for universal curve counting on Calabi–Yau threefolds

    A geometric proof of the antidiagonal product identifies its integer exponents with signed elementary-cluster multiplicities.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove a product formula for the universal series of effective one-cycles on a smooth projective threefold X with trivial canonical bundle and H1(X,𝒪X)=H2(X,𝒪X)=0. The identity holds in Pardon's rational cycle algebra. Its genus-g factor is defined by the circle with opposite weights on two line bundles of degree g−1 whose tensor product is the canonical bundle of the curve. The exponents are the elementary-cluster integers of Ionel and Parker, with finite genus support in each integral curve class. We construct these integers as signed multiplicities in a decomposition of the cycles on X. In the algebra formed from almost-complex families, a deformation on the sum of two copies of a spin line identifies each antidiagonal class with the point class of the corresponding multiple of an elementary curve. Excision for disconnected cycles factors the image of the series of X at each energy bound. Stable maps of fixed genus distinguish the multiplicity-one classes, which suffices to lift the product to the complex cycle algebra.

  5. 05

    Triple curves and unbounded denominators in threefold curve counting

    Scalar exponents have unbounded denominators on one smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold, even in classes of divisibility three.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We exhibit a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold on which the exponents of the scalar local factors in the universal series of effective curves have unbounded denominators, even among homology classes of divisibility three. These factors are defined by scaling both normal directions to a curve with the same weight. For a curve of odd genus 2k+1, the scalar class of its tripled zero section has exact denominator 3k in the integral cycle algebra in Chern number and virtual dimension zero. We also determine the denominator in even genus. From even genus four onward it is smaller than the least product of weights needed to isolate the triple by single-fiber evaluations, even allowing the normal bundle to vary. A deformation separates the moving curves. Counting their embedded images in the cycle space and their contributions in every source genus on the same family determines the difference of the universal classes.

  1. 06

    Universal curve counting under conifold transitions

    Specialization: a rational universal transition formula and a complete compact source calculation with 37 candidates.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For projective conifold transitions of Calabi–Yau threefolds, we lift the numerical degeneration formula to Pardon's rational universal curve-counting algebra. We assume a smoothing with smooth algebraic total space and torsion-free second homology at both endpoints. After removing the exceptional local-curve factors, effective lifts to the common blow-up give a support bound that makes specialization finite in every bounded target degree. We carry out this specialization completely in the primitive line class of a plane in the smoothing of a compact four-node transition. The source calculation uses an Euler-characteristic bound for pure curves on two intersecting rational surfaces, allowing arbitrary multiple exceptional components. Together with a finite genus-zero mirror calculation, this bound determines all 37 candidate universal coefficients: eight are nonzero, and their sum agrees with an independent stable-pair calculation on a plane in the smoothing. We also determine the smoothing coefficient in twice the line class. If the resolution has integral product exponents, then so does the smoothing.

  1. 07

    Unramified covers and wall crossing for local Gromov–Witten invariants

    At two independent double-cover walls the alternating change in all source genera is the local invariant of their common fourfold cover.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Let two independent unramified double covers of an embedded curve acquire normal deformations along transverse walls. We construct such a family over every curve of genus at least two with a complex rank-two normal bundle of degree twice the genus minus two. The alternating difference of the local Gromov–Witten invariants in its four chambers equals the local invariant on the common fourfold cover, in every source genus. At each fixed covering-degree bound one neighborhood suffices for all genera. On a finite principal unramified cover we realize simultaneous crossings for pairwise inequivalent nontrivial irreducible representations of real type, provided their associated flat bundles together embed in the underlying real normal bundle. Fixed vectors determine the signs, and the result is a sum of local invariants on intermediate covers. Along a path of these crossings the determinant signs transport the initial series on the covers in all genera. For arbitrary rigid endpoints, the signs at both endpoints determine the change in the least source genus, the genus of an unramified cover of the given degree.

  2. 08

    Branched triple covers and wall crossing for local curve counts

    A branched cubic crossing changes the genus-five multiplicity while all unramified determinant signs through degree seven agree.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    A branched triple cover of a genus-two curve can change the local curve count while preserving all determinant signs on unramified covers of degrees at most seven. At the crossing the degree-three elementary multiplicity in genus five changes by two in absolute value. At a generic transverse crossing on a prescribed simply branched triple cover with symmetric monodromy over a curve of genus at least two, we determine the local Gromov–Witten change in degree seven and every source genus. Each nonempty subset of branch values gives a nodal map joining the sextic Galois closure to the original curve. Its contribution is the degree-one series of a smooth curve, with sign determined by the number of nodes and the sum of their branch weights, up to a common orientation sign. On a fixed symplectic six-torus the other curves compensate each change in the same integral homology class and elementary genus.

  3. 09

    Spin structures and local Gromov–Witten invariants of curves

    For positive maximal flat symplectic normal holonomy, a spin gluing sign determines the partition sum in every degree and source genus.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We compute the local Gromov–Witten invariants of a curve of genus at least two with positive maximal flat symplectic normal holonomy, in every covering degree and source genus. The orthogonal bundle of the harmonic reduction determines which partitions occur. When it is orientable, the series agrees with the antidiagonal local-curve series of Bryan and Pandharipande. When it is nonorientable, only self-conjugate partitions contribute. The determinant sign on the embedded curve multiplies the degree variable in this sum. The restriction on partitions comes from the change of orientation at the relative contacts when two spin structures are glued across a nonseparating node. On the closure of the locus of triple covers with four simple branch points over a genus-two curve, we compute the intersection matrix of the ten classes measuring the difference between the real spin Euler class and the corresponding complex Chern class, one for each even spin structure.
