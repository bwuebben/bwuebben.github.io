What does a metric remember about a complex?

A representation of a quiver assigns vector spaces to vertices and maps to arrows. Letting the Hermitian metrics on these spaces evolve reveals which subrepresentations destabilize it. For a complex, the differential changes the metric flow even on summands that vanish in cohomology.

A metric on a complex.

A quiver is a directed graph. A representation assigns a complex vector space to each vertex and a linear map to each arrow. A subrepresentation consists of subspaces preserved by all the arrow maps. Give each vertex a charge in the upper half-plane. Adding these charges with multiplicities equal to the dimensions gives the central charge of the representation. Its argument is called its phase.

A representation is semistable when every nonzero subrepresentation has phase at most its own. Stability requires strict inequality for every proper nonzero subrepresentation. A direct sum of stable representations of the same phase is polystable. Every representation has a canonical Harder–Narasimhan filtration whose successive quotients are semistable and have strictly decreasing phases.

One can also put a complex of vector spaces at each vertex, with arrow maps commuting with the differentials. Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit associate an endomorphism to such a complex with Hermitian metrics. The phase of its unitary polar factor drives a flow of the metrics. The linear maps and differentials stay fixed while lengths and angles change. A harmonic metric on a representation solves the moment-map equation at the parameter set by its charges. When does the flow see only cohomology?

Start with the generalized Kronecker quiver, two vertices joined by parallel arrows in the same direction. In degree zero put spaces V and W at the source and target. Put a nonzero space U at the source in degree one and take a surjective differential D from V to U. The cohomology has source ker D and target W, both assumed nonzero. Although passing to cohomology removes an exact pair, that pair initially contributes to the endomorphism driving the metric.

From every initial metric, the endomorphism on the complex approaches exponentially in trace norm the endomorphism on its cohomology, computed with the moving induced metrics and extended by zero. This cancellation holds for all vertex charges in the upper half-plane. When the source charge has larger argument than the target charge, polystable cohomology has a stronger conclusion. After scalar rescaling the induced metrics converge exponentially to a harmonic metric. For semistable cohomology the shifted moment map tends to zero. Convergence on Kronecker quiver complexes

There is also a metric statement about the space of stable cohomology representations. In a holomorphic family with ordered charges and stable cohomology, the infimum of the potential on metrics is real analytic. Applying i∂∂̄ to this function recovers the pullback of the Kähler form of the stable moduli space.

Which potential decreases?

The Kempf–Ness function relates stability to harmonic metrics on quiver representations. On bounded complexes of representations of any finite quiver, the endomorphism defining the flow gives a closed complex-valued one-form on the space of metrics. Integrating along a rescaling of the differentials supplies a primitive. For nonzero total charge, one can rotate this primitive by the negative of the total phase and take its imaginary part. On representations the resulting real function is a positive multiple of the Kempf–Ness function.

Whether this function decreases requires a separate calculation. Suppose the complex is concentrated in degrees zero and one, and its differential surjects onto a term supported at a vertex without loops. The potential is nonincreasing if the charge there has argument at least the total phase and either the total phase is at least π/3 or the differential has rank one. On a three-term Kronecker complex the potential can increase even when the metric induced on its stable cohomology is harmonic. Extending the Kempf–Ness function to complexes

Quillen’s superconnection gives a corresponding potential for two-term complexes of holomorphic bundles on a compact Riemann surface. On a single bundle it recovers a positive multiple of Donaldson’s functional. Existence of the associated bundle flow remains open here.

Recovering the filtration from lengths.

For any nonzero representation of a finite quiver without oriented cycles, take a vector in one step of the Harder–Narasimhan filtration, outside the preceding step. Along the minimizing flow, its log squared length divided by time tends to minus the phase of that quotient. Thus one can recover the entire filtration by comparing the rates at which vectors shrink. This holds for all upper-half-plane vertex charges and all initial metrics.

The mass of the evolving metric tends to the sum of the absolute central charges of the successive quotients. On a polystable quotient the rescaled metric converges exponentially to a harmonic metric. On a general semistable quotient it stays at bounded distance from a weighted Kempf–Ness gradient trajectory. This gives the finer iterated-logarithm asymptotics up to bounded factors. Convergence on acyclic quivers

The same conclusions hold on cohomology for two-term complexes whose nonzero cohomology lies in degree zero and whose differential is supported at one vertex. For generalized Kronecker quivers the comparison also treats bounded complexes whose differentials vanish at one vertex, with the phase shifted according to degree. Through Beilinson’s equivalence, this covers a minimal projective or injective Kronecker presentation of every object of the bounded derived category of the projective line. Convergence for arbitrary presentations with differentials at several vertices remains open.

Earlier phase flows and harmonic paths.

The first examples in the Stability & Degeneration guide showed that trace-norm mass can increase along the flow. They established cancellation for surjective Kronecker source differentials under a large initial singular-value hypothesis. The convergence theorem above removes that initial-scale condition for the stated two-term complexes. Mass increase and acyclic cancellation

A different question in categorical Kähler geometry prescribes boundary norms over a Novikov field and asks for a harmonic path between them. For the identity representation of the A₂ quiver, symmetric charges and a fixed radial area form, full split boundary triples give paths with finite labelled honeycombs.     Convergence of the minimizing flow on Kronecker quiver complexes

    Exponential cancellation of the acyclic part of a surjective two-term Kronecker complex, with harmonic convergence for polystable cohomology in the ordered chamber.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove exponential cancellation of the acyclic part of a two-term complex (V→W)→(U→0) of generalized Kronecker quiver representations under the minimizing flow of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit. The differential D:V→U is surjective with U≠0, and the cohomology H=(kerD→W) is nonzero at both vertices. For all vertex charges in the upper half-plane and every initial Hermitian metric, the endomorphism whose polar phase defines the flow approaches the cohomology endomorphism exponentially in trace norm. Here H carries the induced metric and its endomorphism is extended by zero. If the source charge has larger argument than the target charge and H is polystable, the induced metric multiplied by etargZ(H) converges exponentially to a harmonic metric, where Z(H) is the central charge of H. In the same chamber, semistability suffices for the shifted moment map to tend to zero. Two matrix estimates control the arrow energy. At fixed positive definite imaginary part, minus the argument of the unitary polar factor is strictly monotone for the trace inner product. Schur multiplication by the second divided differences of e−x at ℓi,0,ℓj, with all ℓi>0, sends each positive semidefinite matrix to a matrix J whose negative part has trace at most 25trJ.

  1. 02

    Extending the Kempf–Ness function to complexes of quiver representations

    A primitive for bounded quiver complexes, conditions for monotonicity, a three-term counterexample and a superconnection construction on curves.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For every bounded complex of representations of a finite quiver with vertex charges in the upper half-plane, the superconnection endomorphism of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit defines a closed one-form on the space of Hermitian metrics. We construct a primitive by transgression along the rescaling of the differentials. For nonzero total charge Z, rotating the primitive by Z―/|Z| and taking its imaginary part gives a real potential. On representations this potential is a positive multiple of the Kempf–Ness function and is nonincreasing along the flow defined by the argument of the unitary polar factor of the endomorphism. For a two-term complex in degrees zero and one whose differential surjects onto a term supported at a loopless vertex i, the potential is nonincreasing if the vertex charge zi satisfies argzi≥argZ and either argZ≥π/3 or the differential has rank one. On a three-term Kronecker quiver complex the potential can increase even when the induced metric on its stable cohomology is harmonic. Quillen's superconnection gives the analogous potential for two-term complexes of holomorphic bundles on a compact Riemann surface. On a single bundle it is a positive multiple of Donaldson's functional.

  1. 03

    Convergence of the minimizing flow on acyclic quivers

    The Harder–Narasimhan filtration from metric decay rates on acyclic quivers, with corresponding cohomology results for specified complexes.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    The minimizing flow of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit recovers the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of every nonzero representation of a finite acyclic quiver from the exponential decay rates of its Hermitian metric h(t). This holds for all vertex charges in the upper half-plane and every initial metric. If v lies in the kth filtration step outside the preceding one and Zk is the central charge of the kth quotient, then t−1logh(t)(v,v) tends to −argZk. The mass of the evolving metric tends to ∑k|Zk|. On each quotient the metric rescaled by etargZk converges exponentially to a harmonic metric if the quotient is polystable. In general the rescaled metric stays at bounded distance from a Kempf–Ness gradient trajectory with vertex weights proportional to the imaginary parts of the charges. The iterated-logarithm asymptotics of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit therefore apply up to bounded factors. For two-term complexes with nonzero cohomology concentrated in degree zero and differential supported at one vertex, the same results hold on cohomology when the phase is taken from the polar decomposition. For generalized Kronecker quivers the results extend to bounded complexes whose differentials vanish at one vertex, with the phases shifted by −πn in degree n. Under Beilinson's equivalence this includes every object of Db(ℙ1) in a minimal projective or injective Kronecker model.
