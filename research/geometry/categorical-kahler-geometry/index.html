Categorical Kähler geometry on quivers

Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit proposed studying stability by evolving Hermitian metrics. I study their flow on quiver representations, diagrams of vector spaces and linear maps, and on complexes of these diagrams.

For every nonzero representation of a finite acyclic quiver, the rates at which vectors shrink recover its Harder–Narasimhan filtration. On certain complexes mass can rise while the acyclic part decays. Over a Novikov field, I construct harmonic paths from boundary norms in the A₂ honeycomb case.

Stability and the minimizing flow.

Give each vertex of a quiver a complex charge in the upper half-plane. The central charge of a representation is the sum of the vertex charges weighted by the dimensions of their vector spaces. Its argument is the phase. A subrepresentation is a choice of subspaces preserved by the arrows. A representation is semistable if every nonzero subrepresentation has phase at most its own, and stable if the inequality is strict for every proper nonzero subrepresentation. A direct sum of stable representations of the same phase is polystable.

Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit associate an endomorphism to a representation with Hermitian metrics. The phase of this endomorphism drives a flow in which the arrow maps stay fixed and the metrics vary. A harmonic metric solves the moment-map equation at the parameter set by the charges. On a representation such a metric exists precisely when the representation is polystable. Towards Categorical Kähler Geometry

Recovering the filtration from lengths.

Every nonzero representation has a canonical Harder–Narasimhan filtration by subrepresentations. The successive quotients are semistable and their phases strictly decrease. For a finite quiver without oriented cycles, this algebraic filtration can be read off from a single trajectory of the minimizing flow. Take a vector in one filtration step, outside the preceding step. The logarithm of its squared length divided by time tends to minus the phase of that quotient. This holds for every initial metric and all upper-half-plane vertex charges.

The two lines in the opening example show the rule in its simplest form. Their squared lengths decay at rates π/2 and π/4, so the first line gives the higher-phase subrepresentation. For a general representation the mass decreases to the sum of the absolute central charges of the filtration quotients. On each polystable quotient the rescaled metric converges exponentially to a harmonic metric. On a general semistable quotient it stays at bounded distance from a weighted Kempf–Ness gradient trajectory, giving the finer iterated-logarithm asymptotics up to bounded factors. Convergence of the minimizing flow on acyclic quivers

For a two-term complex with nonzero cohomology concentrated in degree zero and differential supported at one vertex, the filtration theorem holds on cohomology. For a generalized Kronecker quiver it also applies degree by degree to bounded complexes whose differentials vanish at one vertex, with the phase shifted by −πn in degree n. Through Beilinson’s equivalence this includes a minimal projective or injective Kronecker presentation of every object of the bounded derived category of the projective line. The long-time behaviour for general presentations with differentials at several vertices remains open.

Mass can increase on a complex.

For a complex, each vertex carries a sequence of vector spaces and differentials, and the arrows commute with the differentials. The resulting superconnection endomorphism can be nonnormal. I use the argument of its unitary polar factor, with the phase branch prescribed in each degree. For this convention, a two-term example has trace-norm mass with strictly positive initial derivative. This gives a negative answer to Question 5.18 of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit.

The same work gives a first cancellation theorem. For a surjective source differential on a two-term Kronecker complex, a sufficiently large initial least singular value makes the endomorphism approach the one on cohomology exponentially, under the stated bounds on the arrows. Paper I shows how the flow reaches that regime from an arbitrary initial metric in the setting below. Mass increase and acyclic cancellation for phase flows on quiver complexes

When does the flow see only cohomology?

Take the generalized Kronecker quiver, two vertices joined by parallel arrows in the same direction. In degree zero put spaces V and W at the source and target. Put a nonzero space U at the source in degree one, and take a surjective differential D from V to U. The cohomology has source ker D and target W, both assumed nonzero. The exact pair removed by passing to cohomology still contributes to the endomorphism driving the flow.

From every initial metric, for all upper-half-plane vertex charges, the endomorphism on the complex approaches exponentially in trace norm the endomorphism on cohomology, computed with the moving induced metrics and extended by zero. When the source charge has larger argument than the target charge and the cohomology is polystable, the induced metric on cohomology converges exponentially after scalar rescaling to a harmonic metric. For semistable cohomology in this chamber, the shifted moment map tends to zero. Convergence of the minimizing flow on Kronecker quiver complexes

The potential and the Kähler form on moduli.

For every bounded complex of representations of a finite quiver, the superconnection endomorphism defines a closed complex-valued one-form on the space of Hermitian metrics. Integrating along a rescaling of the differentials gives an explicit primitive. For nonzero total charge, rotating the primitive by minus the total phase and taking its imaginary part gives a real potential. On representations it is a positive multiple of the Kempf–Ness function.

This potential has its own monotonicity criterion. Suppose a complex lies in degrees zero and one and the differential surjects onto a term supported at a loopless vertex. The potential is nonincreasing if the argument of the charge there is at least the total phase and either the total phase is at least π/3 or the differential has rank one. In a three-term Kronecker example the potential increases even when the metric induced on stable cohomology is harmonic. Extending the Kempf–Ness function to complexes of quiver representations

For the two-term Kronecker complexes above, the potential also recovers the geometry of the moduli space. In a holomorphic family with ordered charges and stable cohomology, the infimum of the phase potential over metrics is real analytic. Applying i∂∂̄ to that infimum gives the pullback of the Kähler form on stable cohomology moduli. In particular the form vanishes along directions in which the isomorphism class of the cohomology stays fixed. Paper I, §8

The matrix estimate behind the Kronecker bounds

Take the positive eigenvalues ℓi of DD† and form the matrix of second divided differences of e−x at ℓi, 0, ℓj. Multiply this matrix entrywise by any positive semidefinite matrix. The trace of the negative part of the result is at most one third of its trace. This estimate enters the cancellation proof and gives strict decrease of the phase potential in the ordered chamber when the total phase is at least π/3.

Quillen’s superconnection gives a corresponding potential for two-term complexes of holomorphic bundles on a compact Riemann surface. On a single bundle this recovers a positive multiple of Donaldson’s functional. Existence of the associated bundle flow remains open here. For a related limit on weight-filtration asymptotics on surfaces, see Singular graded limits and metric growth under Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow.

Non-Archimedean harmonic paths and honeycombs.

A second problem starts with boundary norms. Take the A₂ quiver with the same vector space at both vertices and the identity along its one arrow. Over the real-exponent Novikov field, prescribe three norms, each diagonalizable in some basis. In the honeycomb case of Conjecture 7.10 of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit, the aim is to realize these boundary data by a harmonic path of norms.

For symmetric central charges and a fixed radial area form, every such triple in every finite rank admits a harmonic path. Minimizing a renormalized convex action constructs the path. Its spectral velocities trace a finite honeycomb in the plane, with semistable labels on its edges and compatible filtrations at its vertices. Repeated boundary spectra and collapsed edges are included.

The path is unique among finite regular paths whose one-sided norm motions agree to first order at each join. In rank-three examples, the subspaces carrying the velocities can switch at a join while preserving the boundary data and labelled support. First-order matching excludes these switches. Whether categorical vertex data alone force first-order matching remains open. A Lipschitz estimate holds for one family with a collapsing face. Continuity for arbitrary boundary data and a global Fukaya–Maurer–Cartan correspondence remain open. Harmonic paths and honeycombs

  1. 11

    Mass increase and acyclic cancellation for phase flows on quiver complexes

    Categorical phase flows: mass increase, general large-scale acyclic cancellation and relaxation under a finite cohomology-energy infimum.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Trace-norm mass can increase along the Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pandit phase flow defined by positivity, giving a negative answer to the proposed mass-monotonicity question with the prescribed degree branches. For two-term complexes over a two-vertex parallel-arrow quiver, with degree-one term supported at the source and surjective source differential, we nevertheless prove quantitative acyclic cancellation. For fixed upper-half-plane charges, arbitrary surviving tuples and bounded initial arrow norms, a sufficiently large least acyclic singular value gives exponential comparison with instantaneous cohomology. The estimates allow unequal surviving dimensions and are independent of arrow count, stabilizers and acyclic condition numbers. The proof controls the polar phase using only the nonsingular cohomology block. For equal surviving dimensions and charges −1+i, 1+i, a finite infimum of the cohomology Kempf–Ness function forces its moment-map defects to tend to zero, without initial balance or attainment of the infimum. When the source differential is invertible, cancellation holds from every positive metric without a large-scale assumption, and the normalized cohomology metric converges. At fixed external differentials, static mass converges in C1 in bounded arrow coordinates. An explicit family converges in C2 while every positive Hölder seminorm of its Hessian diverges. An exact metric primitive also identifies a positive cohomology potential and a negative ambient Levi direction.

    Convergence of the minimizing flow on Kronecker quiver complexes

    Exponential cancellation of the acyclic part of a surjective two-term Kronecker complex, with harmonic convergence for polystable cohomology in the ordered chamber.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For a two-term complex (V→W)→(U→0) of generalized Kronecker quiver representations, we prove exponential cancellation of its acyclic part under the polar-phase interpretation of the minimizing flow of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit. Suppose the differential D:V→U is surjective, U≠0, and the cohomology H=(kerD→W) is nonzero at both vertices. For all vertex charges in the upper half-plane and every initial Hermitian metric, the endomorphism defining the flow approaches that of H exponentially in trace norm, with the induced metric on H and extension by zero. If the source charge has larger argument than the target charge and H is polystable, the induced metric multiplied by etargZ(H) converges exponentially to a harmonic metric, where Z(H) is the central charge of the cohomology. In this chamber semistability suffices for the shifted moment map to tend to zero. The arrow-energy estimate uses the kernel of second divided differences of e−x at the nodes ℓi,0,ℓj with ℓi>0. Its Schur product J with any positive semidefinite matrix satisfies trJ−≤13trJ, where J− is the negative part. We also prove that, at fixed positive definite imaginary part, minus the polar phase is strictly monotone for the trace inner product.

  2. 02

    Extending the Kempf–Ness function to complexes of quiver representations

    A primitive for bounded quiver complexes, conditions for monotonicity, a three-term counterexample and a superconnection construction on curves.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For every bounded complex of representations of a finite quiver with vertex charges in the upper half-plane, the superconnection endomorphism of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit defines a closed one-form on the space of Hermitian metrics. We construct a primitive by transgression along the rescaling of the differentials. For nonzero total charge Z, rotating the primitive by Z―/|Z| and taking its imaginary part gives a real potential. On representations this potential is a positive multiple of the Kempf–Ness function and is nonincreasing along the flow defined by the argument of the unitary polar factor of the endomorphism. For a two-term complex in degrees zero and one whose differential surjects onto a term supported at a loopless vertex i, the potential is nonincreasing if the vertex charge zi satisfies argzi≥argZ and either argZ≥π/3 or the differential has rank one. On a three-term Kronecker quiver complex the potential can increase even when the induced metric on its stable cohomology is harmonic. Quillen's superconnection gives the analogous potential for two-term complexes of holomorphic bundles on a compact Riemann surface. On a single bundle it is a positive multiple of Donaldson's functional.

  3. 03

    Convergence of the minimizing flow on acyclic quivers

    The Harder–Narasimhan filtration from metric decay rates on acyclic quivers, with corresponding cohomology results for specified complexes.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    The minimizing flow of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit recovers the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of every nonzero representation of a finite acyclic quiver from the exponential decay rates of its Hermitian metric h(t). This holds for all vertex charges in the upper half-plane and every initial metric. If v lies in the kth filtration step outside the preceding one and Zk is the central charge of the kth quotient, then t−1logh(t)(v,v) tends to −argZk. The mass of the evolving metric tends to ∑k|Zk|. On each quotient the metric rescaled by etargZk converges exponentially to a harmonic metric if the quotient is polystable. In general the rescaled metric stays at bounded distance from a Kempf–Ness gradient trajectory with vertex weights proportional to the imaginary parts of the charges. The iterated-logarithm asymptotics of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit therefore apply up to bounded factors. For two-term complexes with nonzero cohomology concentrated in degree zero and differential supported at one vertex, the same results hold on cohomology when the phase is taken from the polar decomposition. For generalized Kronecker quivers the results extend to bounded complexes whose differentials vanish at one vertex, with the phases shifted by −πn in degree n. Under Beilinson's equivalence this includes every object of Db(ℙ1) in a minimal projective or injective Kronecker model.

  1. 12

    Harmonic paths and honeycombs

    Harmonic norm paths from all full split boundary triples in every finite rank, with finite labelled honeycombs and uniqueness under first-order matching.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct harmonic paths of non-expansive norms on the identity representation of the A₂ quiver from arbitrary triples of diagonalizable boundary norms over the real-exponent Novikov field. We use symmetric central charges and a fixed radial area form on the plane. In every finite rank, the path has a finite apartment presentation and a finite honeycomb support with actual semistable edge labels and compatible filtrations at its vertices. It is unique among paths with such presentations whose one-sided norm motions agree to first order at each join. The construction minimizes a renormalized convex action. Its algebraic regularity follows from fixed orthogonal subspaces at gaps in the spectral velocities, second-order scalar approximations, and descent of finite norm data from the Hahn field. Repeated boundary spectra and collapsed edges are included. The result gives existence in the honeycomb specialization of the harmonic-path conjecture of Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pandit, with uniqueness under an explicit matching condition absent from the printed edgewise definition.
