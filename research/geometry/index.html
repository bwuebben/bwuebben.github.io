Research / Geometry & moduli

How does geometry organize its possibilities?

As a bundle approaches a stability wall, its metric loses rigidity and small Dolbeault eigenvalues record the scales of that loss. One programme connects algebraic chambers to spectra, metrics on bundles and moduli spaces, and geometric flows, including quotient metrics when stabilizers change. Separate projects concern enumerative wall crossing and the realization of prescribed moduli structures.

Scope of the work

The stability programme proves a connection between chamber geometry, spectra, metrics and flows on a specified family of weak-Fano toric tangent bundles. Its quotient-metric branch begins with uniform relative estimates for central Yang–Mills connections on closed surfaces, with explicit quaternionic space-form comparisons. Later results treat specified three-step extensions, a rank-five repeated-factor deformation problem, full local quotient metrics, and stable, wall and unstable flow. A local rank-three K3 application identifies actual harmonic metric wall crossing through changing stabilizers, including collapse scales and complete rescaled limits. A general local harmonic/quiver comparison on projective K3 surfaces extends the metric method through arbitrary stabilizer changes and is realized by a rank-five, two-parameter example with five walls. Complete rescaled limits retain explicit parameter and base-point convergence hypotheses. A general rank-two surface theorem obstructs two bounded-remainder extensions of weight-filtration metric asymptotics to singular graded sheaves, including nonreduced singularities and repeated factors. Finite-dimensional categorical phase flows admit mass increase together with acyclic cancellation for invertible source differentials and, at large initial scale, arbitrary surviving tuples for surjective source differentials. Nonbalanced cohomology relaxes under a separate finite-infimum hypothesis in equal dimensions at the specified charges. A harmonic-path theorem treats all full split boundary triples in every finite rank for a specified A₂ representation over the Novikov field; its uniqueness statement retains first-order matching. The general compact-Kähler local theorem retains the full convergent obstruction and compares actual HE metrics, every small endomorphism eigenvalue and harmonic quotient metrics at any fixed locally free polystable background, with quadratic error in canonical harmonic coordinates. A compact bicubic example has essential cubic bundle-deformation equations. Near an arbitrary fixed locally free polystable bundle, confined HYM trajectories with small initial normal displacement admit an exact finite-dimensional comparison flow with C¹ initial phase and exponentially vanishing relative metric error. The full obstruction and arbitrary multiplicities are retained, uniformly through weak finite scales and stabilizer changes. Universal confinement, algebraic classification of the corrected dynamics, global completion and a replacement singular asymptotic law remain open. Open FJRW theory and inverse moduli remain separate projects, each with its own hypotheses and conclusions.

Stability and Geometric Degeneration Integrated programme

From realizable chambers to metrics, moduli and flows.

Can finite deformation and symmetry data predict how geometry loses rigidity? The programme connects realizable algebraic stability chambers, small Dolbeault eigenvalues, Hermitian–Einstein metrics and geometric flows, with relative estimates that remain meaningful when positive scales vanish at very different rates.

The two-step theory joins these descriptions on the same weak-Fano toric tangent bundles. Later papers treat specified three-step extensions, repeated factors, local moduli metrics and initial-condition-sensitive dynamics. A general rank-two surface theorem shows that two proposed bounded-remainder metric asymptotics require modification for singular graded quotients, even away from the singularities. It includes nonreduced zeros and repeated factors, with toric and K3 examples. General local dynamical reduction is now available under confinement. Universal confinement and the replacement singular growth law remain open.

Singular quotient metrics and moduli spaces

The quotient-metric branch runs from reducible Yang–Mills connections on closed surfaces to arbitrary fixed locally free polystable backgrounds on compact Kähler manifolds. It distinguishes changes of stabilizer from singularities of the graded sheaf itself. Read the comparison and its hypotheses in the focused guide ↗

At the Wall gives a visual introduction to chambers, weak spectra and metric response.

A finite-dimensional branch studies phase flows on quiver complexes. Trace-norm mass can increase, yet invertible differentials give exponential acyclic cancellation. For surjective source differentials and a sufficiently large least initial acyclic singular value, arbitrary surviving tuples, unequal dimensions and fixed upper-half-plane charges admit exponential comparison with instantaneous cohomology. At fixed dimensions and norm bounds, constants are independent of arrow count, stabilizer and acyclic condition number. Relaxation follows separately in equal dimensions at charges −1+i and 1+i when the cohomology Kempf–Ness function has finite infimum. General quotient geometry remains open.

The K3 metric wall-crossing application uses the stationary and quotient-metric methods on a complex twelve-dimensional rank-three bundle-moduli neighborhood. Uniform comparison with cotangent-Grassmannian metrics gives exceptional-fibre collapse, complete rescaled limits and intrinsic distances through changes of stabilizer. The result is local; global completion and bubbling remain open.

The general K3 metric comparison retains every finite symmetry direction and gives uniform relative harmonic metrics through changing stabilizers, with linear spatial error unless a central sign symmetry gives quadratic error. Its rank-five doubled-triangle example has three spherical factors, two independent polarization parameters and five realized walls. Under the stated convergence conditions, intrinsic metric limits include singular parameter directions and finite successive rescalings.

The general local HYM model retains the full convergent Kuranishi obstruction and reconstructs every sufficiently nearby HE solution at any fixed locally free polystable compact-Kähler background. It compares the complete small endomorphism spectrum and harmonic quotient metrics uniformly through stabilizer changes and separated positive scales. Canonical harmonic coordinates give quadratic error, also with varying polarizations in moving backgrounds. A stable bundle on a smooth bicubic has essential cubic obstruction, and the theorem covers the full three-copy family.

The uniform asymptotic-phase theorem extends exact local HYM reduction to arbitrary fixed locally free polystable bundles, retaining the full convergent obstruction and all multiplicity directions. Under coefficient confinement and small initial normal displacement, an initial metric selects an exact finite trajectory through a C¹ phase map. Relative metric error decays exponentially with constants controlled by the complementary elliptic gap, independently of weak positive finite scales and stabilizer changes. Noncommuting and unbounded reconstructing gauges are allowed; an obstructed bicubic supplies explicit integrable examples.

The harmonic-path branch constructs actual norm paths and finite labelled honeycombs from every full split boundary triple in every finite rank, for the specified A₂ representation, Novikov field, symmetric charges and radial area form. Equality uniqueness holds in the finite regular principal-velocity class with first-order matching. General boundary continuity and a global Fukaya correspondence remain open.

Open FJRW theory Developing programme

From formal wall crossing to geometric scattering.

Open FJRW theory counts disks for the Landau–Ginzburg model xr + ys, and Gross, Kelly and Tessler showed that its invariants depend on how the boundary is fixed. Change the boundary conditions and the counts jump. Three papers ask where the jumps begin, how they are organized, and whether the organization is geometry: a scattering structure on an integral-affine surface, of the kind Gross and Siebert use to build mirrors.

What is proved

Where it begins. The primary invariants ignore the boundary conditions exactly when ĉ = 2 − 2/r − 2/s is below 1, which picks out the simple singularities A, D₄, E₆ and E₈. This proves a prediction Gross, Kelly and Tessler left open.

How it factors. Every change of boundary conditions factors uniquely, in each finite truncation, into automorphisms on rays ordered by slope. Homotopies of boundary conditions realize the product one ray at a time.

Where it lives. For x⁴ + y⁴, at ĉ = 1, the third paper builds an integral-affine annulus with hyperbolic core holonomy of trace 18, and a wall structure on it consistent at its two joints.

The missing construction

The annulus does not yet reproduce the theory. Its walls agree with Gross–Kelly–Tessler wall crossing for one marking and disagree for two: on the first mixed ray the annulus gives −3/4 where the theory requires 1/4. Periods cannot see the difference.

A normal form, imposed from outside, repairs it. The open problem is to construct that normal form from the annulus itself, as an operation at a boundary circle. The paper observes that any solution must be global.

Why it matters

Gross–Siebert scattering builds mirrors out of integral-affine geometry. If the wall crossing of an open Landau–Ginzburg theory supplied such a base, invariants that now depend on a choice of boundary conditions would acquire an intrinsic geometric home.

The second paper notes that theta functions could then recast the open mirror theorem as intrinsic mirror symmetry, and that this would need data not yet constructed. For the quartic the answer is partial, and the papers say so.

When Walls Collide ↗ follows one exact quartic example: two paths disagree, a correction repairs the joint, and the full potential detects a discrepancy that its period class misses.

Realization of moduli structures Exploratory frontier

From algebraic identities to geometric realizability.

Donaldson, Seiberg–Witten, Floer and Gromov–Witten theory all run in one direction: solve an equation, compactify the space of solutions, and read algebra off the way it breaks. The identity d² = 0 is what the boundary of a compact one-dimensional moduli space looks like. Inverse moduli theory runs the other way. Given the algebra, when can it come from broken solution spaces, and when from a natural equation?

What is proved

The equations d² = 0 and the A∞ relations are exactly the oriented filling conditions in dimension one. Above it, extensions are obstructed by smooth bordism classes that the algebra cannot see, and two framed realizations of the same chain complex can differ by η, the generator of the first stable stem.

The principal result is an obstruction on the pentagon K₄. Compatible stabilizing bundles on K₂ and K₃ extend exactly when their glued boundary bundle does, and reflecting one identification produces a Möbius band that no orientable enlargement removes. Doubling does remove it.

The open problem

The manuscript calls itself a sequence of realization and obstruction problems, not an existence theorem, and extracting gluing data from an algebra remains open.

One problem is sharp. At arity n the stable obstruction group is πn−4(O). Doubling kills every torsion class, so arity seven, where the group is ℤ, is the first place a stabilization datum could be obstructed beyond repair by enlargement. Whether that happens is the manuscript's Problem 12.6.

Why it matters

Floer homotopy theory already knows that a chain complex does not determine a Floer homotopy type. The inverse problem asks the sharper question of which algebra geometry can produce at all, and which information a homology theory discards on the way.

What would change the picture is a naturally occurring moduli problem in which one of these obstructions does not vanish. Until then the framework is a map of the questions, and this guide presents it as one.

A single manuscript of 106 pages. Family-index, compactness and determinant-line conditions supply further necessary conditions for Fredholm realization, examined in flow, planar, stable-curve and stable-disk examples. The essay Why broken things make invariants introduces the forward direction this theory reverses.

  1. 01

    Slope stability of tangent bundles of smooth toric Fano varieties

    Constructions of toric Fano varieties with stable tangent bundles, compared with Kähler–Einstein conditions.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct smooth toric Fano nn-folds of Picard number n+2n+2 whose tangent bundles are slope-stable with respect to the anticanonical polarization, for every n≥4n\ge4. The construction gives toric fibrations with fibre the del Pezzo surface of degree six over products of projective lines, parametrized by multisets of roots of A2A_2. Every nonempty multiset of nonzero roots with vanishing sum yields a stable tangent bundle. Among these multisets, the resulting variety is Kähler–Einstein if and only if the multiset is invariant under negation or under the order-three rotation of the root hexagon. We also construct stable, non-Kähler–Einstein examples with nonzero twist sum in every even dimension at least four. The proofs reduce slope inequalities and barycenter computations to integrals over the moment hexagon.

    For a smooth toric Fano variety with strictly semistable tangent bundle, we prove that polystability is equivalent to decomposition as a nontrivial product of smooth toric Fano varieties with anticanonically stable tangent bundles. Exact evaluation of Klyachko’s criterion extends the stability classification to dimensions five and six, recovering the classifications of Steffens and Reynolds in dimensions three and four. Together with the product criterion, this determines polystability for all 8,6308{,}630 varieties in dimensions three through six, and hence the existence of Hermitian–Einstein metrics on their tangent bundles with respect to an anticanonical Kähler form.

  2. 02

    Tangent stability on toric Fano blowups and bundles

    Stability criteria for specified Fano blowups and bundles, with a complete exact census of Picard-number-ten eightfolds.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For Fano blowups of products of projective spaces along invariant codimension-two centres indexed by a tree, using distinct coordinate hyperplanes at each factor, anticanonical tangent stability is equivalent to one divisor-degree inequality at each vertex. The exceptional contributions are mixed intersections on the blowup centres. Stability holds for every tree of maximum degree at most three whose nonleaf factor dimensions equal their valences, with arbitrary positive leaf dimensions. For smooth toric Fano varieties that are locally trivial toric bundles with fibre a product of degree-six del Pezzo surfaces and base a product of projective spaces, the largest Picard number compatible with tangent stability in dimension n≥4 is ⌊5n/4⌋+1, allowing arbitrary integral twists. Every fixed tree of maximum degree at most three with an edge yields bundles attaining this bound after sufficiently long independent edge subdivisions. Their number, up to isomorphism of toric fans, is unbounded as the dimension grows through multiples of four. The proofs use relative tangent bundles, ranks of subspaces spanned by rays, and integral comparisons. The bundle existence proofs also use finite exact rational calculations with uniform error estimates and induction.

  3. 03

    Local stability chambers for toric tangent bundles

    Varying the polarization: an all-dimensional projective-bundle classification with arbitrarily many chamber boundaries, a geometric criterion, and local chambers for the 31 indecomposable semistable census cases.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We classify the anticanonical slope stability of the tangent bundles of Xp,q=ℙ(ℙ1)p+q(O⊕O(1p,−1q)), where p,q are nonnegative integers and p+q≥1. The tangent bundle is stable when p=q, strictly semistable when |p−q|=1, and unstable otherwise. For p=k and q=k−1, the germ of the semistable polarization locus at the anticanonical class is real analytically equivalent to [0,∞)k×ℝk, with stable locus (0,∞)k×ℝk. In particular, the number of independent local chamber boundaries is unbounded.

    For a smooth projective toric variety whose tangent bundle is semistable at a polarization H0, we describe the local chamber at H0 by the order ideals of a finite poset determined by the equal-slope ray subspaces. If each such subspace is spanned by the rays it contains in every maximal cone, nearby stable polarizations exist precisely when the variety is not a nontrivial toric product. Applied to the existing anticanonical census in dimensions three through six, this criterion gives nearby stability in all 31 indecomposable strictly semistable cases, whose local chambers have one, two, or three independent boundaries. We determine locally free, pairwise nonisomorphic Jordan–Hölder factors both for these cases and for the family Xk,k−1.

  4. 04

    Small Dolbeault eigenvalues near stability walls

    From stability chambers to the equilibrium spectrum: every small Hermitian–Einstein eigenvalue is determined to uniform relative error by ranks and slope gaps, even at arbitrarily separated scales.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We determine the small spectrum of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphisms of Hermitian–Einstein bundles near intersections of slope-stability walls. On a compact Kähler manifold of dimension at least two, consider an extension of ⨁j=1kFj by F0, where the factors are pairwise nonisomorphic and stable of equal slope at the limiting Kähler class, and every extension component is nonzero. Write ri=rkFi and let ϵj be the Einstein constant of Fj minus that of the extension. In the chamber ϵj>0, exactly k eigenvalues tend to zero. They agree, to relative error O(∑jϵj), with the eigenvalues of diag(ϵ1,…,ϵk)+r0−1vv*, where vj=rjϵj. The estimate is uniform in all positive ratios of the slope gaps. Thus the ranks and slopes determine the leading spectrum even when the gaps vanish at arbitrarily different rates.

    For two factors we obtain λ1=δ−Dδ2+o(δ2), where δ>0 is the quotient Einstein constant minus the subbundle Einstein constant and D≥0 is given by the Green operator of the graded connection. After rescaling the extension and making unitary identifications with the graded limit, we determine the normalized first eigensection through order δ in every spatial Cm norm. Toric tangent bundles provide examples with arbitrarily many small eigenvalues whose orders of vanishing can be prescribed independently.

  5. 05

    Small Dolbeault eigenvalues along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow

    Small spectral modes along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow, described by evolving weighted graph Laplacians in specified bundle classes.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We study the small eigenvalues of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphisms of a semistable holomorphic bundle along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow, normalized as h−1∂τh=−2(Kh−κ) with Kh the mean curvature and κ its average. The bundles considered are simple bundles on a compact curve whose Jordan–Hölder factors are pairwise nonisomorphic, and extensions of one polystable bundle by another on compact Kähler manifolds, again with pairwise nonisomorphic stable factors. From every smooth initial metric, each small eigenvalue is asymptotic to an eigenvalue of a weighted graph Laplacian, whose conductances evolve by an explicit finite-dimensional flow; the relative error tends to zero. The graph records which stable factors are joined by nonzero extension classes. The asymptotics are governed by the weight grading of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich, and Pandit, the solution of a quadratic program on this graph. Suppose some Lagrange multiplier vector of this program is strictly positive on every active constraint. We then determine the decay exponent and the leading coefficient of every small eigenvalue, and the limits of the spectral subspaces. The eigenvalues of order τ−1 have coefficients independent of the initial metric, and 1/2 is always one of them. At each faster rate the coefficients depend on the initial metric, and their sum is unbounded over metrics on one fixed bundle. A key step shows that the bounded remainder in the asymptotics of the finite-dimensional flow converges. Examples include an eigenvalue decaying like τ−4/3 and, without the positivity condition, an eigenvalue of order (τlogτ)−1. We also treat families of harmonic extensions with fixed metrics, including repeated factors and recovery of the graded object, and we show that the constants in a slope inequality relating the lowest eigenvalue to the averaged trace-free mean curvature are sharp in every rank.

  6. 06

    Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra for bipartite two-step extensions

    The spectral mechanism: relative estimates for every small eigenvalue on connected bipartite supports, uniformly through arbitrarily separated positive scales.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We determine every small eigenvalue of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphisms at a Hermitian–Einstein metric near an intersection of slope-stability walls. The bundle is a fixed extension of one direct sum of stable bundles by another on a compact Kähler manifold, with pairwise nonisomorphic factors of equal slope at the reference form and a connected graph of nonzero extension components. In the stable chamber, the small spectrum is asymptotic to the nonzero spectrum of a weighted Laplacian on this graph. Its vertex masses are the factor ranks, and its edge weights balance the slope differences against the harmonic extension data. The relative error is of order the total edge weight, uniformly as positive stability margins vanish at arbitrarily different rates, and the remaining spectrum has a uniform gap. The proof uses curvature identities across graph cuts to control the nonlinear correction at each vanishing scale. As consequences, we obtain a relative approximation of the metric and a spanning-tree formula for the leading vanishing factor of the zeta-regularized determinant. For trees the leading spectrum is determined by ranks and slopes; cycles can retain dependence on the harmonic extension data.

  7. 07

    Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra with repeated stable factors

    Repeated stable factors: actual HE metric comparison and every small matrix mode, uniformly at arbitrarily separated positive scales.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove uniform relative asymptotics for all small positive Dolbeault eigenvalues on trace-free endomorphism-valued zero-forms for Hermitian–Einstein metrics on simple two-step extensions near an equal-slope Kähler polarization. The stable factors may occur with arbitrary fixed multiplicities, with distinct isomorphism types in the two layers. The finite comparison operator is the commutator Laplacian on the full complex multiplicity algebra, evaluated at an independently balanced representative of the extension. Every small eigenvalue has relative error bounded by a fixed multiple of the total balanced extension energy, and the remaining spectrum has a uniform positive lower bound. No lower bound on positive eigenvalue ratios or stability margins is imposed. The main estimate bounds the curvature residual against every multiplicity projection by its crossing energy; a degree argument then compares the actual metric with the balanced metric without inverting a degenerating finite Hessian. A fixed rank-five bundle on a product of four projective lines exhibits six small modes and arbitrarily separated positive scales.

  8. 08

    Nonsimplicial local stability chambers of toric tangent bundles

    Geometric realization: nonsimplicial tangent chambers, positive-spanning slope access without local freeness, and an explicit Calabi–Yau conifold transition.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct smooth projective toric varieties with nonsimplicial local slope-stability chambers for their tangent bundles. In every even dimension 2k+2≥4, a fibre product of two projective-line bundles over (ℙ1)2k admits an ample real class at which the tangent bundle is strictly semistable. Near this class, the semistable locus is analytically isomorphic to a pointed cone over Δ1×Δ2k−1, times a line, where Δj denotes the j-simplex. The torus-invariant saturated subsheaves of equal slope form the ideal lattice of the complete bipartite poset with two minimal and 2k maximal elements, and the stable line factors realize every centered slope direction. These varieties are weak Fano and not Fano. The fourfold has minimal dimension and Picard rank among toric varieties with such a chamber and nearby stable polarizations. We also prove a local normal form at any ample real class where the tangent bundle of a smooth projective toric variety is semistable. It retains precisely the slope directions realized by polarizations, requires no local-freeness assumption on equal-slope subsheaves, and localizes every nearby Harder–Narasimhan filtration to their equality lattice. If the fan rays in each equal-slope subspace positively span that subspace, every centered slope direction occurs. A semistable surface satisfies this condition although one equal-slope quotient is not locally free.

    Metric response and Hermitian Yang–Mills flow for two-step extensions

    Metrics and flows: intrinsic response, singular inverses and actual HYM comparisons, culminating in the integrated theorem on the same tangent family.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    For two-step extensions of pairwise nonisomorphic stable bundles on a compact Kähler manifold, we quantify the degeneration of Hermitian–Einstein metrics and Hermitian Yang–Mills flow near intersecting slope-stability walls. We assume equal slopes at the reference form and connected support for the extension class. When the centered slopes provide full local parameters, every singular value of the trace-free part of H−1dH, for the Hermitian–Einstein metric H, is relatively asymptotic to the reciprocal of a nonzero eigenvalue of a weighted extension-graph Laplacian. The comparison uses rank-weighted slope and normalized L2 norms and remains uniform for arbitrarily separated positive stability margins. We also construct a finite-dimensional invariant manifold of the flow and compare its solutions with flows from any smooth family of initial metrics. For each tolerance, one late time and then one parameter neighborhood give multiplicative metric comparison, up to a constant scalar, and relative accuracy for every small endomorphism Dolbeault eigenvalue for all subsequent times. The comparison applies in the stable, semistable and unstable regimes. In the two-factor case, we determine a uniform asymptotic profile for the smallest eigenvalue across the wall, including exponential decay on the unstable side. An application to explicit weak Fano toric tangent bundles connects nonsimplicial stability chambers, metric response and flow on the same bundles.

    Hermitian–Einstein metrics and small spectra for three-step extensions

    Three-step extensions: actual metrics, every small spectral scale and intrinsic polarization response.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove uniform relative asymptotics for every small Dolbeault eigenvalue on trace-free endomorphism-valued zero-forms in a class of three-step Hermitian–Einstein extensions, allowing arbitrary separation of positive spectral scales. The graded factors are distinct line bundles of equal slope at a reference polarization on a compact Kähler manifold. Adjacent extension classes vary in fixed cohomology subspaces; the remaining terms are quadratic coexact Hodge primitives of their compositions, with no independent first-to-third-layer parameters. Assume integrability and a connected extension graph. At nearby polarizations, suppose diagonal rescaling balances the squared norms of the adjacent classes against the slope differences, with sufficiently small sum of rescaled squared norms. The rescaled split metric then approximates the Hermitian–Einstein metric with the same determinant, with logarithmic error bounded by a uniform constant times that sum. Every small eigenvalue has the same relative accuracy with respect to the corresponding eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian weighted by these squared norms; the complementary spectrum has a uniform positive gap. The proof controls curvature traces by the energy crossing each vertex cut. A family on the product of three projective lines has a spectral scale undetected by its polarization parameters. At each fixed bundle in this family, both singular values of the intrinsic metric response to the two polarization parameters agree, with the same relative accuracy, with those of the differentiated finite balancing equation.

    Relative metric estimates near reducible Yang–Mills connections

    Relative surface gauge quotient-metric and small-spectrum estimates, uniform across changing stabilizers and arbitrarily separated scales.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We study the natural L2 metric near a central Yang–Mills connection on a closed surface. In the classical quadratic symplectic model, we prove a relative metric estimate with error quadratic in the harmonic deformation parameter, uniformly across all stabilizer types. We also compute the leading metric correction using the Green operators of the reference connection. The proof first eliminates the gauge directions with a fixed positive spectral gap and then minimizes over the remaining finite-dimensional orbit; it requires no lower bound on a positive orbit eigenvalue. The same argument compares every small eigenvalue of the gauge Laplacian with the infinitesimal stabilizer-action operator, including at changes of kernel dimension. As a curved finite-dimensional comparison, quaternionic projective and hyperbolic reductions admit exact orbit-metric formulas on their flat moment-map zero cone. A four-cycle is realized in both settings with independently vanishing weights. The resulting estimates distinguish uniform control of the quotient metric from estimates that depend on the smallest orbit scale.

    L² metrics on Hermitian–Yang–Mills moduli spaces near a stability wall

    Full local moduli near a wall: uniform relative harmonic L² metrics, chart transitions and intrinsic distance.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove a uniform relative comparison between the harmonic L2 metric on a local Hermitian–Yang–Mills moduli space and the quotient metric of its finite-dimensional harmonic deformation model. The result concerns a rank-four family on the product of three projective lines, near a split sum of four line bundles at a stability wall. The model retains the full analytic obstruction and describes a neighborhood of the corresponding Hermitian–Einstein classes. On its smooth complex pieces, the relative metric error is quadratic in the coefficient radius. Both the comparison constant and the allowed radius are independent of the distance to the wall, even when the infinitesimal orbit map and the derivative of the obstruction lose rank. The proof estimates the ambient deformation norm before taking the quotient, avoiding inversion of either degenerating map. We also identify the transition from this wall model to the harmonic model at a reducible rank-three-plus-line endpoint, with quantitative domains of validity. These comparisons extend to intrinsic lengths and their completions; in an explicit family of stable extensions they give a joint asymptotic for the intrinsic distance to the reducible bundle.

    Hermitian–Einstein metrics and moduli of three-step extensions with repeated factors

    Repeated factors in three steps: actual rank-five metrics, six matrix modes and local quotient geometry.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct Hermitian–Einstein metrics and obtain relative asymptotics for all six small eigenvalues of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphism-valued zero-forms in a rank-five deformation problem with a repeated line factor on the product of three projective lines. The estimates are uniform as positive spectral scales separate or vanish.     Metric wall crossing in rank-three K3 bundle moduli

    Actual harmonic metrics, collapse scales, complete rescaled limits and intrinsic distances in a local rank-three K3 moduli wall crossing.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We determine the local harmonic L2 metric near a stability wall in a complex twelve-dimensional moduli space of rank-three bundles on a projective K3 surface. The central bundle is the sum of two copies of one spherical line bundle and a second spherical line bundle, with five-dimensional cross-extension spaces; the central Kähler metric is Ricci-flat.
