Explore the questions, results and relationships across the series. Much of the programme begins with holomorphic vector bundles: families of complex vector spaces attached to a curved base. A Hermitian metric specifies lengths and angles within each fibre. The papers study how these metrics behave near stability walls, how small eigenvalues reveal weak directions, and how the geometry of the spaces of bundles changes.

The atlas follows these ideas through finite models, actual flows, wall crossing and global geometry. Paper 01Importance rank 10 / 28

Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra for bipartite two-step extensions

How can one predict every weak spectral direction as a stable bundle approaches several stability walls at once?

The result. For a fixed two-step extension with pairwise nonisomorphic stable factors on a compact Kähler manifold, every small positive endomorphism eigenvalue is approximated with relative accuracy by this finite graph spectrum in the stable chamber. The estimate allows arbitrarily different rates of approach to the walls, leaves a uniform gap above the small modes, and gives a spanning-tree formula for the leading vanishing determinant factor.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Replace the bundle's stable factors by vertices of a weighted graph, and its nonzero extension classes by edges joining those factors. The graph Laplacian measures how easily the factors can move relative to one another, while curvature identities across every graph cut control the error at each small scale.

Why it matters

This supplies the series' original mechanism for controlling very different small scales without losing the weakest ones.

What remains outside the theorem

The theorem concerns two-step extensions with connected extension graph and equal reference slopes. For a tree, ranks and slopes determine the leading spectrum; for a graph with cycles, the extension data can still matter.

Before reading

Some familiarity with vector bundles and eigenvalues helps; the finite comparison itself can be understood through weighted graph Laplacians.

Paper 02Importance rank 16 / 28

Nonsimplicial local stability chambers of toric tangent bundles

Can complicated stability chambers be realized by the tangent bundles of actual smooth projective varieties?

The result. In every even dimension at least four, the construction gives a smooth projective weak Fano variety with a nonsimplicial local semistability cone and nearby stable polarizations. A general local normal form also describes the slope directions that polarizations actually realize and locates all nearby Harder–Narasimhan filtrations in the equal-slope subsheaf lattice.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

For a toric variety, the symmetries turn the relevant subsheaf slope inequalities into a finite combinatorial problem. A family built from projective-line bundles realizes a complete bipartite pattern of equal-slope factors, producing chamber cones whose generating rays are not independent.

Why it matters

It shows that the chamber configurations motivating the analytic theory occur in genuine algebraic geometry.

What remains outside the theorem

The explicit higher-dimensional varieties are weak Fano, not Fano. The general normal form retains only the realizable slope directions; realizing every centered direction requires an additional positive-spanning condition.

Before reading

The main picture uses cones and slope inequalities; the construction requires basic toric geometry and the notion of bundle stability.

Paper 03Importance rank 13 / 28

Metric response and Hermitian Yang–Mills flow for two-step extensions

How sensitive is a canonical bundle metric to a change in stability, and how does its evolution change when a wall is crossed?

The result. For the specified two-step extensions, the metric-response singular values agree relatively with inverse graph eigenvalues when centered slopes provide full local parameters. The actual flow admits a uniform late-time comparison with the finite dynamics in stable, semistable and unstable regimes, and the two-factor case has an explicit profile through the wall.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

The small graph eigenvalues describe weak restoring directions, so their reciprocals govern the largest responses of the Hermitian–Einstein metric to a change in slope. An invariant finite-dimensional family of metrics then translates the same extension data into a reduced model of Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow.

Why it matters

It connects chamber combinatorics, spectral sensitivity and actual metric evolution on the same bundles.

What remains outside the theorem

The response theorem requires all centered slope directions to be realized locally. The flow comparison chooses a late time for a prescribed tolerance before shrinking the parameter neighborhood; it is not an unrestricted estimate for arbitrary bundles at all initial times.

Before reading

Read Paper 1 for the graph model; basic differential equations give the intuition for relaxation rates and sensitivity.

Paper 04Importance rank 19 / 28

Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra with repeated stable factors

What replaces the scalar graph spectrum when several copies of the same stable factor occur?

The result. For simple two-step extensions with arbitrary fixed multiplicities and distinct factor types in the two layers, every small positive eigenvalue has a uniform relative approximation by the independently balanced matrix model. No lower bound on ratios of positive eigenvalues is needed, and a rank-five example displays six small modes with arbitrarily separated scales.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Repeated factors can mix by matrices, so assigning a single scalar to each factor no longer captures every weak direction. The finite model becomes a commutator Laplacian on the full matrix algebra of multiplicities, and projection-wise energy estimates control its smallest eigenvalues.

Why it matters

It introduces the noncommuting matrix structure needed to study repeated factors and larger automorphism groups.

What remains outside the theorem

The setting remains two-step, with fixed multiplicities and different isomorphism types in the two layers. Paper 15 later treats the broader local deformation problem.

Before reading

Paper 1 explains the scalar case; matrix commutators and orthogonal projections explain the new feature.

Paper 05Importance rank 28 / 28

Hermitian–Einstein metrics and small spectra for three-step extensions

Does relative spectral control survive when extensions are composed through three layers?

The result. For the stated class of integrable three-step extensions of distinct line bundles, a sufficiently small balanced model approximates the Hermitian–Einstein metric and every small eigenvalue with uniform relative error. An example on a product of three projective lines has a spectral scale that the polarization parameters alone cannot detect, and its metric response is compared with the differentiated balancing equation.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

With three layers, adjacent extension classes interact through the equations expressing holomorphicity. The paper retains controlled quadratic correction terms for these compositions and uses graph-cut estimates to compare the corrected geometry with the finite balancing model.

Why it matters

It isolates the extra compatibility issue introduced by composable extensions and shows why stability parameters do not encode every weak scale.

What remains outside the theorem

The adjacent classes lie in fixed cohomology subspaces, and the other terms are prescribed quadratic coexact Hodge primitives, with no independent first-to-third-layer parameter. This is not a theorem for arbitrary three-step filtrations.

Before reading

The graph picture of Paper 1 is useful; the additional ingredient is that composing holomorphic extensions imposes compatibility equations.

Paper 06Importance rank 21 / 28

L² metrics on Hermitian–Yang–Mills moduli spaces near a stability wall

Can a finite deformation model recover distances in the actual space of canonical bundle metrics near a wall?

The result. Near a specified split rank-four bundle on a product of three projective lines, the actual harmonic metric and the full obstructed finite quotient metric differ by a relative error quadratic in the deformation size. The estimate remains uniform when orbit and obstruction maps lose rank, extends to intrinsic lengths and their completions, and describes the transition to a rank-three-plus-line endpoint.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

The harmonic L² metric measures the size of a bundle deformation by integrating its pointwise norm over the base. Instead of solving by inverting maps that become singular, the proof first compares deformation norms in the ambient space and only then takes the quotient by symmetries.

Why it matters

It turns finite spectral reduction into a method for studying the geometry of moduli spaces themselves.

What remains outside the theorem

The theorem concerns a particular local rank-four deformation problem. It retains the full analytic obstruction and does not replace it with an automatically smooth or unobstructed model.

Before reading

Think of a moduli space as the space of bundles up to isomorphism; some knowledge of tangent spaces and quotient metrics helps with the proof.

Hermitian–Einstein metrics and moduli of three-step extensions with repeated factors

Can one control canonical metrics, weak modes and moduli distances when a three-step deformation also has a repeated factor?

The result. In the specified rank-five deformation problem on a product of three projective lines, small simple integrable deformations satisfying the quadratic moment-map equation have Hermitian–Einstein metrics with quadratic logarithmic error and relative control of all six small modes. On smooth pieces of the full local quotient, the same construction compares harmonic and coefficient metrics and identifies the metric on the locus where one repeated line splits off.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

A repeated line factor introduces matrix-valued mixing, while composable extensions introduce nonlinear compatibility conditions. Harmonic orthogonality removes the cubic curvature terms, leaving an error that can be bounded separately in every multiplicity direction.

Why it matters

It tests several central mechanisms together in a concrete noncommuting example with changing stabilizer.

What remains outside the theorem

The result is tied to this rank-five family and its integrability and moment-map conditions. It does not yet supply a general theorem for all repeated-factor filtrations.

Before reading

Papers 4–6 supply the three ingredients: multiplicity matrices, three-layer compatibility and quotient metrics.

Paper 08Importance rank 23 / 28

Initial conditions and asymptotic phase for Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow

Which trajectory of a finite flow model is selected by an actual spatially varying initial metric?

The result. For a specified stable four-line family along a fixed polarization path, this selected phase is continuously differentiable in the initial metric at fixed bundle and polarization. A prescribed smooth determinant-normalized initial metric enters the local regime after a common restart time, and a separate three-layer comparison controls all future times with a small fixed relative error.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

An exact invariant graph separates the slowly evolving harmonic coefficients from transverse directions with a fixed spectral gap. The asymptotic phase assigns the initial metric a reduced trajectory whose relative metric error decays exponentially, even when the slow relaxation rates themselves become very small.

Why it matters

It explains how a finite dynamical description is determined by genuine initial data, rather than by a freely chosen model trajectory.

What remains outside the theorem

The local theorem requires bounded diagonal coordinates and small transverse displacement, and the phase is defined relative to the chosen invariant graph. The entry theorem concerns the specified nearby stable family, not arbitrary unstable initial data.

Before reading

Familiarity with attracting invariant manifolds and slow–fast dynamics is helpful; Paper 3 provides the bundle-flow context.

Paper 09Importance rank 22 / 28

Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow at and beyond a stability wall

Can a reduced flow remain valid at a stability wall and on the unstable side, where the metric itself becomes unbounded?

The result. For the specified acyclic wall family and triangular unstable family, sufficiently small transverse perturbations of the invariant graph admit a unique reduced trajectory with exponentially decreasing relative metric error. The estimates are uniform in the ratio of wall distance to initial energy, and at a fixed nearby unstable polarization the drift-subtracted diagonal limit varies locally invertibly with the three diagonal initial coordinates.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

A moving diagonal presentation absorbs the unbounded metric growth and leaves small harmonic coefficients to describe the remaining evolution. The proof keeps these coefficients confined for all time and tracks the transition from reciprocal decay at the wall to exponential decay beyond it.

Why it matters

It proves the confinement needed for a dynamical model to keep working after stability is lost.

What remains outside the theorem

The unstable theorem requires locally free Harder–Narasimhan line quotients and the stated triangular family. Singular graded quotients fall outside this description and motivate Paper 10.

Before reading

Paper 8 explains asymptotic phase; basic knowledge of stability filtrations helps distinguish wall behavior from unstable growth.

Paper 10Importance rank 05 / 28

Singular graded limits and metric growth under Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow

What fails in the usual weight-based description of metric growth when a graded quotient has point singularities?

The result. For rank-two bundles on compact Kähler surfaces with an equal-slope line subsheaf and a singular torsion-free quotient, the squared L² norm of the inclusion decays faster than the inverse square root of flow time for every smooth initial metric with fixed Hermitian–Einstein determinant. Both a bounded relative metric remainder and a bounded ordinary matrix-logarithm remainder fail to realize the predicted weight model, including for repeated line factors and nonreduced singularities.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

The algebraic weight filtration still predicts two weights, but a residue concentrated at the singular set contributes an additional analytic obstruction. A global normalization and an exact dissipation identity show that this obstruction remains visible in the metric even on regions away from the singular points.

Why it matters

It identifies a concrete obstruction that any general theory of singular HYM asymptotics must address.

What remains outside the theorem

The sharp replacement time law is not established. The stable-side curvature-concentration calculation belongs to a separate toric example and should not be read as a general bubbling theorem for the flow.

Before reading

The key distinction is between a vector bundle and a torsion-free sheaf that fails to be a bundle at finitely many points; familiarity with filtrations and HYM flow helps beyond that picture.

Paper 11Importance rank 12 / 28

Mass increase and acyclic cancellation for phase flows on quiver complexes

Does categorical phase flow always decrease mass, and can parts of a complex with zero cohomology be removed dynamically?

The result. For the stated two-term parallel-arrow quiver complexes, a sufficiently large least acyclic singular value yields exponential comparison with instantaneous cohomology; if the source differential is invertible, cancellation holds from every positive metric without that large-scale assumption. Additional results prove cohomology relaxation under a finite energy-infimum condition and distinguish C¹ mass convergence from the sharper, but limited, regularity of an explicit family.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

An explicit noncommuting example shows that trace-norm mass can increase under the prescribed phase flow. A separate comparison isolates the acyclic directions—those that contribute no cohomology—and controls their cancellation by the singular values of the differential.

Why it matters

It answers the proposed mass-monotonicity question negatively while establishing a quantitative form of dynamical cancellation.

What remains outside the theorem

The cancellation results concern specified two-term quiver complexes, and the hypotheses differ between the surjective and invertible differential cases. The cohomology-relaxation theorem uses particular charges and equal surviving dimensions.

Before reading

Basic chain complexes, cohomology and matrix singular values are enough to understand the cancellation question; categorical stability provides the broader motivation.

Paper 12Importance rank 09 / 28

Harmonic paths and honeycombs

Can arbitrary boundary norm data be joined by a harmonic path with a finite honeycomb description?

The result. For the identity representation of the A₂ quiver over the real-exponent Novikov field, every triple of diagonalizable boundary norms in every finite rank admits such a path, including repeated spectra and collapsed edges. With symmetric charges and a fixed radial area form, the path is unique among finite presentations whose one-sided motions agree to first order at every join.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

The construction minimizes a renormalized convex action and proves that the resulting norm motion can be described using finitely many linear coordinate pieces, called apartments. These pieces form a honeycomb whose edges carry actual semistable algebraic data and whose vertices satisfy compatible filtration conditions.

Why it matters

It establishes existence in a concrete honeycomb specialization of the harmonic-path conjecture and makes the finite algebraic structure part of the theorem.

What remains outside the theorem

This is a result for the stated A₂ representation, field, charges and area form, not the entire harmonic-path conjecture. Uniqueness requires the explicit first-order matching condition in addition to the edgewise definition.

Before reading

Convex minimization and piecewise-linear geometry give the basic picture; non-Archimedean norms and quiver representations are needed for the precise statement.

Paper 13Importance rank 20 / 28

Metric wall crossing in rank-three K3 bundle moduli

What does a concrete K3 bundle wall crossing look like when distances, not just algebraic spaces, are followed through the transition?

The result. In the specified complex twelve-dimensional rank-three K3 moduli problem, the two chamber metrics are uniformly approximated by the standard metrics on T*Gr(2,5) and T*Gr(3,5), with error quadratic in deformation size. This gives the square-root collapse scale of the exceptional fibres, complete rescaled metric limits, distances to contracted loci and matching with the wall metric.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Near the chosen reducible bundle, the deformation equations have finite hyperkähler quotient models built from cotangent bundles of Grassmannians. A correction within complex symmetry orbits compares these model lengths with actual harmonic lengths without losing control at reducible strata.

Why it matters

It makes an algebraic wall crossing quantitatively geometric in a substantial explicit example.

What remains outside the theorem

The central bundle has two copies of one spherical line factor and a second line factor, with five-dimensional cross-extension spaces, on a fixed Ricci-flat K3 background. The result is local near this bundle rather than a global metric comparison for arbitrary K3 moduli spaces.

Before reading

A Grassmannian parametrizes linear subspaces; some familiarity with moduli spaces and metric rescaling makes the geometric transition easier to read.

Paper 14Importance rank 07 / 28

Quantitative metric wall crossing for K3 bundle moduli

Can the metric description of K3 wall crossing remain uniform when several walls and singular strata are approached at unrelated rates?

The result. Near a locally free polystable K3 bundle satisfying the stated stability hypotheses, the actual harmonic metric is uniformly comparable to a flat quiver quotient metric even through changing stabilizers and arbitrarily separated positive scales. Under a simple-representation hypothesis for the complex zero moment fibre, complete quotient limits and finite successive rescalings exist when the required parameters and balanced base representatives converge at each stage.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

A quiver records extension directions between the stable factors, while its matrix symmetries retain the bundle's full automorphism group. Comparing harmonic lengths before minimizing over complementary gauge directions avoids dividing by quantities that vanish when the stabilizer changes.

Why it matters

It provides the reusable K3 metric framework behind later compact contractions and their local models.

What remains outside the theorem

The factors must satisfy the specified stability conditions for an ample polarization and a central Ricci-flat background, which need not have the same class. Complete quotient continuity uses the additional simple-representation hypothesis; iterated metric limits are conditional on convergence at every stage.

Before reading

Paper 13 gives a concrete first example; quivers, symmetry quotients and pointed metric limits supply the general language.

Paper 15Importance rank 01 / 28

Quantitative local models for Hermitian–Einstein metrics

Can a finite-dimensional model describe canonical bundle metrics and every direction in which they become weak near a stability wall?

The result. Near any fixed locally free polystable bundle on a compact Kähler manifold, the model reconstructs every sufficiently nearby Hermitian–Einstein solution and compares both its small spectrum and the harmonic metric on smooth moduli pieces. The estimates remain relative when positive eigenvalues have very different sizes or the symmetry group changes; canonical harmonic coordinates give quadratic error.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Separate the deformation into a finite set of harmonic directions and an elliptic complement, where a uniform estimate is available. Keep the full nonlinear equations that decide which deformations exist, then use the bundle's automorphisms to build a finite quadratic form for its small eigenvalues.

Why it matters

This provides a common local foundation for the series' spectral, metric and wall-crossing results without requiring the deformation equations to be quadratic.

What remains outside the theorem

The background compact manifold and reference bundle are fixed. Uniform control while the underlying manifold itself degenerates requires additional analysis.

Before reading

Start with holomorphic bundles, slope stability and the interpretation of Laplacian eigenvalues as weak deformation directions.

Paper 16Importance rank 04 / 28

Uniform asymptotic phase for Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow near a polystable bundle

Can the full metric flow be matched to one finite-dimensional trajectory even when some relaxation rates approach zero?

The result. Near a fixed locally free polystable bundle on a compact Kähler manifold, confined harmonic coefficients and small initial normal displacement yield exponential relative-metric tracking in a fixed Hölder norm. The phase is continuously differentiable in the initial metric, with estimates independent of the smallest positive finite rates, their ratios and changes of stabilizer.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Construct an invariant finite-dimensional family inside the space of all metrics, so its trajectories are exact solutions after reconstruction. An asymptotic phase selects which trajectory matches a given initial metric, while the uniformly controlled elliptic directions absorb the remaining error.

Why it matters

It gives a dynamical foundation that remains useful precisely where ordinary estimates based on a positive spectral gap become weak.

What remains outside the theorem

The theorem assumes that the finite coefficients stay in the prescribed neighborhood. It does not prove that arbitrary initial metrics enter or remain in that neighborhood.

Before reading

Read Paper 15 for the local reduction, then recall the distinction between slow finite modes and the uniformly decaying elliptic complement.

Paper 17Importance rank 15 / 28

Exceptional cycles and harmonic metrics in K3 bundle moduli

When exceptional cycles collapse in a compact moduli space, can their integral topology and their actual metric size be described together?

The result. For the specified compact fourfold family, the paper identifies primitive classes, their intersection matrix and a modulo-eight attachment condition, together with exact cycle areas and volumes. It proves ambient diameter estimates, complete cotangent-projective-plane limits at separated scales, and convergence of the chamber metrics to the common contraction.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Follow the same curve and surface classes through a compact K3 bundle-moduli contraction, including two interacting projective planes. Combine their integral intersection and boundary data with the harmonic metric, which measures bundle deformations using their least-energy representatives.

Why it matters

The topological cycles and the metric objects are explicitly the same, making the connection between wall crossing and geometric collapse concrete.

What remains outside the theorem

These are specified compact K3 constructions without bubbling, not a reconstruction theorem for every singular moduli space. Successive rescalings retain convergence conditions on normalized parameters and basepoints.

Before reading

A picture of a collapsing exceptional curve or plane is enough to start; integral cohomology explains the attachment conditions.

Paper 18Importance rank 11 / 28

Integral reconstruction and quantitative metric degeneration of K3 bundle moduli

How can local models be assembled into a statement about the metric and topology of an entire compact moduli space?

The result. For compact K3 bundle-moduli contractions without bubbling and with the required convergent quiver charts, the harmonic metric completes to the central space and the chamber spaces converge to it. A compact eightfold example realizes a Grassmannian exceptional fibre with explicit primitive classes, a modulo-six attachment, exact periods and complete rescaled metric limits.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Compare distances in finite quotient models across overlapping neighborhoods, then control how paths cross the singular strata of the compact space. For integral topology, include the complement and all overlap maps, rather than trying to infer global information from the exceptional fibres alone.

Why it matters

It turns the local theory into a global metric statement and identifies the additional information needed for integral reconstruction.

What remains outside the theorem

The chart hypotheses include simple representations in the complex moment-zero fibres. Additive cohomology reconstruction needs geometric overlap and monodromy data; local fibre rings alone do not suffice, and no universal power-law convergence rate is asserted.

Before reading

Read Paper 17 for a compact example, and think of a quiver quotient as a finite-dimensional local model for bundle deformations.

What happens to integral cohomology when one crosses every wall in a concrete compact family?

Does the natural metric obtained from gauge theory agree with the canonical Ricci-flat metric on a singular compact moduli space?

The result. For the specified twice-primitive K3 moduli spaces of Picard rank one, with a rank bound ensuring local freeness, the two metrics agree and the regular-locus completion is the original compact space. Light vectors and scalar interactions near heterotic K3 gauge enhancement

How do very light gauge particles affect scalar interactions as a heterotic K3 compactification approaches enhanced symmetry?

The result. For the stated smooth SO(32) compactification and rank-six bundle family, the complete leading Yang–Mills four-scalar tree amplitude approaches an explicit light-vector exchange formula with quadratic absolute error. The estimate remains uniform as the two deformation scales separate, below the heavier Higgs thresholds and away from light poles, and also determines the leading curvature growth on a selected plane in bundle moduli.

Method, significance and limits

Paper 22Importance rank 02 / 28

Integral topology and small eigenvalues in Calabi–Yau conifold transitions

Can global topology predict the leading small eigenvalues of an actual degenerating Ricci-flat Calabi–Yau metric?

The result. For a polarized conifold transition with a simply connected projective small resolution and a fixed smoothing ray that smooths every node to first order, the small positive two-form eigenvalues decay inversely with the logarithm of the smoothing parameter. Their number is the loss of divisor directions, their coefficients are computed from periods and global intersections, and explicit examples also determine integral vanishing cycles and monodromy.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Measure divisor classes by their periods on the exceptional curves and by their global harmonic norm after removing cohomology that survives smoothing. The relative eigenvalues of these two finite quadratic forms determine which two-form modes become small and their leading rates.

Why it matters

It links global algebraic and integral topology to the spectrum of genuine Ricci-flat smoothings with explicit leading coefficients.

What remains outside the theorem

The smoothing is fixed and approached along a fixed ray; arbitrary independent node scales are not covered. Recovering the integral information uses geometric markings and global data, not just the list of eigenvalues.

Before reading

Start with the picture of replacing a conifold node by an exceptional curve or a vanishing sphere, then recall Hodge theory's relation between forms and cohomology.

Small eigenvalues on degenerating curves and the intersection form

What finite graph describes the slowest scalar modes when a smooth curve breaks into several components?

The result. For a reduced compact curve degeneration with smooth total space and induced metrics that are Hermitian near the central singularities, the number of positive scalar eigenvalues tending to zero is one less than the number of components. After multiplication by the logarithmic degeneration scale, these eigenvalues converge to those of the negative intersection matrix relative to the component areas; the remaining positive spectrum stays uniformly separated from zero.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Assign one variable to each component, its area as a mass, and its intersection numbers as coupling weights. Logarithms of local component equations give nearly constant test functions whose energies can be calculated from winding numbers around the singularities.

Why it matters

It gives a direct geometric recipe for the complete small scalar spectrum, including reduced central fibres with singularities beyond ordinary nodes.

What remains outside the theorem

The central fibre must be reduced and the total space smooth, with the stated metric condition. This is a scalar spectral theorem, not a nonlinear bundle-flow result.

Before reading

Think of masses connected by weighted springs, or a weighted graph Laplacian; intersection numbers supply the graph weights.

Effective Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow on nodal degenerations of curves

Can nonlinear Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow on a degenerating curve be replaced by a small system of interacting component metrics?

The result. On trivial determinant-one bundles over the stated compact nodal curve families, the actual flow follows this finite gradient flow on bounded logarithmically rescaled time intervals. Uniformly bounded spatial initial metrics with the prescribed component limits yield global integrated tracking, with pointwise tracking away from nodes after an initial transient; a rank-two example has genuinely noncommuting component metrics.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Represent the metric on each limiting component by a positive Hermitian matrix, with component area as its mass. Exact stationary metrics along the thin necks couple these matrices through an energy built from squared distances in their symmetric space.

Why it matters

It establishes the basic nonlinear approximation on a degenerating base that the later gluing and all-time papers extend.

What remains outside the theorem

The bundle is trivial, each node joins distinct smooth components, and the nonlinear comparison covers bounded rescaled intervals. General gluing and all-time control require the later results.

The main idea

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Take a free quotient of a K3 surface times a degenerating elliptic curve, carrying a rank-four bundle whose weak modes survive the quotient constraints. The resulting finite energy has endpoint constraints whose relative angle records transport around the elliptic cycle.

Why it matters

It supplies a compact higher-dimensional realization with nonzero bundle curvature and initial data beyond the product ansatz.

What remains outside the theorem

These are type-K quotient threefolds, not simply connected full-SU(3) examples, and the prescribed metrics need not be Ricci-flat. The five-eigenvalue inverse recovers projective cycle transport within the known family, not arbitrary threefold or bundle topology.

Can the finite matrix model follow the actual flow for every time, even when its slowest relaxation rate is much smaller than the others?

The result. For uniformly bounded actual initial metrics and small finite initial data, the bound is the initial integrated discrepancy plus a term vanishing as the parameters tend independently to zero; an explicit stable family has relaxation times of orders h⁻¹, h⁻² and h⁻⁴, all covered by the nonlinear theorem.

Method, significance and limits

The main idea

Use the zero angular mean of the leading component-frame error to write it as a global divergence. Combining the resulting energy estimate with time-integrated bounds for the finite flow avoids dividing the error by the weakest decay rate.

Why it matters

It removes the weakest-rate restriction from the nonlinear comparison on this family and reaches every later relaxation scale.

What remains outside the theorem

The nonlinear result keeps the holonomy generators and node identifications fixed and requires a small finite basin. It controls absolute integrated error, not relative error at the weakest mode, and does not establish nonlinear uniformity under varying constraints.

03 / A little vocabulary

Six ideas that connect the papers.

Stability walls

Stability compares the slope—degree divided by rank—of a bundle with its subsheaves. A wall is a parameter value where slopes become equal, so the type of canonical solution can change.

Canonical metrics and flow

A Hermitian–Einstein metric solves the bundle’s canonical curvature equation. Hermitian–Yang–Mills (HYM) metric flow evolves a metric towards that equation; approaching a wall can introduce very slow directions.

Small eigenvalues

These identify directions with weak restoring force. Relative estimates track even the weakest mode when several rates approach zero at very different speeds.

Moduli spaces

A moduli space parametrizes geometric objects up to isomorphism. Its harmonic L² metric measures the size of a deformation by integrating over the underlying manifold.

Finite models and symmetries

Graphs and quivers encode component or extension data. Quotienting by their symmetries gives finite models for particular infinite-dimensional metric and flow problems.

Degenerating bases

The underlying curve or threefold can itself develop singularities. Thin necks and collapsing cycles create additional small modes, so a theory with a fixed base needs further estimates.
