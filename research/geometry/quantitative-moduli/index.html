Research / Geometry & moduli / Stability & Degeneration

How can degeneration reveal the geometry and topology of moduli spaces?

From singular limits to nearby geometry

We use degenerations of holomorphic vector bundles to understand canonical metrics on individual bundles and natural metrics on their moduli spaces. Finite deformation and symmetry data describe actual solutions near a stability wall. In specified compact K3 families without bubbling, exceptional cycles and integral attachments are linked to harmonic metric collapse and global convergence. The marked six-chamber rank-three family now has explicit complement rings, boundary restrictions and wall actions; reconstruction in general retains overlap and monodromy data. For a specified twice-primitive K3 family with all slope-graded factors locally free, the harmonic metric equals the canonical singular Ricci-flat metric in an ample determinant class; holomorphic tangent-cone charts have quadratic relative error within each fixed background. The broader aim extends this connection to singular compactifications and a quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin programme for geometric flows.

The mathematical setting

Stability of bundles. Geometry of their moduli.

The objects are holomorphic vector bundles on compact Kähler manifolds: families of complex vector spaces varying holomorphically over a fixed base. Their degenerations give access to the geometry of nearby bundles and to the spaces that parametrize them.

Stability and its walls

A holomorphic vector bundle is slope-stable when every coherent subsheaf of smaller positive rank has smaller degree per rank. Degree depends on the chosen Kähler class, or polarization. As that class varies, equality of slopes can mark a stability wall. Stable bundles admit canonical Hermitian–Einstein metrics. So do polystable bundles: direct sums of stable bundles of the same slope.

The geometry of moduli

For a fixed base and fixed topological data, these moduli spaces parametrize polystable holomorphic bundles up to isomorphism. On their smooth pieces, the harmonic L² metric measures infinitesimal changes of a bundle by integrating pointwise inner products over the base. Two geometries therefore enter the problem: a Hermitian metric on each bundle, and a metric on the space of bundles. Stability alone need not make the moduli space smooth.

The local dynamical theorem

Quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin.

The classical Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence relates polystability to the existence of Hermitian–Einstein metrics. The quantitative dynamical programme asks how those metrics respond as stability changes, how Hermitian Yang–Mills flow approaches them, and what the flow does at the wall and beyond it.

The uniform asymptotic-phase theorem now selects an exact finite trajectory from the initial metric near any fixed locally free polystable bundle, retaining arbitrary multiplicities and the full obstruction. Under coefficient confinement and small initial normal displacement, relative metric error decays exponentially, uniformly through weak spectral scales and stabilizer changes. The dynamical results below distinguish this theorem from the remaining questions of general entry, confinement and the algebraic asymptotics of the corrected finite flow.

The singular-sheaf results identify a boundary of the smooth theory. The global topological ambition concerns moduli spaces; consequences for the topology of the base manifold require an additional argument.

The general local theorem

Recover the metric, the weak spectrum, and the geometry of moduli.

Quantitative local models for Hermitian–Einstein metrics starts with any fixed locally free polystable bundle on a compact Kähler manifold. One finite-dimensional construction controls three parts of the stationary problem, even as stabilizers change and positive spectral scales separate arbitrarily.

Local near a fixed background · stationary metrics · locally free bundles

I

Reconstruct

A balanced point satisfying the full obstruction equations determines an actual Hermitian–Einstein metric. Every sufficiently nearby solution is represented, with the prescribed determinant normalization.

II

Resolve

The automorphism action on harmonic deformations determines every small endomorphism Dolbeault eigenvalue to relative accuracy, including its exact changing kernel. The remaining spectrum has a uniform positive gap.

III

Measure

The finite quotient is homeomorphic to an actual moduli neighbourhood. On genuine smooth pieces, the identification is smooth and the actual harmonic L² metric is relatively close to the flat quotient metric restricted to the full obstruction locus.

The weakest directions remain visible.

The comparison constants are independent of the positive finite eigenvalues, their ratios and the stabilizer type. Small absolute error would not give this control: it could overwhelm the weakest mode. The proof estimates each weak direction relative to its own energy.

The finite model, coordinates and scope

Write the central bundle as a direct sum of stable factors with arbitrary fixed multiplicities. Harmonic deformations form the Ext-quiver representation. The full convergent Kuranishi map κ cuts out the integrable locus; the compact automorphism group acts on it, and the flat moment equation imposes balance. No formality assumption or quadratic truncation of κ is made.

In fixed canonical harmonic coordinates, the relative error is O(|t| + r²), where t measures polarization variation and r is the coefficient norm. At fixed polarization it is O(r²). Moving harmonic backgrounds preserve quadratic accuracy under nearby polarization changes. An arbitrary nonlinear change of coordinates need not preserve that order.

The constants and neighborhood depend on the fixed background and its positive complementary elliptic gaps. The metric statement uses genuine tangents to smooth pieces of the reduced analytic quotient. It does not assert a tensor on arbitrary Zariski tangents, uniform radii for every stratum chart, or uniformity through collapse of the background manifold.

Geometric consequences · K3 surfaces

What does the wall reveal about nearby chamber geometry?

A wall model can describe the actual geometry of nearby stable bundles and the moduli spaces they occupy. The K3 papers identify local chamber neighbourhoods and their exceptional loci, then determine lengths, contraction scales and rescaled limits for their harmonic L² metrics. The distance and limit statements require additional path-localization and coverage arguments.

A Grassmannian wall crossing

The rank-three theorem identifies local chamber neighbourhoods with regions in T*Gr(2,5) and T*Gr(3,5), and compares their actual metrics with the hyperkähler quotient metrics. The exceptional fibres include projective planes and the deepest Grassmannian fibre. The theorem determines their collapse, complete pointed Gromov–Hausdorff limits after rescaling, matching to the wall metric and intrinsic distances to contracted loci.

The central bundle has two copies of one spherical line bundle and a second spherical line bundle, with five-dimensional cross-extension spaces on a projective K3 surface with a central Ricci-flat Kähler metric.

When several walls meet

The general K3 comparison is realized by a rank-five bundle with three spherical factors, multiplicities (2,2,1) and two independent polarization parameters. Selected transverse contractions have models T*P⁹, T*P¹¹ and T*P¹⁷, with square-root contraction profiles and matching to the surrounding wall metric.

Complete limits require more than local metric comparison.

For doubled quivers whose complex zero fibre admits a module-simple representation, meaning one with no nonzero proper subrepresentation, the complete stratified quotients vary continuously in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff sense as real central parameters converge. Complete limits of the actual rescaled metrics, including finite successive limits, retain convergence of rescaled parameters and balanced normal base representatives at each stage.

The K3 hypotheses and the order of the error

The general K3 theorem assumes locally free factors that are slope-stable with a common reduced Hilbert polynomial for an integral ample class, and slope-stable of a common slope for a central Ricci-flat class, which may differ. Its formality coordinates give linear spatial error; a verified central sign symmetry improves it to quadratic. The rank-three example has the quadratic estimate, whereas the rank-five triangle has the linear estimate.

The general local theorem’s quadratic estimate is in canonical harmonic coordinates. It does not automatically improve the error after formality straightening, or supply homogeneous rescaled limits for an arbitrary obstructed germ. These results are local. The compact fourfold construction establishes convergent common-slope K3 symplectic coordinates with cubic coordinate error; it and the integral reconstruction theorem treat compact contractions without bubbling. Positive-charge limits remain open.

Compact K3 moduli · Integral topology and harmonic metrics

Integral cycles and metric collapse on the same spaces.

The compact K3 results carry the local models into entire bundle-moduli spaces. They determine how exceptional cycles enter integral cohomology and how the actual harmonic L² metric collapses those same cycles.

Compact K3 bundle moduli · no bubbling · complete convergent quiver charts

Interacting planes in a fourfold

Exceptional cycles and harmonic metrics in K3 bundle moduli constructs a compact two-parameter family with six chambers. In a specified chamber, a moving projective line specializes with multiplicity one to the sum of lines in two exceptional planes. The planes have primitive integral classes and intersection matrix with diagonal entries 3 and off-diagonal entries 1.

An explicit congruence modulo eight describes the degree-four attachment to the marked complement; an isolated-plane example gives the corresponding congruence modulo three. Exact harmonic areas and volumes and ambient diameter bounds hold uniformly as the two positive parameters vanish at unrelated rates. Separated-scale rescalings give complete Calabi limits; finite successive limits retain convergence hypotheses on normalized levels and normal basepoints. The entire chamber spaces converge in compact Gromov–Hausdorff distance to the central harmonic completion.

Two Schubert directions in an eightfold

Integral reconstruction and quantitative metric degeneration of K3 bundle moduli constructs a compact rank-seven bundle-moduli eightfold. Moving projective lines specialize to Schubert lines in a deepest Gr(2,4) fibre. Both middle Schubert classes extend integrally, the fibre is primitive, and its self-intersection is six.

Universal-index restrictions calculate its attachment modulo six. The same classes have exact harmonic periods and volumes, ambient diameter asymptotics and complete cotangent-Grassmannian rescaled limits. Both Schubert directions are needed; divisor information alone does not determine the attachment data.

A global comparison with an explicit modulus

For the stated compact K3 contraction class, the global comparison theorem identifies the completion of the principal-stratum harmonic metric with the central projective moduli space. The hypotheses exclude both non-locally-free sheaves and positive lost charge, and require full convergent quiver charts whose complex moment-zero fibres contain module-simple representations: representations with no nonzero proper subrepresentation. Finite quotient distances give a variational distortion bound for the graph of the actual contraction. That bound tends to zero through arbitrary adjacent chambers, without assuming a universal power-law rate.

Explicit complements and wall actions in all six chambers

Integral attachments and wall correspondences for K3 bundle moduli calculates the missing global data for the marked rank-three family. An explicit transport to the Hilbert square determines integral coordinates for the exceptional surfaces and determinant-normalized universal indices. In one chamber the complement ring is the quotient by the classes of the two planes, and the degree-four boundary restriction is an explicit functional modulo eight.

The formulas cover all six exceptional configurations. Four adjacent crossings are elementary Mukai flops with corrected correspondences; two are isomorphisms over divisorial targets whose opposite exceptional-divisor signs produce a reflection. Their cohomological compositions act on the same integral classes whose harmonic periods and volumes are known from the established metric degeneration.

Canonical metrics without a resolution or a smoothing

Canonical metrics on singular moduli of K3 bundles treats twice-primitive Mukai vectors on a K3 surface of Picard rank one. A rank bound ensures that every semistable sheaf and every slope Jordan–Hölder factor is locally free. The resulting compact moduli spaces have positive-dimensional singular strata and admit neither a symplectic resolution nor a global smoothing.

The harmonic L² metric is the canonical singular Ricci-flat metric in a positive multiple of an ample determinant class. Exact quadraticity in harmonic Kuranishi coordinates gives holomorphic Ext-quiver charts with relative O(r²) metric error. The estimate is uniform through changes of stabilizer within each fixed background chart. The regular-locus completion recovers the original space, and its unique tangent cones are the flat hyperkähler quotients of the extension spaces.

For the Mukai vector (5,H,0) on a quartic K3, the moduli space has complex dimension 18 and singular strata of dimensions 12 and 6. No singular point is locally isometric to its tangent cone. This does not assert that the exponent is optimal or that higher derivatives satisfy uniform estimates across lower strata; the family excludes bubbling.

What general reconstruction still takes as input

Integral cochains on all geometric overlaps recover additive cohomology. This uses the complete overlap and monodromy information. The marked rank-three calculation determines its complement rings and universal-index restrictions from the actual K3 geometry; a general recovery theorem from local degeneration data alone is still missing. A mapping-torus example shows why local fibre rings and divisor classes do not suffice. A general cup-product reconstruction from degeneration, positive-charge compactifications and reconstruction of the base manifold remain beyond these results.

A test beyond quadratic models · Calabi–Yau threefolds

The first obstruction can appear at order three.

Recovering nearby geometry requires the full deformation equations. The local-model construction gives a stable rank-two bundle A on a smooth bicubic Calabi–Yau threefold with analytic deformation ring ℂ{a,b}/(a²b, ab²). Its quadratic obstruction vanishes. The full equations still exclude mixed directions.

The whole deformation problem

For three copies, A ⊗ ℂ³, the theorem controls all nearby polystable deformations in an eighteen-dimensional harmonic space. It retains the full matrix-valued obstruction and all eight small trace-free endomorphism modes, including zeros. The matrix equations are not obtained by simply substituting noncommuting matrices into the scalar cubic relations.

A separate test of weak scales

An explicit diagonal family along a smooth reduced branch has two arbitrarily weak positive modes and four on a larger scale, together with two exact trace-free zeros. Collisions change the kernel dimension from two to four to eight. This tests relative spectral uniformity; it does not assign a spectral exponent to the cubic or nilpotent structure.

A boundary revealed by singular graded sheaves

A singular point changes the metric away from it.

Changing stabilizers can make a moduli space singular even when the graded bundle is locally free. A graded sheaf that is itself singular presents a further problem. For rank-two bundles on compact Kähler surfaces with a saturated equal-slope line subsheaf and singular torsion-free quotient, this second kind of singularity changes the long-time metric behavior even on regions kept away from the singular set.

Singular graded limits and metric growth proves a theorem for rank-two bundles on connected compact Kähler surfaces with a saturated equal-slope line subsheaf and a singular torsion-free quotient. The nonempty finite singular scheme may be nonreduced, and the two line factors may coincide. A projective K3 example realizes both features; a toric tangent bundle provides a second example. What the obstruction leaves open

The established asymptotic theorem for locally free graded objects is unaffected. The obstruction concerns these two extensions of flow asymptotics; it is not a general impossibility theorem for stationary local reductions at singular sheaves. The theorem does not determine a new power-law exponent or the exact slower correction, and it does not rule out a finite description enriched by singular data. Determining the replacement asymptotic law is a central next problem.

How the comparisons work

Why do the estimates survive degeneracy?

The common strategy is to retain every weak direction in a finite-dimensional problem and solve the remaining equation on a complement with a uniform positive gap. The finite problem then carries the degenerating scales. Passing from this reduction to an actual spectrum, a quotient metric or a flow requires a separate comparison in each setting.

01

Keep the full equations

Canonical harmonic coordinates retain the convergent obstruction. Quadratic reconstruction and gauge terms are orthogonal to harmonic one-forms, giving quadratic ambient accuracy.

02

Balance by relative energy

The finite correction is controlled by the energy crossing each multiplicity projection. Its bound survives the loss of a positive orbit gap. This balancing theorem underlies the K3 metric wall-crossing theorem.

03

Compare each weak direction

Exact equivariance ties the elliptic coupling to the commutator energy of the same direction. The spectral reduction therefore gives relative error even for the weakest mode.

04

Minimize in two stages

Eliminate the uniformly coercive infinite-dimensional gauge directions first. Then minimize over finite orbit directions. Derivatives of the balancing gauge are vertical and disappear.

The general geometric verification is in Quantitative local models for Hermitian–Einstein metrics. Its mechanisms build on repeated-factor spectra, complementary quotient minimization and finite balancing. Qualitative local deformation and correspondence results supply further inputs; the additional quantitative issue is control relative to each degenerating direction.

Geometric realizability

Which degenerations can actually occur?

A finite model becomes a geometric example only when a bundle realizes its factors, extension classes and accessible slope directions. The chamber papers establish this part of the programme.

The toric tangent stability census and the blowup-and-bundle paper give explicit families and exact classifications. Local stability chambers then varies the polarization: it constructs an all-dimensional family with arbitrarily many independent chamber boundaries and determines nearby stable loci for the indecomposable strictly semistable examples in the earlier census.

Nonsimplicial tangent chambers goes beyond independent inequalities. It constructs weak-Fano tangent bundles whose local chambers have genuinely nonsimplicial geometry, with every centered slope direction accessible. Its general local normal form retains the slope directions realized by polarizations and requires no local-freeness assumption on the equal-slope subsheaves. A toric surface in the same paper supplies the singular graded object studied later.

The bridge to analysis

The two-step metric-response and flow paper joins the chamber construction to spectral and nonlinear estimates on the same weak-Fano tangent bundles. The general local stationary comparison and confined dynamical reduction now apply near fixed locally free polystable backgrounds with arbitrary multiplicities. Classifying realizable chamber structures, proving entry and confinement for general filtrations, and determining the asymptotics of the corrected finite flow remain wider questions.

Stationary metrics and spectra

What determines every weak spectral scale?

The general commutator form specializes to a weighted graph Laplacian in the two-step setting. These earlier constructions make the finite data explicit and reveal what slopes alone can determine.

Small eigenvalues near stability walls begins with a stable subbundle extended by several stable quotient factors. The bipartite two-step theorem treats extensions between two sums of distinct stable factors. At equal limiting slopes and with connected extension support, a weighted graph Laplacian determines the small endomorphism spectrum with uniform relative error. Ranks give the vertex masses; balancing the harmonic extension data against the slope gaps gives the edge weights.

This description also identifies what the chamber forgets. On a tree, ranks and slopes determine the leading spectrum. On a cycle, harmonic extension data can survive. A spanning-tree expression controls the leading vanishing factor of the spectral determinant in the stated two-step setting.

When extensions compose

The three-step metric theorem includes nonlinear composition and its integrability relations. It treats distinct equal-slope line factors, adjacent extension classes and the prescribed quadratic coexact corrections, with no independent first-to-third-layer parameters. Under its balancing and smallness assumptions, the finite model approximates the actual Hermitian–Einstein metric and every small eigenvalue. An example on the product of three projective lines has a weak scale that its polarization parameters alone do not detect.

When factors repeat

The repeated-factor spectral theorem replaces scalar graph directions by matrix multiplicities for simple locally free two-step extensions. The later rank-five three-step paper constructs actual metrics and controls six small matrix modes in a specified deformation family on the product of three projective lines. These results admit arbitrarily separated positive scales, while retaining their respective hypotheses on factors and extensions.

Metrics on moduli spaces

What happens when stabilizers change?

A metric on a bundle and a metric on its moduli space measure different things. The latter measures deformation after identifying changes induced by gauge transformations. When an object acquires more automorphisms, the orbit directions change rank; a comparison must survive this degeneration as well.

Begin with relative metric estimates near reducible Yang–Mills connections. Near a central Yang–Mills connection on a closed surface, the classical quadratic model approximates the actual quotient metric and every small gauge eigenvalue uniformly across stabilizer types and arbitrarily separated orbit scales. The paper also computes the leading metric correction.

The higher-dimensional stability-wall moduli paper retains the full analytic obstruction near a sum of four line bundles on the product of three projective lines. On smooth complex pieces, the relative metric error is quadratic in the coefficient radius, with a radius and comparison constant independent of the distance to the wall. The estimate persists as orbit and obstruction maps lose rank. Quantitative changes of chart also give comparisons of intrinsic lengths and their completions within the local domains.

The rank-five repeated-factor paper adds a noncommutative multiplicity symmetry. It compares harmonic quotient metrics on smooth pieces of the full local deformation problem and identifies the metric on a specified reducible locus with that of the remaining stable rank-four summand. These concrete higher-dimensional theorems retain their additional geometric conclusions. The local stationary comparisons now fit within the general compact-Kähler theorem.

The K3 wall-crossing results above turn these methods into collapse and distance theorems. They retain the finite symmetries in the balancing problem and compute harmonic lengths by complementary-gauge minimization. The general K3 comparison treats arbitrary quiver stabilizers under its geometric hypotheses.

The surface paper's explicit quaternionic projective and hyperbolic reductions provide a comparison with the finite-Planck Higgs quotient problem. A general quaternionic Kähler comparison across stabilizer changes remains open.

Quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin

Which trajectory belongs to the initial metric?

The uniform asymptotic-phase theorem constructs an exact finite-dimensional invariant reduction near any fixed locally free polystable bundle on a compact Kähler manifold. It retains arbitrary multiplicities, noncommuting automorphisms and the complete convergent Kuranishi obstruction.

If the actual harmonic coefficients stay in a prescribed neighborhood and the initial normal displacement is small, a C¹ phase map selects a reduced trajectory that reconstructs an exact HYM flow on the original holomorphic bundle. Relative metric error decays exponentially at a rate controlled by the complementary elliptic gap. The constants are independent of the smallest positive finite eigenvalues, their ratios and changing stabilizers; reconstructing complex gauges may be unbounded.

Fixed locally free background · coefficient confinement · small initial normal displacement

The selected trajectory follows the corrected invariant-graph field, whose leading term is a moment-map gradient. The graph and phase are choices. The exponential estimate measures tracking of this evolving trajectory; it does not assert uniform exponential convergence of that trajectory to equilibrium. This theorem does not supply universal confinement or an algebraic classification of the corrected flow. An obstructed eighteen-coordinate bicubic family includes explicit noncommuting integrable coefficients and separated finite commutator scales; those eigenvalues are distinguished from actual HYM relaxation rates.

The earlier flow-spectrum paper determines small eigenvalue asymptotics along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow in its curve and two-step settings. The metric-response theorem relates sensitivity to polarization to reciprocal graph eigenvalues and constructs a finite-dimensional invariant manifold for the flow. Its tangent-bundle application connects chamber geometry, small spectra, metric response and evolution on the same family.

A reduced evolution equation does not yet identify which of its solutions represents a prescribed initial metric. The initial-condition and phase paper supplies this identification near a stable four-line family on the product of three projective lines. Relative to a chosen invariant graph, the initial metric selects a finite trajectory with exponentially decaying relative tracking error, uniformly in the weak relaxation rates. The phase depends differentiably on the initial metric at fixed bundle and polarization. A common restart brings prescribed smooth determinant-normalized initial metrics into the local regime under the theorem's neighborhood conditions.

The wall and unstable-flow paper treats acyclic connected extension data at the wall and a specified triangular unstable class with locally free Harder–Narasimhan line quotients. In moving diagonal coordinates, the harmonic coefficients remain small while the diagonal metric factors diverge. For the stated initial metrics, this gives all-time confinement and asymptotic phase; after the unstable linear drift is subtracted, a limiting residual retains three real diagonal initial coordinates.

The earlier families retain their additional entry, confinement and asymptotic conclusions. The general theorem extends phase and relative tracking to arbitrary multiplicities under explicit confinement; the stationary complementary correction alone is not a flow-invariant graph.

Categorical phase flows

Can cancellation occur when mass increases?

A related finite-dimensional branch asks how a metric flow on a complex separates its acyclic part from the surviving cohomology. Its cancellation and relaxation theorems require their own estimates.

Mass increase and acyclic cancellation studies two-term complexes over a two-vertex parallel-arrow quiver. For the phase defined by the positivity condition of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit, an explicit noncommuting example has increasing trace-norm mass. The same equation nevertheless eliminates acyclic coefficients.

When the source differential is invertible, every positive initial metric and every choice of vertex charges in the strict upper half-plane gives exponential cancellation and convergence of the normalized cohomology metric. For surjective source differentials, a general large-scale theorem allows arbitrary surviving tuples, unequal dimensions and fixed strict upper-half-plane charges. With dimensions, acyclic rank, charges and total initial norm bounds fixed, its constants are independent of arrow count, stabilizer and acyclic condition number; the threshold is also independent of acyclic rank. A separate theorem gives relaxation in equal surviving dimensions at charges −1+i and 1+i if the cohomology Kempf–Ness function has finite infimum. Initial balance and attainment are unnecessary, and the energy estimate retains the initial excess. The balanced and Pauli cases are corollaries.

The scope of the uniform estimate

The exponential mixed comparison is with instantaneous cohomology; relaxation has its own energy hypothesis. Removal of the large-initial-scale assumption, a uniform final relaxation rate and convergence of normalized surviving metrics remain open. The result is not a theorem for arbitrary complexes or a general quotient metric. Static coefficient and first-derivative mass convergence allow arbitrary fixed invertible differentials. An explicit scalar-scale family converges in C² while every positive Hölder seminorm of its Hessian diverges; general C² control on arrow space remains open.

Harmonic paths and honeycombs

Can boundary norms determine a finite labelled network?

A second categorical branch starts with boundary norms and constructs a path whose degeneration produces a finite labelled network. It shares the programme’s interest in finite descriptions, in a different geometric setting.

Harmonic paths and honeycombs constructs an actual path of norms from every full split boundary triple in every finite rank. The setting is the identity representation of the A₂ quiver over the specified real-exponent Novikov field, with symmetric charges and a fixed radial area form. A renormalized convex action gives the path; algebraic regularity yields finitely many apartments, exact normalized ends, finite balanced support, semistable edge labels and compatible residue filtrations at its vertices. Repeated spectra and collapsed edges are included.

The uniqueness condition

Equality uniqueness is proved in the finite regular principal-velocity class whose one-sided norm motions match to first order at each join. This is an additional condition in the comparison with the printed HKKP harmonic-path conjecture. General boundary continuity and a global Fukaya–Maurer–Cartan correspondence remain open. The worked collapsing-face family has a separate full-path Lipschitz estimate.

The wider programme asks how to reduce the global input, include bubbling and singular sheaves, and determine which trajectories enter and remain in the controlled region.

Light vectors and scalar interactions.

In an actual heterotic K3 compactification, the local relative spectral comparison controls three leading vector masses even at arbitrarily separated Higgs scales. A first tree vector equation includes the metric and two-form towers.     Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We prove that two bounded-remainder extensions of weight-filtration asymptotics for Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow fail for rank-two bundles on connected compact Kähler surfaces with a saturated equal-slope line subsheaf and a singular torsion-free quotient. For every smooth initial metric with fixed Hermitian–Einstein determinant, the squared L2 norm of the inclusion is o(T−12) as flow time T tends to infinity. The pointwise squared norm divided by this integral converges to a positive constant away from the finite singular set. In the quotient by sheaves supported at points, the full rank-polarized subobject lattice has weights −12,+12 and no subsequent iterated weights. Neither relative metric comparability nor a bounded ordinary matrix-logarithm remainder realizes this model on any nonempty compact subset away from the singularities. A splitting-independent Bochner–Martinelli residue, global normalization and an exact dissipation identity give the obstruction, including for nonreduced singularities and repeated line factors. A projective K3 example realizes both features. For a toric tangent-bundle example, we also identify the stable-side Hermitian–Einstein limit and its multiplicity-one curvature concentration.

    Mass increase and acyclic cancellation for phase flows on quiver complexes

    Categorical phase flows: mass increase, general large-scale acyclic cancellation and relaxation under a finite cohomology-energy infimum.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    Trace-norm mass can increase along the Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pandit phase flow defined by positivity, giving a negative answer to the proposed mass-monotonicity question with the prescribed degree branches. For two-term complexes over a two-vertex parallel-arrow quiver, with degree-one term supported at the source and surjective source differential, we nevertheless prove quantitative acyclic cancellation. For fixed upper-half-plane charges, arbitrary surviving tuples and bounded initial arrow norms, a sufficiently large least acyclic singular value gives exponential comparison with instantaneous cohomology. The estimates allow unequal surviving dimensions and are independent of arrow count, stabilizers and acyclic condition numbers. The proof controls the polar phase using only the nonsingular cohomology block. For equal surviving dimensions and charges −1+i, 1+i, a finite infimum of the cohomology Kempf–Ness function forces its moment-map defects to tend to zero, without initial balance or attainment of the infimum. When the source differential is invertible, cancellation holds from every positive metric without a large-scale assumption, and the normalized cohomology metric converges. At fixed external differentials, static mass converges in C1 in bounded arrow coordinates. An explicit family converges in C2 while every positive Hölder seminorm of its Hessian diverges. An exact metric primitive also identifies a positive cohomology potential and a negative ambient Levi direction.

    Harmonic paths and honeycombs

    Harmonic norm paths from all full split boundary triples in every finite rank, with finite labelled honeycombs and uniqueness under first-order matching.

    Preprint · 2026 · Abstract, PDF & citation ↗

    Read the abstract

    We construct harmonic paths of non-expansive norms on the identity representation of the A₂ quiver from arbitrary triples of diagonalizable boundary norms over the real-exponent Novikov field. We use symmetric central charges and a fixed radial area form on the plane. In every finite rank, the path has a finite apartment presentation and a finite honeycomb support with actual semistable edge labels and compatible filtrations at its vertices. It is unique among paths with such presentations whose one-sided norm motions agree to first order at each join. The construction minimizes a renormalized convex action. Its algebraic regularity follows from fixed orthogonal subspaces at gaps in the spectral velocities, second-order scalar approximations, and descent of finite norm data from the Hahn field. Repeated boundary spectra and collapsed edges are included. The result gives existence in the honeycomb specialization of the harmonic-path conjecture of Haiden–Katzarkov–Kontsevich–Pandit, with uniqueness under an explicit matching condition absent from the printed edgewise definition.
