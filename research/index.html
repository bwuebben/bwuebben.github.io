Research programs

The questions
behind the papers.

A paper isolates one question, sharply enough that an argument becomes possible. The sharpening has a price: the paper stops saying which constructions it borrowed, which distinctions it exposed for its neighbours, and what it left unresolved. These guides put that context back.

01 · Markets & inference

What can a successful model actually tell us?

A return is observable. Its explanation is not, and a model that earns has not thereby explained anything. These papers carry the distinction into portfolio construction, into the testing of ideas that now cost nothing to produce, and into the design of a firm that has to decide what to believe.

02 · Knots, four-manifolds & Floer theory

What survives when we cut a space apart?

Cut a space apart and a geometric question becomes a comparison between algebraic objects, one for each piece. The art lies in keeping the information needed to glue the pieces back together, which is exactly what the pieces, examined one at a time, forget.

03 · Calabi–Yau transitions

When can a singular space become smooth?

Every singularity has a local story. A compact space has a global one, and the two need not agree. Six papers work out when the local possibilities can coexist, and how mirror symmetry exchanges the relations that decide it.

  • From local singularities to global relationsIntegrated programme

04 · Geometry & moduli

How does geometry organize its possibilities?

As a bundle approaches a stability wall, its metric loses rigidity and small Dolbeault eigenvalues record the scales of that loss. One programme connects algebraic chambers to spectra, metrics and geometric flows. Related work studies quotient metrics when stabilizers change. Separate projects concern enumerative wall crossing and the realization of prescribed moduli structures.

  • From realizable chambers to metrics and flowsIntegrated programme
  • From formal wall crossing to geometric scatteringDeveloping programme
  • From algebraic identities to geometric realizabilityExploratory frontier
Open the guide↗

05 · Geometry, gravity & information

Which information does a physical description retain?

Every physical description keeps some information and discards the rest. In compact Calabi–Yau models of M-theory, geometry decides which transitions can happen at all; in black-hole physics, an entropy curve records far less than the quantum channel behind it. These papers ask what each description captures and what a complete account would need beyond it.

  • From compact geometry to coupled Higgs transitionsDeveloping programme
  • From entropy curves to the channel behind themDeveloping programme

06 · AI, institutions & power

What changes when intelligence becomes abundant?

Abundant analysis does not abolish the need for institutions, for physical infrastructure or for someone who answers for a decision. It moves the scarcity onto them. This work follows AI from the decisions inside a firm to the markets between machines and the relations between states.

Connections across programmes

Local pieces, global objects.

Much of the mathematics here comes back to one experience. A local construction is easy to write down; the difficulty is proving that the local pieces fit together. When they do not, the obstruction is a property of the whole that no single piece can see.

  • Calabi–Yau threefolds

    Each singularity can admit a smoothing of its own. Whether they smooth together is decided by integral relations among exceptional curves, which belong to the threefold and to none of its singular points.

  • Floer theory

    A comparison between gauge theory and symplectic geometry can hold for every three-manifold separately. Whether it respects the cobordisms that join them, and with which signs, is a further theorem.

  • Open FJRW theory

    Wall-crossing automorphisms are defined one wall at a time. A scattering structure asks them to be consistent around every joint of an integral-affine surface at once.

  • Inverse moduli theory

    Degeneration data can be algebraically coherent at every stratum and still admit no compatible geometric realization.

  • Stability walls

    Near an intersection of walls, the small spectrum is modelled by a weighted Laplacian on the graph of extension components, with one estimate as the margins vanish at arbitrarily different rates. On a tree, ranks and slopes decide the leading spectrum; around a cycle, harmonic extension data can survive.

These are different problems with different obstruction theories, and no theorem here unites them. Wall, relation and gluing name different mechanisms in each place, and a shared vocabulary is not a shared proof. What recurs is a habit of attention: find the structure that governs compatibility, then decide when locally valid data define a global object.

Where a bridge between programmes is real, the guides state it. The relation lattice of the Calabi–Yau papers becomes a gauging constraint in compact M-theory. The infinitesimal orbit operator connects singular quotient metrics to Hermitian–Einstein spectra. A second thread, what a summary forgets, runs through black-hole information, markets and the essay Not Enough.
