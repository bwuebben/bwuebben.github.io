# bwuebben.github.io

Source for my personal site, published by GitHub Pages at
**<https://bwuebben.github.io/>**.

Paper releases follow the release protocol kept in the papers repository.

A GitHub *user site* — the repository name must stay exactly
`bwuebben.github.io`, and GitHub allows one per account. Everything is static
HTML, CSS, and the explorers' client-side JavaScript; there is no
server-side code. Apart from the explorers and interactive essays, the pages require no runtime JavaScript (the landing
page carries a JSON-LD `Person` block, which browsers do not execute). The
checked-in pages are ready to serve. A small Python helper updates shared
navigation and stylesheet links when the site's configuration changes.

## Site structure

The top navigation is Home, Research Programs, Explorers, Writing, Papers, Updates,
and About. The catalogue keeps subject navigation and complete descriptions;
there are no search or filter controls. Its routes include `/research/` and six
subject guides, `/explorers/`, `/writing/` and its interactive essays,
`/about/`, `/updates/`, and `/feed.xml`.

The Research Programs menu starts with Overview, followed by the six topic guides
and the focused programme guides, grouped under Topic guides, Focused guides and Atlas. The Atlas subsection links Degeneration to its interactive paper atlas.
The focused guides are Atiyah–Floer theory and Stability & Degeneration. In `site.json`,
an optional `heading` on a menu link inserts a section label before that link.

Edit `site.json` for navigation. Editorial page content comes from
`tools/build-editorial.py`; the biography comes from `content/about.html` and
identity metadata from `data/person.json`. The essay sources live in
`content/`. Do not edit their generated HTML directly. The complete rebuild ends
with `python3 tools/integrate-site.py` and `python3 tools/update-papers.py`, which
keep the public sitemap and paper pages current.

`/writing/i-rest-my-case/` displays `assets/witten.jpeg` and its caption.
It is generated by `build_writing_updates()` and styled by
`assets/image-note.css`; its entry follows Gradient of Mind in Writing.

The Atiyah–Floer reading route includes Papers I–III: comparison over
𝔽₂, integral transported orientations, and the identification and
normalization of the usual maps. Paper III has its own abstract page,
archived preprint, source and PDF, and appears in the topology guide, the
focused Atiyah–Floer guide and the cobordism explorer’s reading list.

The categorical phase-flow paper in the Stability & Degeneration guide is revised
on 3 November 2026: the 29-page manuscript proves general large-scale cancellation,
separate nonbalanced cohomology relaxation, nonscalar static estimates and sharp
regularity for an explicit family. Its paper page records the original date,
revision date, current public-repository PDF and versioned Zenodo archive.

The heterotic K3 application has its home in the Physics guide, with companion links from Geometry and the focused Stability & Degeneration guide. Its paper page describes the uniform complete leading Yang–Mills amplitude, relative first-order vector correction, selected curvature coefficient and partial next-tree results. The released PDF, Zenodo DOI, full abstract and reciprocal paper links are recorded in `data/papers.json`.

## Rebuilding and checks

Research descriptions in `data/updates.json` are mini abstracts: state the
problem, result and essential qualifications directly. Omit page counts,
"A new preprint", "This paper", "The paper" and publication-availability
announcements. The first sentence must stand alone as the homepage excerpt.
`tools/check-editorial.py` rejects page counts and common manuscript-framing
phrases in update descriptions; it does not replace an editorial read.

Run the whole sequence after any change to editorial sources, essays or paper
data; intermediate states are not consistent, because later steps restore shared
navigation and the sitemap.

```sh
python3 tools/build-editorial.py
python3 tools/build-essays.py
python3 tools/integrate-site.py
python3 tools/update-papers.py
python3 tools/update-site.py --check
python3 tools/update-papers.py --check
python3 tools/check-editorial.py
node tools/check-essay-models.cjs
node tools/check-cobordism-explorer.cjs
node tools/check-at-the-wall.cjs
node tools/check-when-walls-collide.cjs
for f in tools/essay-checks/*.cjs; do node "$f"; done
for f in assets/essays/*.js; do node --check "$f"; done
node --check assets/essay-experiments.js
node --check assets/geometry-overview-model.js
node --check assets/geometry-overview.js
node tools/check-stability-atlas.cjs
git diff --check
```

tools, verification files, and external paper URLs. Papers linked on Zenodo, arXiv, SSRN,

A valid sitemap and successful live test do not guarantee indexing. Record any
remaining fetch errors or pending requests accurately, and allow time for Google
to crawl and update its reports. See Google's [URL Inspection guide](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/12482179)
and [Sitemaps report guide](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7451001).

### Individual paper pages

Each catalogue title opens a stable `/papers/<slug>/` page. The shared template
contains the full author-written abstract, Scholar citation tags, ORCID and
archive links, and a BibTeX download. Mathematical notation is static MathML;
the page, abstract, and navigation require no JavaScript. Most pages
serve a verified public PDF. SSRN-only papers without a public PDF have their complete
registered abstracts and a **Read on SSRN** link while PDF downloads are blocked;
their missing mirrors are tracked in the catalogue data. Do not
advertise a local PDF or emit `citation_pdf_url` when no local PDF exists.

Edit `data/papers.json`, `templates/paper.html`, and `assets/paper.css`, rather
than generated page files. Each `record.json` is a generated copy of the paper's
metadata, including its public source and PDF checksum. Manuscript dates,
revision dates, and archive deposit dates are distinct. Stable slugs and existing
catalogue anchors must survive title changes.

```sh
python3 tools/update-papers.py
python3 tools/update-papers.py --check
```

The generator updates pages, citations, provenance, catalogue title links,
sitemap entries, shared navigation, and stylesheet versions. It checks PDF
checksums before writing. `--fetch-pdfs` retrieves a **missing** PDF from its
reviewed public URL and accepts it only if its checksum matches the catalogue;
it does not silently update a manuscript to a new version. The generator uses
only the Python standard library.

For a paper revision, download the latest released public PDF, verify it, and
update the local PDF and its catalogue record together: full abstract, title,
authors, dates, source commit, checksum, page count, and archive identity. Keep
`current version (PDF)` linked to the public repository. Archive links remain
visible even when that archive contains an older revision. Only use
`citation_doi` when it identifies the version described by the page; otherwise
the suggested citation uses the stable manuscript page URL. Do not rebuild or
change the manuscript as part of a website refresh. Preview representative long
titles, mathematical abstracts, and archive-only pages at desktop and phone
widths. Actual Scholar inclusion remains a separate verification.

## The poem page

`poem.html` is the HTML setting of the LaTeX source for *Gradient of Mind*; the
epigraph on the landing page links to it.

Its mathematics is **pre-rendered with KaTeX at build time**, not typeset in the
browser. `tools/render-poem-math.js` runs `katex.renderToString` over the four
formulas and writes the markup straight into the page, so the site ships no
runtime JavaScript and depends on no CDN — only `assets/katex/katex.min.css`
and the woff2 fonts beside it (324 KB in total, and the `.woff`/`.ttf` fallback
URLs are stripped from the CSS because they are not shipped).

Each formula is emitted as HTML for sighted readers plus MathML for screen
readers; the KaTeX stylesheet hides the MathML visually. That stylesheet also
prefixes a few generic KaTeX class names (`base`, `strut`, `sizing`, ...) with
`katex-`, and the script renames its output to match. To change a formula,
edit its TeX in `tools/render-poem-math.js` and re-run the script. It writes
the markup straight into the `.pmath` blocks of `poem.html`, matched by their
`data-formula` attributes:

```sh
npm install katex        # once, anywhere on NODE_PATH
node tools/render-poem-math.js
```

KaTeX colours itself from the inherited CSS `color`, so the maths follows light
and dark mode with no extra work.

## Stability and Geometric Degeneration

The short name is **Stability & Degeneration**. The focused guide asks how
degeneration can reveal the geometry and topology of moduli spaces; its subtitle
is "From singular limits to nearby geometry". The existing URLs and anchors
remain unchanged; "stability walls" is still used in paper titles and mathematical prose.

The [focused programme guide](https://bwuebben.github.io/research/geometry/quantitative-moduli/)
leads with the route from degeneration data to nearby geometry and compact
global K3 topology and metric limits. It identifies the bundles, slope-stability, polarization walls
and the distinction between bundle metrics and moduli-space metrics. The general
compact-Kähler local theorem and the K3 applications establish local
topology and metric geometry; a dedicated section explains the compactification
and attaching data required for global topological applications. The uniform
asymptotic-phase theorem gives the general local dynamical reduction and C¹ phase
under coefficient confinement and small initial normal displacement; it leads
the dynamical reading route.
The essential cubic bicubic example and singular-flow
obstruction retain their scope. The earlier chamber, spectral, balancing and
quotient-metric papers retain their constructions and additional consequences.
Its source is `content/quantitative-moduli-guide.html`; the opening diagram
in `content/quantitative-moduli-figure.html` connects degeneration data to nearby
geometry, K3 wall crossing and compact global assembly with specified inputs. A wireframe wall-crossing
schematic illustrates the opening, and the mathematical-setting section explicitly
introduces the quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin programme. Its signed
two-factor flow plot shows the exact model profile in dimensionless variables,
with the actual-flow comparison and its scope explained alongside it. Regenerate
these two SVG assets with
`uv run --with matplotlib python tools/build-quantitative-moduli-figures.py`.
Both support light and dark appearance; the wall-crossing surfaces are schematic.
The theorem section
retains the full finite equations and three stationary comparisons.
Inline SVG figures show the five K3 walls,
the cubic obstruction, a vanishing graph mode, relative error and the two kinds
of singularity. The cubic diagram is explicitly a real slice of the reduced
zero set and does not depict embedded nonreduced structure.

`SUBGUIDES` in `tools/build-editorial.py` groups the manuscripts by mathematical
role, starting with the general local theorem. References use descriptive titles
and links; manuscript numbers indicate the suggested reading order. Styles are
confined to `assets/quantitative-moduli.css`. Native disclosures carry technical hypotheses;
the fixed locally free background, scope of the stationary theorem and confinement
and initial-displacement hypotheses of the dynamical theorem remain visible in
the main text. The guide preserves every existing anchor, three reading routes and the link
to At the Wall. Fixed-background dependence, coordinate-sensitive error orders,
K3 rescaling hypotheses and the distinction between cubic obstruction and
spectral scale separation remain explicit. Compact K3 contractions without bubbling now
have global harmonic completion and integral attachment calculations. Reconstruction
without complement and monodromy input, bubbling, general entry and confinement,
the algebraic asymptotics of the corrected finite flow and the singular replacement
law remain separate questions.
Exponential tracking is distinguished from convergence to equilibrium.
The topology sought is primarily that of moduli spaces; implications
for the base manifold require an additional argument.

The [Geometry & moduli overview](https://bwuebben.github.io/research/geometry/#stability-degeneration)
leads with degeneration as a route to geometry and topology, including compact
K3 integral attachments and global harmonic metric collapse. Its introduction,
scope, reading route and homepage summary reflect the same programme as the
focused guide. The global theorem retains absence of an ideal boundary and full
convergent quiver charts with module-simple points; additive reconstruction and
middle-degree attachment calculations retain their different global inputs.
