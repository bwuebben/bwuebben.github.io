Specialization and integral curve counts.

When a threefold with ordinary double points is smoothed, the exceptional rational curves of a small resolution disappear. Several curve classes on the resolution can then give the same class on the smoothing. How do their counts combine?

First divide out the local factor for each exceptional curve. In balanced antidiagonal coordinates, the exponent for a nonzero smoothing class in each genus is the finite sum of the normalized resolution exponents above it. These exponents are integers. In scalar coordinates the same summation transfers integrality when the resolution exponents are integral, a hypothesis that can fail on a smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold.

The conifold formula assumes a projective transition with smooth algebraic total space, trivial canonical bundles and torsion-free second homology at both smooth endpoints. If the first and second cohomology of the structure sheaf vanish at both endpoints, the proof uses fixed-target counts. In the broader setting it assumes that the connected Gromov–Witten evaluations respect the relations from analytic families.

In a compact four-node example, the calculation uses stable-pair evaluation on geometric local classes to find that eight of the 37 candidate lifts of a primitive line class contribute. For the sixteen-node quintic containing a plane, the normalized lifts in smoothing degree d have exceptional coordinate between 0 and 4d. The upper bound is attained among the candidates. Determining which of them carry nonzero counts is a further problem.

Opposite circle weights give integral generators for curve counts. Equal weights can give unbounded denominators on one smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold, even in classes of divisibility three. The local wall-crossing results describe how covers and spin structures enter the contribution of an embedded curve.

The two-circle calculation interprets the Euler-characteristic grading as a rotation weight. The first-variation theorem compares the normalized indices over the Picard family in all degrees to first order. At exchanged grading one, the full volume-character dependence agrees for each Young diagram. At exchanged gradings two and three, the diagram-by-diagram comparison gives equality for every Euler characteristic with the volume character free.
