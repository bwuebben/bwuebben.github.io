Universal curve counting Integrated programme

Specialization and integral curve counts.

A conifold transition changes both a threefold and the curve classes it carries. Universal curve counting records curve contributions in an algebra before extracting numerical invariants. This programme asks whether transition formulas hold in that algebra, whether they make compact examples computable, and when their product expansions have integer exponents.

Specialization and a compact calculation

The first paper proves a rational universal specialization formula for projective conifold transitions with smooth algebraic total space and torsion-free second homology at both endpoints. A geometric support bound makes the sum finite in each bounded target degree. In a compact four-node example, it determines all 37 candidate contributions over a primitive line class: eight are nonzero, and their sum agrees with an independent stable-pair calculation on the smoothing. The smoothing coefficient in twice the line class is also determined.

The boundary of integrality

The second paper constructs a compact counterexample for Pardon's scalar local-curve factors. The genus-ten exponent at three times a primitive class has fractional part 2/3. An exact local calculation and a stable-pair count on a Hirzebruch surface produce the obstruction. The same paper proves integrality in classes of divisibility at most two. The elementary integer expansion is an input to this comparison.

Antidiagonal local factors

Antidiagonal local curves and integral exponents constructs local classes using opposite circle weights on the normal directions to a balanced curve. Assuming Pardon’s Gromov–Witten evaluation for analytic families, their product gives the universal series of every smooth projective Calabi–Yau threefold with exponents indexed by effective classes in full integral homology. When several classes have the same multiple, pullback sums over every effective root. The exponents are the Ionel–Parker elementary-cluster integers. The cluster evaluation characterizes the compatible universal factor, and agreement in degree two already forces the two normal line bundles to have equal degree.

A geometric product formula

A geometric product formula for universal curve counting proves the antidiagonal factorization by deforming the local normal geometry. In almost-complex families each multiple of an elementary curve represents the corresponding antidiagonal class. Excision for disconnected cycles gives a finite product at bounded energy. Stable maps of the least genus distinguish the multiplicity-one classes and lift the product to Pardon’s rational cycle algebra. The exponents count signed elementary clusters in each effective integral class and genus.

What the results leave open

The rational specialization theorem survives the failure of general scalar integrality. Its integrality-transfer statement remains conditional on integral exponents on the resolution. Further questions concern geometric support bounds for larger compact computations and the dependence of other local factors on their normal geometry.

Read the specialization paper first for the transition setting, then compare the scalar counterexample with the conditional antidiagonal factorization and its geometric proof.
