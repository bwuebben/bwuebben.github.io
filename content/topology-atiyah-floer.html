Atiyah–Floer comparisons Developing programme

From knot complexes to functorial Atiyah–Floer theory.

The Atiyah–Floer conjecture predicts that two Floer theories of a three-manifold agree. One counts instantons on a four-dimensional cylinder; the other counts holomorphic curves in a space of flat connections on a surface that splits the manifold in two. Agreement as groups is the first theorem one wants. These papers ask what comes after it, along two routes.

Knots: agreement as complexes

For the torus knots T(3,n), the pillowcase complex is chain homotopy equivalent over 𝔽₂ to the Daemi–Scaduto complex of singular instanton homology. For the hyperbolic pretzel knots P(−2,3,q) the two homologies agree as ℤ/4-graded groups. A different decomposition needs bounding cochains: with none, the pairing has rank 7 where instanton homology has rank 9. Constructing the cochains of Cazassus, Herald, Kirk and Kotelskiy where zero cochains give the wrong rank, and extending the chain-level identification to all torus knots, are the open problems on this route.

Three-manifolds: agreement as functors

The Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy isomorphism for framed instanton homology intertwines the cobordism maps of connected cobordisms: over 𝔽₂ in the homotopy category of chain complexes, over ℤ up to one sign for each cobordism, and, after a comparison of orientation conventions, for the usual framed instanton maps in their singular Hopf-link realization. The point operation of the splitting surface is respected only after a correction of 8, 4 and 0 in genus one, two and higher, produced by bubbles on the matching line. Comparisons with Scaduto's nonsingular cap convention and with prescribed relative-spin operations remain open.

The knot papers use singular instanton homology and the cobordism papers use framed instanton homology; no paper relates the two comparisons. The point-operations paper is independent of the cobordism series and can be read after either route. The bounding-cochain paper is conditional on Conjecture D of Cazassus, Herald, Kirk and Kotelskiy, and says so. The pillowcase explorer and the cobordism explorer give visual introductions to the two routes.
