Atiyah–Floer comparisons Developing programme

From knot complexes to functorial Atiyah–Floer theory.

The Atiyah–Floer conjecture predicts that two Floer theories of a three-manifold agree. One counts instantons on a four-dimensional cylinder; the other counts holomorphic curves in a space of flat connections on a surface that splits the manifold in two. Agreement as groups is the first theorem one wants. These papers ask what comes after it: do the theories agree as chain complexes for explicit knots, and does the agreement respect the maps induced by four-dimensional cobordisms and the operations that come from the splitting surface?

What is proved

Knots, exactly. For the torus knots T(3,n), the pillowcase complex is chain homotopy equivalent, over the field of two elements, to the Daemi–Scaduto complex of singular instanton homology. For the hyperbolic pretzel knots P(−2,3,q) the two homologies agree as ℤ/4-graded groups. A different decomposition needs bounding cochains: with none, the pairing has rank 7 where instanton homology has rank 9.

Cobordisms. The Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy isomorphism for framed instanton homology intertwines the cobordism maps for connected cobordisms. Over the field of two elements this holds in the homotopy category of chain complexes; over the integers, on homology and up to one sign for each cobordism.

Point operations. The comparison respects the operations of the splitting surface only after a correction of 8, 4 and 0 in genus one, two and higher, produced by bubbles on the matching line. Without it the identity is false.

The missing theorems

For knots, construct the bounding cochains of Cazassus, Herald, Kirk and Kotelskiy where zero cochains give the wrong rank, and prove that they restore instanton homology; then extend the chain-level identification to all torus knots.

For three-manifolds, compare the integral maps with Scaduto's homology-oriented maps. The two orientation conventions can differ by a grading operator rather than an overall sign, so the comparison needs ℤ/4 grading data.

Between them. The knot papers use singular instanton homology and the cobordism papers use framed instanton homology. No paper relates the two comparisons. This is a question the guide asks, not one the papers claim to answer.

Why it matters

Much of the power of Floer theory lies in its maps: an invariant of a closed four-manifold is assembled from the maps of the pieces it is cut into. Agreement of groups says that two theories count the same thing. Naturality says that they are, for the purposes of gluing, the same theory, so that a computation on either side can be carried out on the other.

The genus corrections are part of the answer. They show that the dictionary between the two sides is not the obvious one, and that its terms are determined by bubbling the comparison cannot avoid.
