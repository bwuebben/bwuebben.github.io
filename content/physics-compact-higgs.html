Compact M-theory transitions Developing programme

From compact geometry to coupled Higgs transitions.

In M-theory on a singular Calabi–Yau threefold, each singular point carries its own five-dimensional field theory, and each could open a Higgs branch on its own. In a compact space none of them is on its own: they share the vector fields of the compactification. Four papers ask which combinations of local directions survive that sharing, and what the transition looks like once gravity is kept.

What is established

One pairing, two readings. The pairing of divisors with curves is at once the electric charge of wrapped membranes and the gauging of local flavour symmetries. The rigid invariant Higgs directions are exactly the relation space of the Calabi–Yau smoothing criterion; an actual transition must also avoid every local discriminant.

Together or not at all. In the threefold X₉, two nodes and an E₂ point share a one-dimensional relation space, so none can be Higgsed without the others. No limit that decouples gravity at bounded local scales keeps the shared gauging.

The transition, computed. For X₉ the papers compute the surviving integral charge lattice and continue all eight periods along a marked branch, fixing the genus-zero prepotential with its constant term. With gravity retained, a quaternionic Kähler moment map has no constant to adjust, so the parameter that would smooth the singularity is absent. Under a stated regularity hypothesis the branch keeps the shape ℂ²/ℤ₄.

The open problems

The relation criterion is exact within its geometric hypotheses. It is not a spectrum and not a metric.

The geometry at finite Planck mass. The full quaternionic Kähler hypermultiplet space is unknown. In particular, is the completion at the E₂ point the Higgs branch of the interacting E₂ theory coupled to supergravity?

The magnetic spectrum. Stable sheaves are counted on each side, but deciding which remain physical particles needs a stability condition along the continued periods.

Breadth. Cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degree one to four carry deformations that link homology does not see, and need a new local-to-global comparison.

Why it matters

Most accounts of Higgs transitions in string compactifications are local: a singularity, its field theory, its branch. Here the global geometry is not scenery. It is a constraint inside the physics, exact in its geometric setting, and gravity forces sectors to move together that a local analysis would treat separately.

It is a rare place where a theorem about smoothing relations reads, line for line, as a statement about which rigid Higgs directions exist, and where the boundary between what is proved and what is assumed can be stated precisely.
