I · The question

From an isomorphism to a functor.

Floer homology assigns a group to a three-manifold and a map to each four-dimensional cobordism between three-manifolds. The groups are the invariants; the maps are what makes the theory useful, because an invariant of a closed four-manifold is assembled from the maps of the pieces it is cut into. An isomorphism between two Floer theories therefore raises a second question at once: does it carry the maps of one theory to the maps of the other?

The two sides

On the gauge-theoretic side stands framed instanton homology, the version of instanton Floer homology introduced by Kronheimer and Mrowka in which the three-manifold is summed with a three-torus, so that the flat connections that matter are irreducible. On the symplectic side, a Heegaard splitting cuts the three-manifold into two pieces meeting along a surface. The flat connections on the surface form a symplectic moduli space, the connections that extend over each piece form a Lagrangian in it, and the Lagrangian Floer homology of the two is the symplectic invariant. In the framed construction the two pieces are compression bodies rather than handlebodies.

Daemi, Fukaya and Lipyanskiy proved that the two groups are isomorphic. Their isomorphism counts solutions of a mixed equation: anti-self-dual connections on a four-dimensional part and holomorphic curves on a two-dimensional part, coupled by a matching condition along a common boundary.

The organizing question

A cobordism W from M₀ to M₁ induces a map F(W) in framed instanton homology. The symplectic side has its own candidate maps S(W), defined by counting holomorphic triangles with the correspondences of compression bodies as boundary conditions. Naturality asks that the two routes around the square agree:

ΨM₁ ∘ F(W) ≃ ± S(W) ∘ ΨM₀Ψ is the mixed comparison. The sign is one sign for the entire map.

Three questions are folded into this line. Does the square commute at all, say with coefficients in the field of two elements? Does it commute with integer coefficients, once every count is signed by a coherent orientation system? And when it does, which orientation system has been used, and how does it relate to the conventions in which framed instanton homology is usually defined? The three cobordism papers answer these in turn.

II · Three papers, one progression

Parity, then signs, then conventions.

The three papers are not variations on one theme. Each removes a limitation of the one before it: the first establishes the square modulo two, the second signs it, and the third identifies the signed square with the conventions in which framed instanton homology is usually defined.

Paper I · Coefficients in 𝔽₂

From isomorphisms to cobordism maps

Mixed isomorphisms of Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy type intertwine the Kronheimer–Mrowka cobordism maps with maps defined by holomorphic curves alone, for every connected cobordism between connected three-manifolds that carries a framed arc between its ends and an SO(3) bundle trivialized along the ends and the arc. The symplectic maps count holomorphic triangles, with the correspondence of a compression body as one boundary condition for one- and three-handles. They are independent of the handle decomposition and form a functor isomorphic to framed instanton homology.

Two analytic results carry the proof: an adiabatic limit in which a compression body collapses next to a holomorphic strip and becomes a Lagrangian boundary condition, and an excision theorem along a torus for the mixed equation.

Paper II · Coefficients in ℤ

From parity to signed counts

Orientations are transported from the gauge theory through the mixed equation. With them, the integral instanton cobordism maps in the almost-complex orientation convention are intertwined with signed symplectic maps, up to chain homotopy and one overall sign for each cobordism, so that the symplectic maps form a projective functor on homology. The new input is that the two degenerations of Paper I, collapse and excision, preserve orientations.

Where the integral framed instanton homology vanishes, the complexes are contractible and the maps are zero. The paper leaves open how its transported orientations relate to the homology-oriented integral maps of the literature.

Paper III · Integral chain-level comparisons

From transported orientations to the usual conventions

Two comparisons close the gap left by Paper II. The first identifies the comparison with the usual framed instanton maps, defined through the singular Hopf-link realization with their homology orientations, by way of the Kronheimer–Mrowka excision cobordism. The identification holds up to an explicit grading conversion and one overall sign, compatibly with changes of presentation and with the cobordism maps. The second compares two choices inside the Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy orientation construction, the system calibrated to Paper II's gauge reference and the system in which attaching a generator sphere preserves orientation, and constructs integral chain homotopy equivalences between them for every closed connected oriented three-manifold.

The equivalences are finite weighted count expressions: integer combinations of products of ordinary instanton counts, surgery counts, and forward and reflected mixed counts. Their coefficients are an algebraic choice; the composite comparison has a fixed projective homotopy class.

What the three say together

On homology, the usual framed instanton theory, with the stated convention conversion, is naturally isomorphic up to sign to a symplectic theory whose maps are counts of holomorphic curves. The result is not only an isomorphism of groups for each three-manifold but a comparison compatible with the cobordisms between them.

How to read the claims

The three are research manuscripts. Each states its hypotheses in its introduction, and independent specialist refereeing of the analytic arguments is outstanding. The second theorem of Paper III constructs a coherent system of normalizing maps; it does not identify that system with independently prescribed geometric counts, and the final section of the paper says which identifications remain open.

III · A second direction

Operations, and the bubbles that correct them.

Floer homology carries more than cobordism maps. The splitting surface acts on it through point, loop and surface operations, and a comparison of theories should respect these as well. A separate paper studies the point operation in the embedded-Lagrangian setting of Daemi, Fukaya and Lipyanskiy, and finds that the naive identification is false.

What is proved

In the positive Pontryagin normalization, the symplectic point operation agrees with the gauge-theoretic one only after a shift of 8, 4 or 0, according as the marked component has genus one, two, or at least three. One coherent orientation system serves both matching components, and together with the known surface and loop comparisons the result gives the corrected action of the observable algebra over every commutative coefficient ring. When four is invertible, the quarter-Pontryagin normalization gives the shifts 2, 1 and 0.

Where the correction comes from

An index-four mixed sequence can develop a bubble on the matching line, of normalized energy one-half. Holomorphic doubling identifies the limiting models, a Dirichlet construction glues them back to regular index-zero solutions, and the relative incidence degrees of these ends, eight and four in genera one and two, are the correction terms. The dictionary between the two theories is therefore not the obvious one. Its terms are fixed by degenerations the comparison cannot avoid.

The point-operations paper and the cobordism series share the mixed equation but work in different settings: the embedded-Lagrangian comparison with its admissible bundles on one side, the framed torus-summed construction on the other. Neither depends on the other. The essay Why broken things make invariants explains the bubbling in non-technical terms.

What is settled, and what is not.

Near term: two prescribed conventions

The nonsingular cap. Scaduto's framed construction defines its reference lines and homology-oriented maps through a particular torus cap. How does the boundary gauge action on that cap's reference line compare with the singular Hopf-link and almost-complex reference actions, and how does the comparison behave under cobordism gluing? A closed index formula alone does not identify the action. The incoming reference operator, the outgoing bundle action and the quotient identification must be kept together in one determinant-gluing diagram.

Relative-spin operations. A relative-spin system fixes background classes and spin data on the Lagrangian correspondences before any count is made. For a specified compatible system, do its strip, cup, cap, triangle and stabilization maps agree, up to the required integral homotopies, with the transported symplectic maps and their mixed comparison? Agreement on strip complexes would not settle the auxiliary operations, and the target system must be specified together with its behaviour under changes of genus.

Longer term: coherence and the knot strand

The present results hold on homology, up to one sign for each cobordism, or at chain level up to homotopy. A comparison that respects compositions coherently at chain level, and eventually a categorical or spectrum-level refinement, would be the natural continuation. No paper here attempts it.

The knot papers in the topology guide compare singular instanton homology with pillowcase complexes; the cobordism papers compare framed instanton homology with the Floer homology of a splitting. No paper relates the two comparisons. Whether the two strands can be assembled into one theory, in which knot complexes and three-manifold complexes are related by maps the comparison respects, is a question this guide asks and none of the manuscripts claims to answer.

One sharply posed unsolved problem is worth more here than a list of conjectures. The two questions above are stated in Paper III with the conventions they require.

V · Reading order, hypotheses and companions

Where to begin, and what each theorem assumes.

Reading order

Begin with the introduction of Paper I and its Theorems A, B and C, which state the comparison over 𝔽₂ and the two analytic inputs. Paper II can then be read for its orientation transport, with the unsigned analysis taken from Paper I. Paper III assumes both and is read for its two comparison theorems and its final section of open questions. The point-operations paper is independent and can be read at any point.

Hypotheses that travel with the theorems

The cobordism series concerns connected cobordisms between connected three-manifolds, decorated with a framed arc between the ends and an SO(3) bundle trivialized along the ends and the arc; the bundle and framing data are part of the morphism. The comparison uses the framed construction, in which the three-manifold is summed with a three-torus, and Heegaard presentations of positive genus whose primary perturbations lie in a specified small admissible domain. The common-data statements of Paper III hold where the hypotheses of the Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy construction and of Papers I and II are both satisfied, and extend to arbitrary regular small presentations by geometric transport.

None of the papers constructs the units, counits or closed pairings of a full field theory, and none asserts a comparison with orientation conventions beyond those named in the table above.
