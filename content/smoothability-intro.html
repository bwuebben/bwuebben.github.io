In superstring theory, a Calabi–Yau threefold can supply the six compact dimensions of spacetime. Can two topologically different shapes for those dimensions be connected by a continuous path of string vacua? In 1987 Miles Reid suggested that families of smooth Calabi–Yau threefolds might meet through singular spaces, an idea now known as Reid’s fantasy. Mirror symmetry gives the same physics a second geometric description. David Morrison predicted that a conifold transition connecting two families should run in reverse on their mirrors.

Start with several rational curves, each topologically a two-sphere, and shrink them to points called nodes. If the nodes can be smoothed together, a three-sphere appears where each curve collapsed. On the mirror, Morrison predicted the opposite sequence. Three-spheres shrink to nodes, and a resolution replaces each node with a curve. In type IIB theory a brane wrapped around a collapsing three-sphere appears to a four-dimensional observer as a particle whose mass tends to zero. In suitable examples, several such particles can condense and open a new branch of vacua.

Which of these passages does the compact geometry allow? The threefold illustrated here has thirty singular points. Each can be smoothed in its own neighbourhood, yet no smoothing exists for the whole threefold. In a different example, four curves satisfy a single relation with coefficients (1, −1, −1, 1). Those very integers reappear in a relation among the four vanishing spheres on the mirror.
