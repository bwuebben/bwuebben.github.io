What is established

For two actual massless bundle scalars, the complete leading Yang–Mills tree amplitude approaches an explicit light-vector exchange formula. Its absolute error is uniform as b/a tends to zero, below the heavier Higgs masses and away from the light poles. All internal vector and scalar modes are included.

The three squared masses are proportional to b², a² and a²+b² with relative geometric control. The defined first-order coupled vector equation has a ratio-uniform relative correction bound. The selected scalar curvature coefficient grows as 1/b² with uniform relative error.

The next interaction coefficient

A quadratic potential-mixing estimate controls the response to K3 metric deformations, and the universal graviton exchange is computed. The graviton's massless pole requires an angular exclusion beyond the leading Yang–Mills domain.

The full next heterotic coefficient still requires the corrected scalar states, neutral-field sources, induced contacts and mixed exchanges. Finite-string-scale remainders, quantum corrections and derivatives across all stabilizer strata remain open.

Why the estimates are uniform

Harmonic orthogonality gives quadratic accuracy for the actual cubic vertices. The scalar partners' exchange cancels the zero-momentum contact term exactly. Fixed complementary gaps then control the remaining internal tower without dividing every error by the smallest Higgs mass.

The physical curvature identification first extracts the leading large-volume coefficient; the resulting geometric estimate is uniform in the hierarchy. The underlying local geometry belongs to the Stability and Geometric Degeneration programme.
