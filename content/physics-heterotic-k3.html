Heterotic K3 compactification Developing programme

From bundle degeneration to light fields.

A smooth K3 can support gauge bundles that approach a reducible limit. Additional gauge generators become parallel there, and their vector fields become massless. The same degeneration changes scalar interactions. Quantitative Hermitian–Einstein geometry makes both effects calculable in a specified compactification.

What is established

Three leading squared vector masses are proportional to b², a² and a²+b². Their relative geometric errors remain quadratic even when b/a tends to zero. The defined first-order tree vector equation includes both complete leading metric and two-form towers, with an anti-self-dual identity preserving the massless gauge subspaces.

A compensated reduction identifies a leading neutral-scalar interaction with inverse-square growth along each fixed positive deformation ratio. It retains the other neutral fields and specifies when the massive Higgs multiplets can be eliminated.

The remaining estimates

The scalar statement extracts the tree large-volume coefficient before approaching enhancement. A uniform simultaneous finite-string-scale limit would require bounds on higher physical coefficients and their derivatives. The first-order vector bound likewise does not control an uncomputed remainder in exact string poles.

Nonzero-FI wall crossing, quantum corrections and derivative estimates across all stabilizer strata remain separate problems.

Why the relative estimate matters

An absolute spectral error can overwhelm the smallest Higgs mass. The geometric theorem controls each mass relative to its own size. Scalar interactions require additional derivative estimates and identification with the full physical target; a metric comparison alone does not supply either step.

The underlying local theorem belongs to the Stability and Geometric Degeneration programme. The compactification is an application with its own physical normalization and approximation regimes.
