Two different metrics

A stable bundle has a canonical Hermitian–Einstein metric on its fibres. The moduli space has another metric: the harmonic L² pairing measures how much the bundle changes as its moduli point moves. Understanding one does not automatically determine the other.

A wall supplies a simpler object

A polystable bundle splits into stable factors of the same slope. Extensions of those factors, their automorphisms and their obstruction equations give finite data from which one can reconstruct nearby geometry.

What the general local theorem recovers

Near any fixed locally free polystable bundle on a compact Kähler manifold, the full local model reconstructs Hermitian–Einstein solutions and identifies the local quotient topology. On genuine smooth pieces it compares the harmonic moduli metric with the finite quotient metric. Arbitrary multiplicities and the convergent obstruction are retained.

Why the full equations matter

A stable bundle on a smooth bicubic Calabi–Yau threefold has deformation equations a²b = ab² = 0. Their quadratic part vanishes, yet mixed directions are obstructed at order three. Keeping only the first obstruction would invent deformations that do not exist.

What the degeneration does—and what the global data supply

The explicit six-chamber calculation determines the marked complement rings, boundary restrictions and universal-index classes in the rank-three fourfold family. Four corrected Mukai flops and two divisorial isomorphisms give its cohomological wall actions. The general reconstruction theorem still takes integral cochains on all geometric overlaps, including monodromy, as input; a fibre's cohomology and divisor classes alone do not determine them.

Zoom in: a complete metric model

Rescale near a shrinking exceptional cycle and a complete Calabi or cotangent-Grassmannian metric appears. Successive rescalings keep convergence conditions on normalized parameters and normal basepoints.

Zoom out: the whole contraction

The compact K3 theorem identifies the central harmonic completion and bounds distortion of the actual contraction graph. It requires no ideal boundary and full convergent quiver charts with module-simple points in their complex zero fibres. Its bound tends to zero through arbitrary adjacent chambers.

Canonical metrics without a resolution or a smoothing

Canonical metrics on singular moduli of K3 bundles treats twice-primitive Mukai vectors on a K3 surface of Picard rank one. A rank bound ensures that every semistable sheaf and every slope Jordan–Hölder factor is locally free. The resulting compact moduli spaces have positive-dimensional singular strata and admit neither a symplectic resolution nor a global smoothing.

The harmonic L² metric is the canonical singular Ricci-flat metric in a positive multiple of an ample determinant class. Exact quadraticity in harmonic Kuranishi coordinates gives holomorphic Ext-quiver charts with relative O(r²) metric error. The estimate is uniform through changes of stabilizer within each fixed background chart. The regular-locus completion recovers the original space, and its unique tangent cones are the flat hyperkähler quotients of the extension spaces.

For the Mukai vector (5,H,0) on a quartic K3, the moduli space has complex dimension 18 and singular strata of dimensions 12 and 6. No singular point is locally isometric to its tangent cone. This does not assert that the exponent is optimal or that higher derivatives satisfy uniform estimates across lower strata; the family excludes bubbling.

From contraction geometry to a genuine Calabi–Yau smoothing

The conifold spectral theorem determines all small positive two-form eigenvalues of the actual Ricci-flat metrics from exceptional periods and the global quotient harmonic norm. It treats fixed polarized families with simply connected projective small resolutions, first-order smoothing at every node and approach along a fixed ray. Three compact applications include an original-side four-node family from the Calabi–Yau smoothing programme; the split quintic also has explicit integral rings and attachment maps.

This extends the connection between degeneration, topology and geometry to genuine smoothings of the underlying threefold. The detailed guide separates the new spectral theorem from the inherited smoothing and metric-existence results. Independent node scales, arbitrary gauge bundles and HYM flow on these families remain further questions.

03 · Follow an initial metric

Which finite trajectory represents the flow?

The quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin programme asks how algebraic stability controls the evolution toward canonical metrics, including near a wall where some relaxation scales become very slow.

The initial condition selects the phase

The uniform asymptotic-phase theorem gives an exact finite-dimensional comparison flow near a fixed locally free polystable bundle. A C¹ phase map selects its trajectory from the initial metric, retaining full obstruction and all multiplicity directions.

Under coefficient confinement and small initial normal displacement, relative metric error decays exponentially at a rate controlled by the complementary elliptic gap. The estimate is uniform through weak positive finite scales and changing stabilizers.

The diagram is schematic. Tracking a moving trajectory is distinct from uniform exponential convergence to equilibrium. General entry, confinement and the algebraic asymptotics of the corrected finite flow remain open.

The wider programme

Different families expose different parts of the problem.

The foundational toric and extension families supply realizable chambers, separated spectral scales, and additional entry and confinement theorems for flow. The categorical branches ask related finite-description questions with their own objects and hypotheses.

Chambers, weak spectra and local K3 models

The K3 metric wall-crossing application uses the stationary and quotient-metric methods on a complex twelve-dimensional rank-three bundle-moduli neighborhood. Uniform comparison with cotangent-Grassmannian metrics gives exceptional-fibre collapse, complete rescaled limits and intrinsic distances through changes of stabilizer. This result is local; the later compact K3 constructions address global completion under explicit no-bubbling hypotheses.

The general K3 metric comparison retains every finite symmetry direction and gives uniform relative harmonic metrics through changing stabilizers, with linear spatial error unless a central sign symmetry gives quadratic error. Its rank-five doubled-triangle example has three spherical factors, two independent polarization parameters and five realized walls. Under the stated convergence conditions, intrinsic metric limits include singular parameter directions and finite successive rescalings.

The full stationary and dynamical theorems

The general local HYM model retains the full convergent Kuranishi obstruction and reconstructs every sufficiently nearby HE solution at any fixed locally free polystable compact-Kähler background. It compares the complete small endomorphism spectrum and harmonic quotient metrics uniformly through stabilizer changes and separated positive scales. Canonical harmonic coordinates give quadratic error, also with varying polarizations in moving backgrounds. A stable bundle on a smooth bicubic has essential cubic obstruction, and the theorem covers the full three-copy family.

The uniform asymptotic-phase theorem extends exact local HYM reduction to arbitrary fixed locally free polystable bundles, retaining the full convergent obstruction and all multiplicity directions. Under coefficient confinement and small initial normal displacement, an initial metric selects an exact finite trajectory through a C¹ phase map. Relative metric error decays exponentially with constants controlled by the complementary elliptic gap, independently of weak positive finite scales and stabilizer changes. Noncommuting and unbounded reconstructing gauges are allowed; an obstructed bicubic supplies explicit integrable examples.

Categorical phase flows and harmonic paths

A finite-dimensional branch studies phase flows on quiver complexes. Trace-norm mass can increase, yet invertible differentials give exponential acyclic cancellation. For surjective source differentials and a sufficiently large least initial acyclic singular value, arbitrary surviving tuples, unequal dimensions and fixed upper-half-plane charges admit exponential comparison with instantaneous cohomology. At fixed dimensions and norm bounds, constants are independent of arrow count, stabilizer and acyclic condition number. Relaxation follows separately in equal dimensions at charges −1+i and 1+i when the cohomology Kempf–Ness function has finite infimum. General quotient geometry remains open.

The harmonic-path branch constructs actual norm paths and finite labelled honeycombs from every full split boundary triple in every finite rank, for the specified A₂ representation, Novikov field, symmetric charges and radial area form. Equality uniqueness holds in the finite regular principal-velocity class with first-order matching. General boundary continuity and a global Fukaya correspondence remain open.

Singular sheaves

A rank-two surface obstruction rules out two bounded-remainder extensions of metric asymptotics for singular graded quotients, including nonreduced zeros and repeated factors. The replacement growth law is still unknown.

Global reconstruction

The marked rank-three example has calculated complements and restriction maps. Recovering such data in broader families and reconstructing general cup products from degeneration remain further questions. The canonical singular metric theorem still excludes bubbling; sharp cone errors and derivative asymptotics near lower strata are also open.

General dynamics

The general phase theorem is confined and local. One still needs entry and confinement for wider initial data, and an algebraic description of the corrected flow's long-time behaviour.
