The geometric route

Begin with the toric tangent stability papers, then local stability chambers. Continue to nonsimplicial tangent chambers, which constructs weak-Fano examples, determines accessible slope parameters and gives a criterion without local freeness. Its surface example also supplies the singular graded limit studied below. The blowup-and-bundle paper includes an exact census of all 6,281 Picard-number-ten eightfolds; every strictly semistable case is a product.

The spectral route

Begin with eigenvalues near stability walls, then bipartite extensions and repeated stable factors. The three-step metric theorem treats composable extensions whose integrability imposes relations. It compares actual Hermitian–Einstein metrics and every small spectral scale with a finite balanced model, even when the positive scales separate arbitrarily.

Where the routes meet

Metric response and flow for two-step extensions joins the independent chamber geometry and spectral mechanism on the same tangent bundles. The later papers ask what survives when extensions compose, stabilizers change or a graded quotient becomes singular. Their conclusions concern explicit geometric classes; they do not assert a theorem for every filtration.

From metrics on bundles to metrics on moduli

L² metrics near a stability wall retains the full analytic obstruction in a four-line deformation problem on the product of three projective lines. The harmonic quotient metric has uniform relative accuracy even as orbit and obstruction maps lose rank. The three-step repeated-factor paper proves actual metric and six-mode spectral estimates in a rank-five model, and compares its local moduli metric along a reducible boundary. These extend the earlier surface quotient-metric work in different, explicitly stated geometric settings.

Which finite trajectory belongs to the initial metric?

The initial-condition and phase theorem gives uniform exponential tracking relative to a chosen invariant graph near a stable family. The wall and unstable-flow theorem replaces equilibrium confinement by an all-time estimate in moving coefficients: the coefficients stay small while diagonal metrics diverge. After subtracting unstable linear drift, a limiting residual retains initial-condition information. The earlier flow-spectrum paper remains a dynamical entry point.

What changes when the graded sheaf is singular?

Singular graded limits and metric growth gives a toric surface example where a bounded-remainder extension of the categorical weight-filtration law fails, even away from the singular point. Every smooth initial metric with fixed Hermitian–Einstein determinant has strictly faster decay on the equal-slope line than the proposed model. The same point carries the curvature defect in the stationary wall limit. The existing asymptotic theorem with locally free graded object remains intact.

The next questions

A general theory should accommodate other stable factors and filtrations, extend initial-condition-sensitive dynamics to repeated factors, and identify the replacement asymptotic law in the singular example. The present estimates establish components of that programme and expose a specific obstruction; they do not determine the missing singular growth law.
