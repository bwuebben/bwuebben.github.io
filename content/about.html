Mathematics, markets,
and the work of understanding.

An idea is worth what it lets you see. A mathematical construction can expose a relation hidden in the objects themselves; a model of a market can expose the difference between earning a return and understanding where it came from. My work moves between the two, and in both I care as much about what an explanation leaves open as about how far it reaches.

I trained as a mathematician at Cornell, where my doctoral research focused on the mathematical foundations of superstring theory.

Day to day, I run the systematic investing business at one of the world’s largest asset managers.

I work fast, and I hunt. I build, learn, adjust, and build again. I would rather pay for a mistake than for timidity. What I have learned is where that temperament is safe: in the search for the drum that sounds the same and is not. There the example is its own proof.

I try things. Some of them fail, and I get up. What stands now was built on what fell. Adversity does not get a vote, and custom gets none either: I will not be told what is right, what is beautiful, or what is proper. Mores are made, not inherited, and I make my own. I pursue the beauty that I see. I will not follow the herd, and I will not be cattle. There will be no shepherding committee. The herd goes where the grass is. I go where the scent is.

Truth leaves a scent, and it is seldom where the signposts point. I follow it relentlessly. I do not need anyone's approval. I stopped asking a long time ago. Some of what I write down along the way will turn out to be wrong, and that does not trouble me. It is the cost of doing business, and the business is truth. Nor is any of this for my amusement. Mathematics is not a game, and those who play it as one have not understood what they are holding. It decides what can be kept secret and what can be read, what can be seen and what stays dark, which code holds and which gives way. Nations have stood or fallen on a few pages of it, and will again. I know what I am holding. Mathematics is something to build with, and I want what I build to be standing when the people who come after arrive, and to be of use to them.

I hold no office at any university. My days are spent on Wall Street, which is a street, and I fight on it the way one fights on a street, every day, against everyone there. I am not Faltings or Atiyah, not Kontsevich or Witten. I am Bernd, Bernd Johannes Wuebben, who seeks beauty for the door it opens, and who wants to know how the world is joined and to see it whole while the light lasts. At this point in my life I want that for truth, for beauty, and for what can be done with it. Rank was never on the list. Mathematics and theoretical physics are the deepest things I know how to want; philosophy and psychology stand just behind them. The time is short and the questions are not, and a person walks through one door at a time.

AI is here to stay. Either it replaces us or we hold it the way we hold a calculator, but it will not go away, and I will not pretend otherwise. I am using it to think. It is a new instrument, and I mean to learn every sound it can make, not for standing but for truth. I will not be slowed, shamed, or talked out of it. I will use it ruthlessly, and I will go where the wind carries the scent. One way or another, I will converge to the truth. The route is mine. The limit never was.

Markets and mathematics

A price is a claim about the future that somebody was willing to fund. Most of my work asks what can be known about such claims: whether a model that earns is a model that explains, where compensation for risk ends and compensation for absorbing other people’s constraints begins, and why the part of the cross-section that pays is so often the part nobody can name.

These are one question approached from two sides. Performance and identification are governed by the same covariance spectrum, and they are complementary rather than aligned: a high-Sharpe stochastic discount factor need not identify priced risk, and the alpha tends to live in the statistically unnameable bulk, at a floor set by statistical power rather than by anyone’s skill.

That pushes outward rather than inward — toward who is on the other side of a trade and what they are solving for, toward markets as aggregation mechanisms that sometimes aggregate belief and sometimes only aggregate attention, and toward the possibility that the discretionary–quantitative divide is not a matter of technique but of what a person is willing to accept as warrant for a belief.

Research interests over the years
  • Asset pricing theory — stochastic discount factors and the statistics of high-dimensional return panels; the spectral structure of the cross-section, and whether a high-Sharpe model identifies priced risk or only describes it; the equilibrium level of factor compensation; information-theoretic foundations, from Kelly and Grinold to portfolios as codes; the demand side — inelastic markets, intermediary capital, who is on the other side; and how factors are born, crowd, and decay.
  • AI and the machine economy — institutional cognition as competitive advantage, prediction markets as informational infrastructure, and what disciplined discovery looks like once hypotheses are free and only validation is scarce.
  • Geometric topology — exotic smooth structures on four-manifolds, fundamental groups of Luttinger fillings, and structural approaches to existence questions.
  • Gauge theory, Floer theory, and moduli spaces — instanton knot homology, Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten theory, SO(3)-monopole compactification and gluing, and functoriality in the Atiyah–Floer comparison, including cobordism maps and surface operations; more broadly, the architecture by which equations become compactified moduli spaces and algebraic structures, and when that architecture can be run in reverse.
  • Stability and geometric degeneration — using singular limits to understand the geometry and topology of nearby moduli spaces; canonical Hermitian–Einstein metrics, quiver models and weak spectra; collapsing cycles, integral topology and wall crossing in K3 moduli; and quantitative dynamical Kobayashi–Hitchin theory.
  • Algebraic geometry — mirror symmetry, slope stability of tangent bundles on toric Fano varieties, deformations and smoothability of Calabi–Yau threefolds, curve counting through conifold transitions, and enumerative wall crossing in open FJRW theory.
  • Theoretical physics — M-theory compactifications, five-dimensional supersymmetric fixed points and Higgs branches, Calabi–Yau extremal transitions, quantum gravity, holography, black hole thermodynamics and the information paradox, quantum information, random-matrix theory and free probability, critical phenomena and universality.
  • International relations — how large-scale AI deployment re-centres physical constraint as a source of power, and how delegating decisions to machines erodes deterrence by blurring authorship and intent.
