Black-hole information Developing programme

From entropy curves to the channel behind them.

A Page curve records one number at each moment: the entropy of the radiation a black hole has emitted. Its rise and fall is the signature of unitarity, and reproducing it has become the test a calculation must pass. Three papers ask two questions about the curve. How exactly is the turnover shaped? And how much does the curve, even computed exactly, reveal about whether the information itself comes out?

What is established

The shape of the turn. In the west-coast model, for any broad distribution of horizon areas, the exact planar entropy differs from the diagonal approximation of Marolf, Wang and Wang by −q log q. For a symmetric distribution that is half a bit at the Page point; the correction peaks after it, so the transition is asymmetric. It is the cost of assuming that the radiation's sectors are orthogonal.

Its width. At a charged critical point, in a canonical random-state model, the transition broadens: its width grows as S3/4 rather than S1/2, with a quartic rather than Gaussian profile.

What it cannot see. A channel that keeps its input and one that erases it can emit radiation with identical entropies at every step: 0, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, 0 bits for six qubits. Ensembles with identical averaged spectra can fluctuate differently.

The open problem

In finite dimensions, what would suffice is known: correlations with a reference entangled with the input separate the two channels, and the full Choi state determines a channel completely. The problem is gravitational.

In a specified model of evaporation, which spectral, operator and reference-correlation data does the proposed description fix, and which predictions differ under a common preparation? Answering it requires a recovery or fluctuation calculation inside that model, which none of the papers yet performs.

Why it matters

Reproducing the Page curve is widely read as evidence that information escapes. The exact examples show that the inference needs more than the curve. A gravitational calculation of reference correlations would turn the warning into a test, one that distinguishes recovery of information from processes that only reproduce its entropy.

The third paper arranges the data as entropy, limiting spectrum, connected fluctuations and correlations with a reference. It presents the list as an organization of questions, not a theorem: agreement at one level does not establish agreement at the next.

The tutorial starts from entanglement and Hawking radiation and contributes exposition rather than new results. Readers who know the replica-wormhole literature can begin with the first research paper. Each conclusion holds in the model its paper specifies, and none of them derives the entropy profile of an evaporating gravitational black hole. For nontechnical introductions, read Not Enough, The same law in a black hole and a stock market and Two ways for a theory to fail.
