Categorical Kähler Geometry Integrated programme

Reading stability from a Hermitian metric.

Give each vertex of a directed graph a vector space and each arrow a linear map. The result is a quiver representation. Assign a complex charge to each vertex and a Hermitian metric to each space. The charges determine which subrepresentations can destabilize the whole. Can one recover those subrepresentations just by watching the metrics evolve?

For every nonzero representation of a finite quiver without oriented cycles, the minimizing flow of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit recovers the Harder–Narasimhan filtration from the exponential decay rates of squared lengths. The theorem holds for every initial metric and all vertex charges in the upper half-plane. Passing from representations to complexes introduces exact pieces that contribute to the flow even though they disappear in cohomology. For the two-term Kronecker complexes treated here, their contribution decays exponentially from arbitrary initial metrics.

For bounded complexes of any finite quiver, a closed one-form on metrics has an explicit primitive. When the total charge is nonzero this gives a real potential. It is nonincreasing in specified classes and can increase on a three-term complex. The Categorical Kähler Geometry guide gives the hypotheses and explains how these results connect with the earlier work on mass increase and on harmonic paths.
