Which singularities can a compact threefold smooth?

A node can always be smoothed in a small neighbourhood. On a compact Calabi–Yau threefold, its smoothing parameter has to fit together with the parameters at every other node. A small resolution replaces each node by a rational curve. Friedman’s criterion asks for a homological relation among these curves whose coefficient at every node is nonzero.

The toric examples also have singularities with local obstructions. Deforming the ambient toric pair can produce smoothings, while the classification of isolated mirror pairs provides compact examples on which to test the global condition. Vanishing periods impose the homological relation even along a smoothing arc whose first derivative vanishes.

From a smoothing criterion to the whole deformation space

The mixed smoothing criterion treats nodes together with specified del Pezzo and quadric cones. At a degree-six cone there are two local choices, a line and a plane of deformations. Once a choice is made at every such cone, the partial smoothing theorem determines which original or residual singularities can persist together. On this selected base, a cone whose parameter cannot move to first order stays fixed throughout the family.

Local equations for deformations takes all the local choices together. The theorem assumes a projective Calabi–Yau threefold with nodes and exact degree-five or degree-six del Pezzo cones, and a smooth projective crepant resolution small at the nodes and obtained by blowing up the cone vertices. The resolution gives a linear space of relations among the link classes. Intersecting it with the product of the local deformation spaces, with their defining equations, gives the entire global base up to a smooth factor.

For an arbitrary isolated Gorenstein toric threefold singularity, the polygon determines equations in the cohomology of its link, including nilpotent directions. A change of volume form acts through a common change of height at the edges. The link-period theorem identifies the compact deformation space by imposing linear homology relations on these local equations, up to smooth parameters. In one projective example the relation between two identical links preserves a direction with square nonzero and cube zero.

Two compact examples explain why the equations matter. Each has a smooth six-dimensional reduced base and two extra tangent directions. Write x and y for the two nilpotent coordinates. In both examples their squares vanish. In one, two relations share a hexagonal face and impose the additional equation xy = 0. In the other their supports are disjoint, and xy survives. Testing along a single truncated parameter gives identical answers for the two spaces.

What survives on the mirror?

On a conifold transition, a resolution introduces exceptional curves and a smoothing introduces vanishing three-spheres. The mirror conifold theorem identifies their integral relations for the Batyrev families on the nodal components reached by its construction near the maximally degenerate limit. Extending the comparison to other components, or producing the reverse transition throughout the admissible class, remains open.
