Discovery, construction and organization Proposed agenda

From alpha discovery to evidence-based capital allocation.

Language models have made investment hypotheses free. The evidence that can judge them still arrives one year per year. A portfolio, meanwhile, is built from an expected-return forecast as if the forecast were simply given. Between the two lies a question that none of the three papers answers: how should capital be allocated when the forecasts were themselves selected from a vast, adaptively generated space of hypotheses?

What the papers establish

Discovery. After searching M strategies over Teff years of evidence, certification needs a Sharpe ratio of roughly √(2 ln M / Teff): about 1.9 for ten thousand ideas over five years. The inequality is known; the point is that cheap generation drives M up while Teff grows by one year per year. In two pre-registered experiments, none of 898 machine-generated strategies passed a false-discovery correction, a result the paper reads as a measure of the window, not of the models.

Construction. Three methods bring an arbitrary forecast into hierarchical and regularized portfolio construction. The forecast is taken as given.

Organization. The design for an AI-native firm treats sizing as part of its epistemology: the robustness of the evidence behind a belief, not only its point estimate, should set the position.

The proposed question

Join the three. Derive an expected-return forecast adjusted for the size of the search that produced it and the length of the record that tested it, and carry it into portfolio construction, so that a position reflects how its forecast was found as well as what it says.

Nothing in the papers does this yet. The validation paper's verdict is binary: below the floor, predictability "is not absent. It is merely unfundable." The portfolio paper places no prior on the forecast. The AI-native design proposes fragility-based sizing as a governance principle, without deriving or testing it.

Why it matters

Selection alone makes the best of M worthless strategies look good: its expected Sharpe ratio is near √(2 ln M / T). A portfolio that sizes by point estimates funds that illusion in proportion to its size.

The interesting case is predictability that is real but cannot yet be certified. A rule for funding it in proportion to the evidence would turn the bottleneck from a reason to abstain into a quantity one can manage.
