Geometric topology and Floer theory

Instanton homology of knots

Four papers on the knot version of the Atiyah–Floer program, which compares singular instanton knot homology with Lagrangian Floer homology of immersed curves in the pillowcase. Three of them treat torus knots and are best read in order. The first assembles the instanton side for the (3,n)-torus knots and two-bridge knots. The second determines the instanton differential for every torus knot. The third computes the pillowcase side for the whole (3,n) family and finds that it matches the instanton side in every grading. The fourth treats the (−2,3,q) pretzel knots: building on the third, it proves the same agreement for a decomposition of every knot in the family, and it studies Maurer–Cartan deformations for a second decomposition, where the comparison remains conjectural. Papers, sources and exact computations.

  • Traceless characters and instanton gradings for 2-bridge and (3,n) knots An account of the instanton chain complex of the (3,n)-torus knots (its generators, gradings and differential) together with the traceless character varieties of two-bridge knots. Each result is credited to its source in the work of Lewallen, Klassen, Hedden–Herald–Kirk, Nagasato–Yamaguchi, Daemi–Scaduto and Li–Ye. Comparing the size of the complex with the size of its homology gives the rank of the differential for every torus knot, and settles the (3,n) pattern for all n. A second proof of the homology rank runs through Khovanov homology. The one nonzero differential of the knot 8₁₉ is matched with a bigon in the pillowcase. arXiv:2607.26095 · current version (PDF), 3 October 2026, 14 pages · code
  • The S-complex differential of torus knots For every torus knot, the instanton chain complex built from traceless characters is larger than its homology. The gap is governed by two classical invariants, the signature and the Alexander polynomial. This paper computes the rank of the differential through floor sums and proves a sharp lower bound for it. It classifies exactly which torus knots have no differential at all, which supplies the converse to a criterion of Daemi and Scaduto. It also shows how the differential splits between gradings, and this determines the graded rational instanton homology of every torus knot. A comparison with the pillowcase computations of Hedden, Herald and Kirk leads to a conjecture linking the two complexes at chain level. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 3 October 2026, 12 pages · source
  • Pillowcase Floer homology of the torus knots T(3,n) Hedden, Herald and Kirk conjectured that a Lagrangian Floer homology of immersed curves in the pillowcase recovers singular instanton homology. This paper computes the pillowcase side for every (3,n)-torus knot. For small perturbations in an explicit range, the complex has exactly the generators predicted by the instanton side. Depending on n modulo 6, it has either no differential or exactly one, so its homology agrees with instanton homology in every grading. This confirms the conjecture for an infinite family with nonzero differentials, and it matches the generators and differential of the instanton complex in the paper above. A numerical computation suggests that, without a bounding cochain, the answer for the knot 8₁₉ depends on the chosen decomposition. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 3 October 2026, 40 pages · code
  • Instanton and pillowcase homology of the (−2,3,q) pretzel knots This family of pretzel knots begins with two torus knots and continues with hyperbolic knots. Their instanton homology is computed over the integers, together with its gradings. Regluing the tangle that Hedden, Herald and Kirk use for a torus knot gives a decomposition of every knot in the family, and for it the pillowcase Floer homology is computed by hand: it agrees with instanton homology in every grading and has nonzero differentials, which confirms the conjecture of Hedden, Herald and Kirk for this family and for a family of twisted torus knots. For Smith's decomposition the curves need a bounding cochain; explicit Maurer–Cartan deformations are constructed and compared with the Khovanov invariants of Kotelskiy, Watson and Zibrowius. arXiv:2607.26096 · current version (PDF), 3 October 2026, 59 pages · code

Donaldson theory and the Atiyah–Floer program

Four manuscripts in gauge theory. Three take up the Atiyah–Floer comparisons of Daemi, Fukaya and Lipyanskiy and show how they interact with point operations and with four-dimensional cobordism maps, the latter first with mod-two and then with integer coefficients. The fourth proves a one-bubble neighborhood theorem in SO(3)-monopole moduli spaces, an analytic input to Feehan and Leness’s approach to Witten’s conjecture. PDFs, LaTeX sources, and project repository.

  • Point operations and matching-line bubbles in the Atiyah–Floer comparison Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy’s admissible SO(3) Atiyah–Floer comparison intertwines the surface and loop operations; this paper supplies the point-class calculation. Matching-line bubbling produces a universal genus-dependent scalar correction — nonzero in genera one and two — and thereby gives the full corrected observable-algebra action. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026
  • Cobordism maps and Atiyah–Floer isomorphisms for framed instanton homology I Daemi, Fukaya and Lipyanskiy proved a version of the Atiyah–Floer conjecture: framed instanton homology agrees with the Lagrangian Floer homology of a Heegaard splitting. This paper asks whether the comparison also respects four-dimensional cobordisms, and proves that, with mod-two coefficients, it does: comparison maps of their type carry the Kronheimer–Mrowka cobordism maps to maps defined purely by counting holomorphic curves, for every connected cobordism. The symplectic side thereby becomes a functor in its own right. The proof rests on two new analytic results, a collapse of a compression body next to a holomorphic strip, realizing a degeneration proposed by Salamon and Wehrheim, and an excision theorem along a torus. The sequel on integer coefficients appears below. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), revised 27 September 2026, 148 pages
  • Cobordism maps and Atiyah–Floer isomorphisms for framed instanton homology II The sequel develops the comparison over the integers for the geometric symplectic maps of Paper I, with one overall sign per cobordism. It transports orientations from gauge theory through the mixed equation and compares collapse, torus excision, reflection, and compression cups and caps. The analytic foundations and the shifting of mixed configurations in families are proved in the paper, so the main theorem rests only on the analytic results of Paper I. The gauge orientation convention is explicit; comparison with homology-oriented integral instanton maps and relative-spin quilt orientations remains open. Not yet independently refereed. Scope and source. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 30 September 2026, 86 pages
  • One-bubble neighborhoods in SO(3)-monopole moduli spaces An oriented one-bubble neighborhood theorem supplies the analytic neighborhood input to Feehan and Leness’s level-one link calculation underlying their low-degree comparison for Witten’s conjecture. The theorem treats compact regular Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces of arbitrary dimension whose solutions have nonzero spinor. Strong approximation in critical norms and recovery of the gluing parameters give reverse gluing; comparison with Donaldson’s instanton-addition map identifies the orientation. The low-degree consequence retains abundance, effectiveness, and Seiberg–Witten simple type. Higher bubbling levels and collisions remain necessary further work toward the general conjecture. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 9 September 2026; revised 27 September 2026, 58 pages

Algebraic geometry and mirror symmetry

Tangent bundle stability of toric Fano varieties

Two papers on the slope stability of tangent bundles of smooth toric Fano varieties with respect to the anticanonical polarization: a complete census through dimension six, followed by blowups indexed by trees and toric bundles, constructions that show which geometric interactions decide stability. Papers, code, and data.

  • Slope stability of tangent bundles of smooth toric Fano varieties A Klyachko-criterion sweep with exact certificates over all 8,630 smooth toric Fano varieties in dimensions three through six. Every Kähler–Einstein variety in the census has polystable tangent bundle, but not conversely — 102 of the 109 five-folds with stable tangent bundle are not Kähler–Einstein. arXiv:2608.20411 · current version (PDF) · certificates and data
  • Tangent stability on toric Fano blowups and bundles Two constructions show how geometric interactions govern anticanonical tangent stability. For blowups of products of projective spaces indexed by trees, stability reduces to one divisor-degree inequality per vertex. Every tree of maximum degree three is stable when nonleaf factor dimensions equal their valences and leaf dimensions are arbitrary positive integers. A specified degree-four neighbourhood forces instability, while other degree-four and degree-five arrangements remain stable. In a class of toric bundles allowing arbitrary integral twists, the sharp stable Picard maximum is ⌊5n/4⌋ + 1 for n ≥ 4. Every fixed subcubic tree with an edge yields further bundle maximizers after sufficiently long independent subdivisions. The proofs combine geometric arguments, exact rational computations and uniform estimates. The unrestricted Picard bound remains open. Preprint · current version (PDF), 20 September 2026 · source and exact proof data

Smoothability of Calabi–Yau threefolds

Six papers on smoothings of singular Calabi–Yau threefolds. Papers 1–3 study anticanonical hypersurfaces in Gorenstein toric Fano fourfolds: local singularity types, smoothings induced by deforming the ambient toric pair, and isolated mirror pairs. Paper 4 develops a global obstruction from vanishing periods, Paper 5 proves a necessary and sufficient smoothing criterion for threefolds with nodes and del Pezzo cone points, and Paper 6 proves the mirror counterpart: the vanishing three-spheres of the Batyrev mirror satisfy exactly the relations of the exceptional curves. Database-wide counts are computer-assisted, over a Kreuzer–Skarke input copy verified for reflexivity and absence of repetitions up to lattice equivalence. Papers, code, and data.

  • Non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds from reflexive polytopes Explicit projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with a single nonsmoothable singular point: an anticanonical cone over F₁, or a pentagon cone that deforms but admits no smoothing. The local census classifies 217 isolated Gorenstein toric germs with bounded primitive edge vectors, and a scan of the Kreuzer–Skarke classification finds the local obstruction in 39,175,536 polytopes (approximately 8.27%) — a lower bound for global nonsmoothability. Paper 1 · arXiv:2609.28499 · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide
  • Deformations of toric pairs and the smoothing of Batyrev Calabi–Yau threefolds Trinomial deformations of the ambient toric pair smooth the singular points over a chosen smoothable two-face whenever its dual edge has an interior lattice point, and a one-node example shows that this dual-edge threshold is sharp. Exactly 3,774 of the 30,241 smoothings in the Batyrev–Kreuzer census meet the criterion face by face; the remaining 26,467 need relations among exceptional curves from different faces. Paper 2, research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • Doubly isolated Batyrev mirror pairs and non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds Exactly 590 reflexive four-polytopes have both associated Batyrev hypersurfaces with at most isolated singularities; their two-faces fall into twelve lattice classes. The F₁ cone occurs in a unique mirror pair, with resolution Hodge numbers (20,26) and (26,20): one member is locally nonsmoothable, while every germ of its mirror is smoothable. The self-dual 24-cell is the unique smooth/smooth pair. Paper 3, research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • A vanishing-cycle obstruction to smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds Vanishing periods show that any smoothing with nodes and degree-six or degree-seven del Pezzo cone points forces a nonzero relation among link homology classes on a crepant resolution. The obstruction rules out smoothing the 26-node, four-cone mirror member from Paper 3 even though every one of its singularities is locally smoothable, and it obstructs 76 of the 77 polytopes with at most nine vertices in the stated pentagonal framework. Paper 4, research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • Smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and del Pezzo cone points A necessary and sufficient smoothing criterion for projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and exact anticanonical cone points over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees 5, 6 and 7 and over ℙ¹×ℙ¹: a homological relation must avoid every local discriminant. Selected deformation bases are smooth under a nonzero degree-six projection hypothesis. The examples include a nonsmoothable threefold whose deformation space has four smooth components, and X₁₉, which smooths with a smooth 30-dimensional deformation base. Paper 5, research manuscript · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide
  • Mirror Symmetry for Conifold Relations in Calabi–Yau Threefolds For a class of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces in toric fourfolds, we establish Morrison's numerical prediction for conifold transitions under mirror symmetry, as a count in the coefficient space of the Batyrev mirror family, and strengthen it to an integral statement. For suitable degenerations of the Batyrev mirror family near the maximally degenerate limit, we prove that the exceptional curves of a small resolution and the mirror vanishing three-spheres have the same integral relation lattice, and that the spheres generate a torsion-free subgroup. Every relation is realised by an explicit integral four-chain on the mirror hypersurface, constructed from a relative tropical two-cycle. Near the mirror members constructed, the locus where all the spheres collapse is smooth of codimension equal to the relative Picard number. We characterise smoothability by relations among the mirror spheres and obtain a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror whenever a smoothing exists. The results apply to nodal crepant partial resolutions, induced by projective toric morphisms, of a specified class of anticanonical hypersurfaces with nodes and cones over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees six and seven. The required triangulations are established by computer-assisted verification. Paper 6, research manuscript · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide

Wall crossing and scattering in open FJRW theory

Genus-zero open FJRW theory for the Landau–Ginzburg model xʳ + yˢ, following Gross, Kelly and Tessler: where wall crossing begins, how boundary transport factors by slope, and an annular scattering diagram for the quartic theory. Papers and exact verification code.

  • The central-charge threshold for wall-crossing in two-variable open FJRW theory Primary open FJRW invariants of xʳ + yˢ are independent of canonical boundary conditions precisely when the central charge is less than one — equivalently, for the simple Fermat singularities. The paper classifies the first walls, exhibits a universal one-insertion descendent wall, and identifies each wall generator as a Hamiltonian vector field in a graded subalgebra of the tropical vertex Lie algebra. Paper 1, research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source
  • Boundary-ray factorization and geometric wall-crossing in open FJRW theory of xʳ + yˢ Primary transitions factor uniquely by increasing slope, and descendent transitions factor coefficientwise on finite divisor-closed windows. The paper constructs the two extremal boundary systems, reconstructs every open potential from its initial coefficients, and realizes each finite factorization by Gross–Kelly–Tessler canonical homotopies. For the quartic case, Paper 3 shows that these homotopies can be chosen compatibly as the windows grow. Paper 2, research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source
  • Annular scattering in quartic open FJRW theory Where should the wall-crossing of quartic open FJRW theory live geometrically? The paper shows that its first descendent generators do not fit on the natural disk-shaped base of the Landau–Ginzburg mirror and instead force an annular base with hyperbolic monodromy around its core. On this annulus it builds a consistent scattering diagram whose wall algebra contains the Gross–Kelly–Tessler wall-crossing algebra, and shows that the diagram nevertheless differs from their wall-crossing by a correction that periods cannot see. It also chooses the Gross–Kelly–Tessler boundary-condition homotopies compatibly and transports their primary Frobenius structure. A geometric normalization at the ends of the annulus that would remove the correction is left open. Paper 3, research manuscript · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source

Moduli spaces and geometric analysis

Two papers that approach moduli from opposite directions: how the Dolbeault spectrum degenerates as Hermitian Yang–Mills flow carries a semistable bundle toward its limit, and which prescribed degeneration data compactified moduli spaces can realize.

  • Small Dolbeault eigenvalues along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow As Hermitian Yang–Mills flow carries a semistable bundle toward its graded limit, some eigenvalues of the Dolbeault Laplacian on endomorphisms tend to zero. The paper determines how fast each of them vanishes and with which leading coefficient, for bundles on curves with distinct stable factors and for two-step extensions in any dimension. Under a positivity condition on the weight grading of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit, the eigenvalues that decay at the slowest rate have coefficients fixed by the bundle, one of them always one half, while at faster rates the coefficients remember the metric the flow started from. The paper also shows that the bounded correction in the known flow asymptotics converges, and that a related slope inequality is sharp in every rank. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), September 2026 · paper source and verification code
  • Inverse Moduli Theory: Realizability of Degeneration Data by Compactified Moduli Spaces An inverse problem for Floer and related theories: realizing prescribed gluing data and algebraic identities by compactified moduli spaces, and determining when those realizations arise from geometric Fredholm problems. The principal result gives an obstruction to extending prescribed compatible stabilizing bundles across the pentagon associahedron: a reflected endpoint identification creates a nonorientable boundary bundle, while doubling the datum removes the obstruction. Chain and higher associativity identities become oriented filling conditions in dimension one, with higher extensions governed by smooth bordism. The analytic realization criteria remain conditional. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · paper source and finite calculations

Exotic four-manifolds

Exotic smooth structures on S²×S² and ℂP²#ℂ̄P², approached through Lidman and Piccirillo’s slicing-distinguished pair of four-manifolds. The current proof is incomplete; see the author’s note.

  • An exotic S²×S² and an exotic ℂP²#ℂ̄P² Upgrading Lidman–Piccirillo’s slicing-distinguished four-manifold pair from equal cohomology rings to homeomorphic is equivalent to producing an exotic S²×S². arXiv:2608.17267 · current version (PDF) · scripts and certificates Author's note: Issues have been identified in the geometric derivation of the fundamental-group relations in Part II, so the claimed simple connectivity and its exotic-manifold consequences are not established by the current proof. The existence of an exotic S²×S² is a major open problem in four-dimensional topology: it asks whether one of the simplest four-dimensional spaces admits a different smooth structure. The problem has resisted decades of work. Earlier claimed constructions include that of Akhmedov and Park (arXiv:1005.3346, 2010), which has not provided an established resolution. The present paper is another attempt at this difficult problem, and its current shortcomings are acknowledged here explicitly. The author remains committed to pursuing the problem and is currently working on a repair.

Theoretical physics

Compact Higgs transitions

Four papers on how local Higgs branches change when their singular regions belong to one compact Calabi–Yau threefold. In M-theory, vector fields of the compactification can gauge flavor symmetries shared by several local sectors, so Higgs directions that exist in separate local models can be obstructed or become available only through a joint transition. Paper 1 develops the general gauging framework, Papers 2 and 3 study an explicit compact transition through its quantum couplings and its magnetic sheaves, and Paper 4 treats the Higgs branch at finite Planck mass. Papers, sources, and verification code.

  • Global Constraints on Higgsing Localized Five-Dimensional Sectors in Compact M-Theory Vacua Which local Higgs directions survive when their singularities belong to one compact Calabi–Yau threefold? Wrapped M2-brane charges and compact divisor pairings determine a shared Abelian gauging matrix for conifold and E₁–E₄ sectors: degrees 8 (ℙ¹×ℙ¹), 7, 6 and 5. Its branchwise kernel is the invariant-coordinate image of the rigid moment-map zero locus and, under the stated exact-germ and projective crepant-resolution hypotheses, the global first-order deformation image. Combined with the mixed smoothing theorem, this gives an exact geometric existence criterion: the kernel must contain a vector outside every local discriminant. At E₄, an integral identification with the SU(5) diagonal coordinates gives five separate nonzero conic-pencil conditions. Degrees 1–4 remain outside this mixed comparison. Selected deformation bases are smooth when the relation space projects nontrivially to each selected E₃ summand. Examples distinguish cooperative Higgsing in X₉, sectors forced to remain undeformed, and nonzero E₃ deformations forced to retain a singularity. The finite-Planck scalar metric is not determined. Paper 1 · current version (PDF), revised 7 September 2026 · source and guide
  • Bulk Gauging and Quantum Couplings across a Compact Higgs Transition In X₉, two conifold sectors and one E₂ sector can Higgs only together, through an explicit compact transition from Hodge numbers (6,122) to (3,123). Existence also follows from the exact mixed smoothing criterion; the explicit construction identifies the smooth phase needed for the charge and quantum calculations. The paper determines the primitive surviving charge lattice and cubic couplings, and proves that the shared flavor gauging becomes non-dynamical in every gravity-decoupling limit with bounded local Kähler scales. At finite auxiliary-circle radius, four conifold states span a primitive rank-three electric lattice. Laurent mutation and continuation of all eight regular periods identify the surviving genus-zero prepotential with that of the smooth phase on the marked branch, including its integral normalization and constant term. The four-state description is not uniform along the decompactification limit, which ends where the del Pezzo surface collapses; on an adjacent face of the nef cone the same states are massless five-dimensional hypermultiplets (Paper 4). Paper 2 · current version (PDF), revised 23 September 2026 · source and guide
  • Magnetic Sheaves and Stability Walls in a Compact Calabi–Yau Transition Magnetic objects probe the same compact transition through sheaf counts and stability constraints. Fixed-support Hilbert schemes on the rigid F₁ and ℙ² surfaces give rank-one numerical Donaldson–Thomas contributions; two isolated sheaves on a reducible moving divisor each contribute +1 and remain Gieseker stable along ample polarizations approaching the contraction. Their vanishing deformation and obstruction groups also imply local persistence, with the same isolated contributions, in smooth polarized deformations of the resolved threefold. This does not transport the sheaves through the singular transition. The continued periods of Paper 2 constrain constituent phases and light electric-particle emission near the marked transition, while the probe charges carry confined magnetic flux after Higgsing. The physical BPS spectrum at the endpoint remains undetermined. Paper 3, supporting technical note · current version (PDF), revised 6 September 2026 · source and guide
  • The Higgs Branch of a Compact Calabi–Yau Transition at Finite Planck Mass When a compact Calabi–Yau threefold degenerates, light states of M-theory can condense along a Higgs branch. Without gravity its local structure is a hyperkähler quotient; with gravity the geometry becomes quaternionic Kähler, and its metric is unknown. The paper shows that the local structure can be determined anyway. Quaternionic Kähler geometry has no Fayet–Iliopoulos parameter, and a new local model for quaternionic Kähler quotients near fixed points shows that gravity neither removes nor changes the orbifold singularity where the branch begins; it corrects only the metric, at second order in the distance. The result covers a whole class of transitions, including the cooperative X₉ transition of Paper 2, and a census of toric Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces finds thousands of instances. The physical conclusions rest on a stated regularity hypothesis. Paper 4 · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide

Black hole information

Four papers on black hole information recovery. Two test the Page curve quantitatively, against the exact replica spectrum and across the phase diagrams of AdS black holes; the third shows that reproducing a Page curve does not identify the microscopic resolution of the information paradox; the fourth separates the informational completion that replica wormholes provide from geometric completion.

  • The Page Curve Through the Transition: Testing the Diagonal Approximation Against the Exact Replica Spectrum Both sides of the diagonal approximation are computable in the west-coast model, so this computes them. It reproduces the shape of the broadened crossover to about one per cent of the curve’s amplitude but overstates the entropy deficit at the transition by roughly ten per cent, and summing all non-crossing permutations rather than the two dominant saddles yields a Marchenko–Pastur law for the radiation spectrum. SSRN, March 2026
  • The Page Curve in Higher Dimensions: Phase Transitions, Critical Phenomena, and Universal Broadening The width of the Page transition is set by the square root of the heat capacity — a result derived away from criticality, which an AdS black hole violates, since it has a phase diagram. The width proves independent of spacetime dimension for large Schwarzschild–AdS, is parametrically enhanced near the Hawking–Page transition, and diverges with a different exponent at the Reissner–Nordström critical point — so the broadening discriminates between the two transitions. SSRN, March 2026
  • The Page Curve is not Enough: Universality, Ensemble Dependence, and Microscopic Ambiguity in Black Hole Information Recovery Reproducing a Page curve does not identify the microscopic resolution of the information paradox. A four-level universality hierarchy separates what is forced from what is diagnostic: any two scrambling dynamics forming approximate unitary designs with matching thermodynamics agree on everything through level three, so fuzzballs, islands, random unitary evolution, state-dependent interiors and ER=EPR are indistinguishable there and diverge only at the fine-grained fourth level. SSRN, April 2026
  • Informational Singularities and the Completion of Gravity: Replica Wormholes, Indeterminism, and Cosmic Censorship Replica wormholes restore a coherent map from initial to final states without removing any curvature singularity — informational completion without geometric completion. Making that distinction precise yields the notion of an informational singularity, logically independent of the geometric one and coming apart from it in both directions, and motivates recasting strong cosmic censorship as a claim about coherent state maps rather than about Cauchy horizons. SSRN, August 2026

AI and the machine economy

  • Prediction Markets as the Informational Substrate of the Machine Economy The three institutional gaps blocking autonomous machine-to-machine commerce — adjudication, reputation, and liability — share one root: there is no market price for agent performance risk, deliverable quality, or failure correlation. Five prediction-market mechanisms are designed to supply those signals, with the instrument delimited as carefully as it is developed: an informational deficit can be priced, but a primitive needing an agreed vocabulary rather than a resolving fact needs coordination instead. SSRN, April 2026
  • The Institutional Plumbing for a Machine Economy: A Readiness Assessment of Building Blocks, Missing Infrastructure, and Commercial Opportunities Software agents can now pay, but paying is not economic agency: open exchange between agents with no prior relationship also needs identity, delegated authority, bonding, verification, adjudication, settlement, portable reputation, and somewhere for residual loss to land. Auditing that stack against what actually shipped through 2026 — where roughly two-thirds of settlements moved inside linked clusters and a fifth were fictitious — gives the conclusion that machine commerce expands along a verification-and-risk frontier, not a payment frontier. SSRN, March 2026

International relations and politics

  • Material Deployment Power in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: How Physical Constraints Reshape International Order Prevailing accounts treat AI as an informational technology; deployment at scale instead re-centres physical constraint, since it depends on energy, transmission, cooling, and critical materials that expand slowly and unevenly. The paper defines material deployment power — the capacity to mobilize those physical systems under rivalry — and shows it shapes trade policy and alliance behaviour independently of innovation leadership. SSRN, April 2026
  • Algorithmic Plausible Deniability: The Structural Erosion of Deterrence in the Age of Artificial Intelligence Delegating decisions to machines generates persistent uncertainty over authorship and intent, weakening deterrence even between rational states that prefer stability; unlike strategic ambiguity it is not chosen, but emerges from the delegation itself. The effects are asymmetric — hostile acts become more deniable while signals of restraint become less credible, which undermines escalation control. SSRN, April 2026
