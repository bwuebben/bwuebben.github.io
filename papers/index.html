Knots and the pillowcase

  • Traceless characters and instanton gradings for 2-bridge and (3,n) knots An account of the instanton chain complex of the (3,n)-torus knots (its generators, gradings and differential) together with the traceless character varieties of two-bridge knots. Each result is credited to its source in the work of Lewallen, Klassen, Hedden–Herald–Kirk, Nagasato–Yamaguchi, Daemi–Scaduto and Li–Ye. Comparing the size of the complex with the size of its homology gives the rank of the differential for every torus knot, and settles the (3,n) pattern for all n. A second proof of the homology rank runs through Khovanov homology. The one nonzero differential of the knot 8₁₉ is matched with a bigon in the pillowcase. arXiv:2607.26095 · current version (PDF), 6 October 2026, 15 pages · code
  • The S-complex differential of torus knots For every torus knot, the instanton chain complex built from traceless characters is larger than its homology. The gap is governed by two classical invariants, the signature and the Alexander polynomial. This paper computes the rank of the differential through floor sums and proves a sharp lower bound for it. It classifies exactly which torus knots have no differential at all, which supplies the converse to a criterion of Daemi and Scaduto. It also shows how the differential splits between gradings, and this determines the graded rational instanton homology of every torus knot. A comparison with the pillowcase computations of Hedden, Herald and Kirk leads to a conjecture linking the two complexes at chain level, which the next paper proves for the (3,n) family. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 6 October 2026, 12 pages · source · Zenodo:23643177
  • Pillowcase Floer homology of the torus knots T(3,n) Hedden, Herald and Kirk conjectured that a Lagrangian Floer homology of immersed curves in the pillowcase recovers singular instanton homology. This paper computes the pillowcase side for every (3,n)-torus knot. For small perturbations in an explicit range, the complex has exactly the generators predicted by the instanton side. Depending on n modulo 6, it has either no differential or exactly one, so its homology agrees with instanton homology in every grading. This confirms the conjecture for an infinite family with nonzero differentials, and, combined with a result of Daemi and Scaduto, it shows that the pillowcase complex is chain equivalent to the instanton complex, proving the conjecture of the paper above for this family. A numerical computation suggests that, without a bounding cochain, the answer for the knot 8₁₉ depends on the chosen decomposition. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 6 October 2026, 40 pages · code · Zenodo:23643181
  • Instanton and pillowcase homology of the (−2,3,q) pretzel knots Hedden, Herald and Kirk conjectured that a Lagrangian Floer homology of immersed curves in the pillowcase recovers singular instanton homology. This paper proves the conjecture for the hyperbolic pretzel knots P(−2,3,q). Regluing the tangle that Hedden, Herald and Kirk use for a torus knot gives a decomposition of every knot in the family; the regluing acts on the pillowcase by a shear, and the resulting Floer complex is computed by hand. It agrees with instanton homology in every grading and has nonzero differentials. The same method treats a family of twisted torus knots, and the instanton homology of the whole pretzel family is computed over the integers. arXiv:2607.26096 · current version (PDF), 6 October 2026, 37 pages · code
  • Maurer–Cartan elements in the pillowcase and the Bar-Natan invariant for the (−2,3,q) pretzel knots For a second decomposition of the same pretzel knots, due to Smith, the pillowcase curves give the wrong answer unless they carry bounding cochains, as a conjecture of Cazassus, Herald, Kirk and Kotelskiy predicts. Working in the algebraic model of Kotelskiy, Watson and Zibrowius, the paper constructs explicit Maurer–Cartan deformations, each a smoothing of the curve at a single self-intersection, that give the correct answer for the knots P(−2,3,5) and P(−2,3,7), and a second closure of the tangle selects one of them, conditionally on the conjecture and a hypothesis on the support of the cochain. The selected object differs by a single term from the Bar-Natan invariant of the tangle, and rational closures cannot tell the two apart. The paper asks whether the object the conjecture assigns to the tangle is its Bar-Natan invariant. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 6 October 2026, 33 pages · source and code · Zenodo:23643150

Atiyah–Floer functoriality

  • Cobordism maps and Atiyah–Floer isomorphisms for framed instanton homology I Daemi, Fukaya and Lipyanskiy proved a version of the Atiyah–Floer conjecture: framed instanton homology agrees with the Lagrangian Floer homology of a Heegaard splitting. This paper asks whether the comparison also respects four-dimensional cobordisms, and proves that, with mod-two coefficients, it does: comparison maps of their type carry the Kronheimer–Mrowka cobordism maps to maps defined purely by counting holomorphic curves, for every connected cobordism. The symplectic side thereby becomes a functor in its own right. The proof rests on two new analytic results, a collapse of a compression body next to a holomorphic strip, realizing a degeneration proposed by Salamon and Wehrheim, and an excision theorem along a torus. The sequel on integer coefficients appears below. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), original 25 September 2026; this version 3 November 2026, 156 pages · Atiyah–Floer Cobordism Explorer · arXiv:2611.10002
  • Cobordism maps and Atiyah–Floer isomorphisms for framed instanton homology II The sequel proves the comparison over the integers. The mixed isomorphisms of Paper I carry the integral instanton cobordism maps, oriented by almost-complex structures, to signed counts of holomorphic curves, up to one overall sign for each cobordism, so the symplectic maps form a projective functor on homology. Orientations are transported from gauge theory through the mixed equation. The new input is that collapsing a compression body and excising along a torus preserve orientations, and that the sign of attaching a sphere to a strip is stable under small perturbations and under reversing the splitting. Paper III below identifies the usual singular Hopf-link maps and the calibrated DFL count; the nonsingular cap and prescribed relative-spin comparisons remain open. Not yet independently refereed. Scope and source. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), original 30 September 2026; this version 9 November 2026, 110 pages · Zenodo:24097426
  • Cobordism maps and Atiyah–Floer isomorphisms for framed instanton homology III Integral chain homotopy equivalences identify the usual singular Hopf-link framed instanton maps with the gauge-calibrated DFL mixed count and the transported symplectic maps, up to one overall sign and an explicit grading conversion. For every closed connected oriented three-manifold, surgery generation turns an algebraic orientation normalization into finite integer combinations of products of geometric counts; a polynomial in forward and reflected mixed counts transfers it to the symplectic side. The proof retains the companion analytic results and the stated ordinary surgery and oriented gluing inputs. The nonsingular cap and prescribed relative-spin comparisons remain open. Research preprint · current version (PDF), 26 October 2026; revised 30 October 2026, 32 pages · scope and source · Zenodo:24097427
  • Point operations and matching-line bubbles in the Atiyah–Floer comparison Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy’s admissible SO(3) Atiyah–Floer comparison intertwines the surface and loop operations; this paper supplies the point-class calculation. Matching-line bubbling produces a universal genus-dependent scalar correction — nonzero in genera one and two — and thereby gives the full corrected observable-algebra action. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), August 2026 · Zenodo:23643185

Four-manifolds

  • One-bubble neighborhoods in SO(3)-monopole moduli spaces An oriented one-bubble neighborhood theorem supplies the analytic neighborhood input to Feehan and Leness’s level-one link calculation underlying their low-degree comparison for Witten’s conjecture. The theorem treats compact regular Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces of arbitrary dimension whose solutions have nonzero spinor. Strong approximation in critical norms and recovery of the gluing parameters give reverse gluing; comparison with Donaldson’s instanton-addition map identifies the orientation. The low-degree consequence retains abundance, effectiveness, and Seiberg–Witten simple type. Higher bubbling levels and collisions remain necessary further work toward the general conjecture. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 9 September 2026; revised 27 September 2026, 58 pages · Zenodo:23643193
  • Toward an exotic S²×S² and an exotic ℂP²#ℂP̄² For a specified Luttinger filling, the paper derives the complete fundamental-group presentation and expresses the group as a one-relator quotient of an iterated HNN extension containing the genus-two surface group. It also determines the universal central extension with cyclic kernel, constructs geometric realizations, and extends the boundary involution over the filling. The working revision adds a proposed Lagrangian-torus capacity obstruction, exact tori detecting a transfer kernel, and a conditional symplectic-homology argument; these geometric additions await independent mathematical verification. A simply connected comparison explains their limitation, and a group-algebra criterion isolates a further nonvanishing problem. The final question is whether the filling relation kills a specified fiber commutator; it remains open, and the exotic homeomorphism conclusions are conditional. Partial-results paper · 56-page working revision · arXiv:2608.17267 · latest version (PDF), October 21, 2026 · paper source and exact algebra verification

Smoothability and mirror relations

  • Non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds from reflexive polytopes Explicit projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with a single nonsmoothable singular point: an anticanonical cone over F₁, or a pentagon cone that deforms but admits no smoothing. The local census classifies 217 isolated Gorenstein toric germs with bounded primitive edge vectors, and a scan of the Kreuzer–Skarke classification finds the local obstruction in 39,175,536 polytopes (approximately 8.27%) — a lower bound for global nonsmoothability. Paper 1 · arXiv:2609.28499 · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide
  • Deformations of toric pairs and the smoothing of Batyrev Calabi–Yau threefolds Trinomial deformations of the ambient toric pair smooth the singular points over a chosen smoothable two-face whenever its dual edge has an interior lattice point, and a one-node example shows that this dual-edge threshold is sharp. Exactly 3,774 of the 30,241 smoothings in the Batyrev–Kreuzer census meet the criterion face by face; the remaining 26,467 need relations among exceptional curves from different faces. Paper 2 · arXiv:2610.06884 · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • Doubly isolated Batyrev mirror pairs and non-smoothable Calabi–Yau threefolds Exactly 590 reflexive four-polytopes have both associated Batyrev hypersurfaces with at most isolated singularities; their two-faces fall into twelve lattice classes. The F₁ cone occurs in a unique mirror pair, with resolution Hodge numbers (20,26) and (26,20): one member is locally nonsmoothable, while every germ of its mirror is smoothable. The self-dual 24-cell is the unique smooth/smooth pair. Paper 3 · arXiv:2610.03753 · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • A vanishing-cycle obstruction to smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds Any smoothing of a projective Calabi–Yau threefold with nodes and degree-six or degree-seven del Pezzo cone points forces a relation among link classes on a crepant resolution that is small over the nodes and blows up the cone vertices. The relation is nonzero at every node and degree-seven point, and at each degree-six point smoothed through the one-dimensional component. The vanishing-period argument applies to smoothing arcs of arbitrary contact order. It rules out a smoothing over a formal disc of the 26-node, four-cone mirror member from Paper 3, although every singularity is locally smoothable; on its mirror partner, two nodes cannot be smoothed by any analytic deformation. The obstruction also excludes 76 of the 77 polytopes with at most nine vertices in the stated pentagonal framework. Paper 4 · arXiv:2610.35911 · current version (PDF), August 2026 · source and guide
  • Smoothing Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and del Pezzo cone points A necessary and sufficient smoothing criterion for projective Calabi–Yau threefolds with nodes and exact anticanonical cone points over del Pezzo surfaces of degrees 5, 6 and 7 and over ℙ¹×ℙ¹: a homological relation must avoid every local discriminant. Selected deformation bases are smooth under a nonzero degree-six projection hypothesis. A nonsmoothable example has a reduced deformation space with four smooth components, showing why a relation nonzero at every singular point is insufficient. X₁₉ is smoothable and has a smooth 30-dimensional deformation base, although no proper ambient family in the paper’s class of single-degree Minkowski decompositions induces a smoothing. Paper 5 · arXiv:2610.35915 · current version (PDF), September 2026 · source and guide
  • Mirror Symmetry for Conifold Relations in Calabi–Yau Threefolds For a class of Batyrev mirror families, we establish Morrison's predicted codimension for nodal degenerations and strengthen it to an equality of integral relation lattices. The result holds on every connected component of the nodal coefficient locus meeting our construction near the maximally degenerate limit. After marking and orienting the nodes, the relations among exceptional curves of the original small resolution coincide with those among mirror vanishing three-spheres, which generate a torsion-free subgroup. Each such component is smooth of codimension equal to the relative Picard number. We realise every relation near the limit by an integral four-chain lifted from a tropical two-cycle relative to the discriminant on Gross's degeneration base. The comparison characterises smoothability of the nodal partial resolution and, when it is smoothable, yields a symplectic conifold transition on the mirror. We treat specified projective toric partial resolutions of hypersurfaces with nodes and degree-six or degree-seven del Pezzo cone points. In an explicit four-node family, every member of the unique codimension-three nodal component admits a projective small resolution. For general members, these resolutions are birational to Batyrev mirrors of smoothings of the original nodal threefold, giving the reversed transition predicted by Morrison in this family. Paper 6, research manuscript · current version (PDF), 29 September 2026; revised 2 November 2026, 234 pages · source and guide · Zenodo:23904357

Universal curve counting

  • Universal curve counting under conifold transitions A rational universal specialization theorem for projective conifold transitions with smooth algebraic total space and torsion-free second homology at both endpoints. A geometric support bound makes each bounded-degree sum finite. In a compact four-node transition, all 37 candidate contributions over a primitive line class are computed: eight are nonzero and their sum agrees with an independent stable-pair calculation. The smoothing coefficient in twice the line class is also determined. Integrality transfer is conditional on the resolution. Paper 1, preprint · current version (PDF), 28 October 2026, 18 pages · sources and exact calculations · Zenodo:23750304
  • A counterexample to integral product exponents in universal curve counting A compact smooth Calabi–Yau threefold has a genus-ten scalar local-curve product exponent at three times a primitive class with fractional part 2/3. An exact degree-three Frobenius-algebra calculation gives a divisibility obstruction, and a stable-pair moduli space isomorphic to projective thirteen-space has virtual count −14, violating it. Integrality holds in classes of divisibility at most two. The integer elementary expansion is an input to the counterexample. Paper 2, preprint · current version (PDF), 28 October 2026, 13 pages · sources and exact calculations · Zenodo:23750305

Stability and Geometric Degeneration

  • Slope stability of tangent bundles of smooth toric Fano varieties A Klyachko-criterion sweep with exact certificates over all 8,630 smooth toric Fano varieties in dimensions three through six. Every Kähler–Einstein variety in the census has polystable tangent bundle, but not conversely — 102 of the 109 five-folds with stable tangent bundle are not Kähler–Einstein. arXiv:2608.20411 · current version (PDF) · certificates and data
  • Tangent stability on toric Fano blowups and bundles For the specified invariant Fano blowups of products of projective spaces indexed by trees, anticanonical tangent stability reduces to one divisor-degree inequality per vertex. Smooth toric Fano bundles with degree-six del Pezzo-product fibres attain the sharp stable Picard maximum ⌊5n/4⌋ + 1 in this bundle class. A new exact census certifies all 6,281 Picard-number-ten eightfolds: 64 stable, 692 strictly semistable and 5,525 unstable; every strictly semistable fan is a nontrivial product. The unrestricted Picard bound remains open. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 20 September 2026; this version 29 October 2026, 75 pages · source and exact proof data · Zenodo:23774632
  • Local stability chambers for toric tangent bundles The number of local stability-chamber boundaries for toric tangent bundles is unbounded. This paper proves the anticanonical stability trichotomy for projective-line bundles over products of lines with positive and negative unit twists, and determines the local chambers of the strictly semistable family. A geometric criterion also gives nearby stable polarizations for all 31 indecomposable strictly semistable examples in the existing census in dimensions three through six. Their chambers have one, two, or three independent boundaries. The paper identifies locally free, pairwise nonisomorphic stable factors for both the family and these finite examples. Stability and Geometric Degeneration continues the programme with nonsimplicial chambers, spectra and flows. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), 26 October 2026; revised 27 October 2026, 17 pages · source and exact computations · Zenodo:23682140
  • Small Dolbeault eigenvalues near stability walls For an extension of several stable quotient factors by one stable subbundle, with all factors pairwise nonisomorphic and of equal limiting slope and every extension component nonzero, ranks and slope gaps determine the leading small spectrum of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphisms at the Hermitian–Einstein metric. This paper gives an explicit matrix for every small eigenvalue, with relative error uniform even when the positive gaps vanish at arbitrarily different rates. For two factors it determines the second-order correction and, after rescaling and unitary identification with the graded limit, the first eigensection through first order. Toric tangent bundles supply arbitrarily many small eigenvalues with independently prescribed orders of vanishing. The metric-response and HYM-flow paper develops the corresponding dynamical theory in its stated two-step setting. Preprint · current version (PDF), 27 October 2026; abstract revised 27 October 2026, 13 pages · source and matrix computations · Zenodo:23717764
  • Small Dolbeault eigenvalues along Hermitian Yang–Mills flow As Hermitian Yang–Mills flow carries a semistable bundle toward its graded limit, some eigenvalues of the Dolbeault Laplacian on endomorphisms tend to zero. The paper determines how fast each of them vanishes and with which leading coefficient, for bundles on curves with distinct stable factors and for two-step extensions in any dimension. Under a positivity condition on the weight grading of Haiden, Katzarkov, Kontsevich and Pandit, the eigenvalues that decay at the slowest rate have coefficients fixed by the bundle, one of them always one half, while at faster rates the coefficients remember the metric the flow started from. The paper also shows that the bounded correction in the known flow asymptotics converges, and that a related slope inequality is sharp in every rank. Research manuscript · current version (PDF), September 2026 · paper source and verification code · Zenodo:23643223
  • Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra for bipartite two-step extensions Approaching intersecting stability walls can produce eigenvalues on widely separated scales. For fixed two-step extensions with distinct stable factors of equal limiting slope and a connected extension graph, this paper determines every small eigenvalue of the Dolbeault Laplacian on trace-free endomorphisms at a Hermitian–Einstein metric from a weighted graph Laplacian, with uniform relative error. It also gives a relative metric approximation and a spanning-tree expression for the leading vanishing factor of the zeta determinant. Ranks and slopes suffice on trees; cycles can retain dependence on the harmonic extension data. Preprint · current version (PDF), 27 October 2026; abstract revised 27 October 2026, 22 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23717660
  • Uniform Hermitian–Einstein spectra with repeated stable factors For simple locally free two-step extensions with repeated stable factors within the layers, every small Hermitian–Einstein endomorphism eigenvalue is relatively asymptotic to an independently balanced matrix model, uniformly at arbitrarily separated positive scales. A projection estimate and degree argument compare the actual metric with the balanced metric without inverting the smallest finite Hessian eigenvalue. A fixed rank-five bundle on a product of four projective lines realizes six small modes. Preprint · current version (PDF), 29 October 2026, 18 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23780241
  • Nonsimplicial local stability chambers of toric tangent bundles Smooth weak-Fano toric tangent bundles realize nonsimplicial stability chambers with every centered slope direction accessible. A general local normal form describes nearby Harder–Narasimhan filtrations without assuming the equal-slope subsheaves are locally free. Positive spanning gives full slope access, including a surface with a non-locally-free quotient. A positive-twist Bott family obstructs equal slopes for its natural filtration, while the fourfold example separates tangent walls from toric chambers and yields an eight-node Calabi–Yau transition from Hodge numbers (4, 60) to (2, 66). Preprint · current version (PDF), original 27 October 2026; this version 29 October 2026, 24 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23774770
  • Metric response and Hermitian Yang–Mills flow for two-step extensions For fixed two-step extensions with pairwise nonisomorphic stable factors of equal limiting slope and a connected extension graph, this paper quantifies the response of Hermitian–Einstein metrics to changes of polarization. When the centered slopes give full local parameters, the singular values of the trace-free part of H⁻¹dH, for the Hermitian–Einstein metric H, are asymptotic to reciprocal graph eigenvalues in rank-weighted slope and normalized L² norms. A finite-dimensional invariant manifold gives late-time metric comparison up to a constant scalar and relative spectral comparison for Hermitian Yang–Mills flow at nearby stable, semistable and unstable parameters, with the comparison time chosen before the parameter neighborhood. An application connects chamber geometry, metric response and flow on the same weak Fano toric tangent bundles. Preprint · current version (PDF), 27 October 2026; abstract revised 27 October 2026, 27 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23717742
  • Hermitian–Einstein metrics and small spectra for three-step extensions For canonical three-step extensions of distinct equal-slope line bundles, diagonal balancing controls the actual Hermitian–Einstein metric and every small endomorphism eigenvalue with relative accuracy uniform in positive scale ratios. A family on the product of three projective lines realizes an additional weak scale and quantitative intrinsic polarization response. Preprint · current version (PDF), 31 October 2026, 28 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23850990
  • Relative metric estimates near reducible Yang–Mills connections Near a central Yang–Mills connection on a closed surface, the classical quadratic model approximates the actual gauge quotient metric and every small gauge eigenvalue with relative error quadratic in the deformation parameter, uniformly across changing stabilizers and arbitrarily separated orbit scales. The paper computes the leading metric correction and gives explicit quaternionic projective and hyperbolic comparisons, including a common weighted four-cycle. Preprint · current version (PDF), 28 October 2026, 14 pages · source and exact finite calculations · Zenodo:23740619
  • L² metrics on Hermitian–Yang–Mills moduli spaces near a stability wall The full obstructed local quotient near a four-line wall on the product of three projective lines carries a harmonic L² metric uniformly comparable to its finite coefficient quotient metric. The comparison persists through loss of orbit and obstruction rank; quantitative changes of chart yield intrinsic distance estimates in a selected family. Preprint · current version (PDF), 31 October 2026, 34 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23850992
  • Hermitian–Einstein metrics and moduli of three-step extensions with repeated factors A rank-five three-step deformation problem with a repeated line factor admits actual Hermitian–Einstein metrics and relative control of six small matrix modes without a lower bound on their positive ratios. The same model gives relative harmonic quotient metrics and an isometric identification along a specified reducible locus. Preprint · current version (PDF), 31 October 2026, 29 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:23850994
  • Metric wall crossing in rank-three K3 bundle moduli Actual harmonic L² metrics on a complex twelve-dimensional rank-three K3 bundle-moduli neighborhood have a uniform quadratic relative comparison with the cotangent-Grassmannian quotient metrics. The estimate persists through stabilizer changes and independently collapsing scales, giving exceptional-fibre collapse, complete rescaled limits and intrinsic distance asymptotics. The result is local and concerns locally free bundles. Preprint · current version (PDF), 6 November 2026, 21 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:24021822
  • Quantitative metric wall crossing for K3 bundle moduli A general local comparison identifies actual harmonic L² metrics with quiver quotient metrics near polystable bundles on projective K3 surfaces, under the stated stability and Ricci-flat background hypotheses. The estimates remain uniform through changing stabilizers and separated positive scales. A rank-five, two-parameter family realizes five walls with selected transverse cotangent-projective contractions; the paper proves intrinsic distance asymptotics and complete rescaled limits under explicit convergence conditions. Preprint · current version (PDF), 6 November 2026, 30 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:24024998
  • Exceptional cycles and harmonic metrics in K3 bundle moduli Compact K3 bundle-moduli fourfolds connect interacting exceptional planes to primitive integral classes, a boundary attachment modulo eight, and the actual harmonic metric. A moving projective line specializes with multiplicity one to the sum of two plane lines. Exact areas and volumes, ambient diameter estimates through separated scales, and complete rescaled limits describe the same cycles; the chamber metrics converge to the completed central harmonic metric. Preprint · current version (PDF), 7 November 2026; revised 7 November 2026, 31 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:24047888
  • Integral reconstruction and quantitative metric degeneration of K3 bundle moduli A compact rank-seven K3 bundle-moduli eightfold realizes moving projective lines and a deepest Grassmannian fibre Gr(2,4), with both middle Schubert classes extending integrally and an explicit attachment modulo six. Exact harmonic periods, volumes and ambient diameter asymptotics accompany a general quantitative comparison theorem for compact K3 contractions without bubbling. Additive reconstruction retains cochains on all overlaps, monodromy and a marked complement ring. Preprint · current version (PDF), 7 November 2026; revised 7 November 2026, 27 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:24047889
  • Integral attachments and wall correspondences for K3 bundle moduli Integral complement rings, boundary restrictions and adjacent wall correspondences are calculated for a compact six-chamber family of rank-three K3 bundle moduli. Explicit Hilbert-square transport determines universal-index classes and the boundary functional modulo eight; four corrected Mukai flops and two divisorial isomorphisms give the cohomological wall actions. The same cycles have exact periods and volumes in the established harmonic metric degeneration. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 8 November 2026; this version 8 November 2026, 24 pages · source and reproducible calculations · Zenodo:24054004
  • Canonical metrics on singular moduli of K3 bundles The harmonic L² metric on a class of compact twice-primitive K3 bundle moduli spaces is the canonical singular Ricci-flat metric in an ample determinant class. Holomorphic Ext-quiver charts give relative O(r²) approximation by the hyperkähler tangent cones, uniformly through stabilizer changes within each fixed chart. These spaces have positive-dimensional singular strata, no symplectic resolution and no global smoothing; their regular-locus completion is the original moduli space. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 8 November 2026; this version 8 November 2026, 17 pages · LaTeX source · Zenodo:24060228
  • Integral topology and small eigenvalues in Calabi–Yau conifold transitions For fixed polarized Calabi–Yau conifold smoothings with a simply connected projective small resolution, first-order smoothing at every node and approach along a fixed ray, exceptional periods and global intersections determine the complete leading small positive two-form spectrum of the actual Ricci-flat metrics. The coefficient form uses the full quotient harmonic norm. Compact applications connect integral topology to genuine smoothing geometry, including the original-side four-node family from the Calabi–Yau smoothing programme. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 9 November 2026; this version 9 November 2026, 41 pages · source and exact calculations · Zenodo:24096887
  • Small eigenvalues on degenerating curves and the intersection form For reduced degenerations of compact curves with smooth total space and metrics Hermitian near the central singularities, the complete small scalar spectrum is determined by the negative intersection matrix relative to component areas. The leading coefficients are 2π times its positive generalized eigenvalues, with inverse-logarithmic decay and a gap in the remaining spectrum. A direct proof allows arbitrary singularities of the reduced central curve and identifies the product coefficient through weighted spanning trees. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 10 November 2026; this version 10 November 2026, 11 pages · source and guide · Zenodo:24120959
  • Small Hodge eigenvalues on nodal degenerations of threefolds For nodal degenerations of compact threefolds with smooth Kähler total space and induced fibre metrics, exceptional periods and a quotient harmonic norm determine all leading small positive two-form eigenvalues. The small resolution must have vanishing first Betti number and second real cohomology generated by line-bundle classes. The small forms are coexact, primitive and of type (1,1), with a gap in the remaining positive spectra in degrees one and two. Preprint · current version (PDF), original 10 November 2026; this version 10 November 2026, 17 pages · source and guide · Zenodo:24120961
  • Effective Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow on nodal degenerations of curves On trivial determinant-one bundles over compact nodal curve degenerations, actual noncommuting Hermitian–Yang–Mills metric flow follows a finite matrix graph flow on logarithmic time scales. Component areas supply the masses and each node supplies a coupling.
