At the wall.

A small change.
An enormous response.

A geometric structure can be stable and still be close to losing its grip. Near the boundary, some changes cost almost no energy. Its metric becomes exquisitely sensitive.

Here a four-dimensional complex manifold leaves a surprisingly small footprint: four vertices, four edges, and three numbers tending to zero.

What to notice

A vector bundle has a slope: degree per rank. Stability asks that every proper subsheaf have smaller slope than the whole. A wall is where one of those strict inequalities becomes an equality. Changing the polarization changes the slopes.

For this tangent bundle, four line factors and their extensions control what happens near a meeting of walls. The square above is a slice of its local chamber. The graph’s small eigenvalues measure the energy of relative changes between factors; their reciprocals describe the leading sensitivity of the Hermitian–Einstein metric.

The geometry and the calculation

The example is the tangent bundle of X = ℙB(O ⊕ L) ×B ℙB(O ⊕ L), with B = ℙ¹ × ℙ¹ and L = O(1, −1). This is a smooth projective weak-Fano fourfold, not a Fano fourfold. Its tangent sequence has factors R₁, R₂, Q₁, Q₂, all of rank one.

Set x = σ(p, 1 − p) and y = σ(q, 1 − q). The stable inequalities are x₁, x₂, y₁, y₂ > 0, with x₁ + x₂ = y₁ + y₂ = σ. The leading edge weights are kij = xiyj/σ. The explorer diagonalizes L = D diag(k) Dᵀ on the subspace whose coordinates sum to zero. All quantities displayed are computed from this finite operator.

For the actual Hermitian–Einstein metric in the strict chamber, λj = νj(L)(1 + O(σ)), j = 1, 2, 3, while the remaining spectrum has a uniform positive gap. In the paper’s slope coordinates and normalized L² norms, the three metric-response singular values are νj−1(1 + O(σ)). These are singular values, not an identification of every response direction with a plotted graph eigenvector.

The theorem is uniform through separated positive scales. It supplies no numerical value for the error constant displayed here, so σ is not a certified percentage error. On a nonsplit semistable boundary bundle this stationary Hermitian–Einstein metric is not asserted; boundary graphs are limiting finite models only. This is an endomorphism Dolbeault spectrum, not the full Yang–Mills Hessian.

The softest direction becomes softer much faster than the others. In the geometric theorem, the next eigenvalue stays bounded away from zero. The disappearing modes are a specific, finite part of the spectrum.

The path is realized by polarizations

Set x₁ = t⁶, x₂ = t², y₁ = t⁴, y₂ = t² − t⁴ + t⁶. These positive demands are realized by the paper’s ample chart on the fixed tangent bundle. Its actual eigenvalues satisfy λ₁ = (4/3)t⁶(1 + O(t²)), λ₂ = (3/2)t⁴(1 + O(t²)), λ₃ = 2t²(1 + O(t²)), and λ₄ ≥ γ > 0. The plotted solid curves are graph eigenvalues; the dashed curves are their asymptotic leading terms. The wider end of the plot illustrates the finite model without asserting a numerical geometric error bound.

Same slopes, different spectrum

Fixed demands and the missing datum

Both graphs have unit vertex masses and demands b = ρ(2, 1, −1, −2), with ρ = 0.02. The tree weights are ρ(1, 1, 0, 1). For the cycle, k = ρ[[a, 2 − a], [1 − a, a]], where 0 < a < 1 solves a²/((2 − a)(1 − a)) = θ. Thus every row and column sum stays fixed.

The ratio θ = c₁₁c₂₂/(c₁₂c₂₁) comes from harmonic extension data, invariant under factor rescaling. This is an instance of Paper III’s general cyclic mechanism. It is not a free control on the fixed tangent bundle in experiments 1–2: that geometric example has a reference symmetry fixing its leading cycle ratio.

04 · The two-factor flow

There is another side.

Move the signed slope gap through zero. The long-time profile changes from a positive limit, to inverse-time decay, to exponential decay.

Three signed profiles, with your selected gap highlighted. Vertical axis logarithmic; time starts at τ = 1.

Stable side—Small-eigenvalue profile Pδ(τ)

The profile and what the flow theorem says

Pδ(τ) = δ / (1 − exp(−2δτ)),   P₀(τ) = 1/(2τ).

These are exact evaluations of the profile in Paper V, not numerical solutions of the Hermitian Yang–Mills PDE. For the paper’s fixed two-factor extension and any smooth positive initial-metric family, each tolerance admits one sufficiently late time and then one parameter neighborhood such that the actual smallest eigenvalue divided by this profile stays within that tolerance for every subsequent time. Both signs of δ and δ = 0 are included.

For δ > 0, P tends to δ. At the wall it equals 1/(2τ). For δ < 0, P ∼ |δ| exp(−2|δ|τ). The experiment does not claim that a stationary Hermitian–Einstein metric continues across the wall, or that this scalar formula describes a general many-factor flow.
