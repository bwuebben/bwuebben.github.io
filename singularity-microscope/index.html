The mathematics of the microscope

A Hermitian metric assigns lengths and angles to the fibres of a complex vector bundle. Hermitian–Yang–Mills flow adjusts that metric toward a preferred curvature condition. In the setting of Paper 32, curvature concentrates at a finite set of point defects.

For distinct equal-degree line factors and reduced point defects on a compact Kähler surface, the inclusion mass Z(T) is asymptotic to a positive constant divided by T. The concentration radius ρ(T) is proportional to √Z(T), hence to T−1/2. The constants depend on the fixed geometric data.

ρ(T) / ρ(T₀) ≈ √(T₀/T)
Z(T) / Z(T₀) ≈ T₀/T

The controls evaluate these leading laws as an exact illustrative model. T₀ represents a time already in the asymptotic regime; the theorem does not give a universal numerical starting time. At the shrinking scale, actual flow metrics converge to an explicit charge-one anti-self-dual metric. Its density relative to the peak is (1 + R²)−4, where R = r/ρ(T).

The unit charge belongs to the limiting bubble. Total Yang–Mills energy along the original flow need not be constant. Nonreduced defects and repeated line factors do not carry the same sharp-profile conclusion.

Two exact functions, one shared radius

The normalized inclusion measures the squared length of the fixed holomorphic line inclusion, divided by its limiting outer height. The normalized energy counts the fraction of the charge-one bubble’s total curvature energy 8π² inside a four-dimensional ball.

Inclusion: H(R) = R² / (1 + R²)
Energy: C(R) = R⁴(R² + 3) / (1 + R²)³

Both equal 1/2 at R = 1. In the actual flow, the inclusion half-height radius and the half-defect-energy radius have the same leading asymptotic value. The plotted equality is exact for the limiting profile.

A schematic family with two distinct unit defects. This control is an approach parameter, not a flow clock or a computed metric distance. The ledger describes the limiting decomposition.

A limit can be singular and still lie at finite distance. Completing the space means including the objects a finite journey can approach.

Where the completion theorem applies

This experiment introduces the separate setting of Paper 33. On a projective K3 surface with its Ricci-flat metric, a generic polarization and a primitive Mukai vector of rank at least two and positive square, with nonempty stable bundle locus, the L² metric has Uhlenbeck completion. Ideal connections supply the limiting objects.

For rank two, stable residual bundles with distinct simple bubble points form totally geodesic hyperkähler orbifold strata. The paper also identifies affine tangent cones at stable-residual points; a full identification with flat ADHM quotient metrics remains conditional outside the established locus. The local flow example above and this moduli-space completion theorem have different hypotheses.
